Raast: Pakistan's Quiet Digital Payments Revolution

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 17, 2026 · 2 min read

In a small shop in Hyderabad, a vegetable seller who never owned a bank account now receives payments on his mobile phone in seconds. He does not know the term for it, but he is part of one of Pakistan's biggest financial shifts in decades: Raast, the State Bank of Pakistan's instant payment system.

Launched as a national project, Raast lets anyone send and receive money instantly, free of charge, using just a mobile number or an alias. No IBAN to remember, no waiting hours for an interbank transfer, no fee deducted at either end. For a country where cash has ruled for generations, that simplicity is revolutionary.

The numbers tell the story. Person-to-person transfers on Raast have grown into the hundreds of billions of rupees, and merchant payments are climbing as shopkeepers discover they can accept digital money without buying expensive point-of-sale machines. A QR code printed on paper is enough.

"A QR code printed on paper is enough — that simplicity is revolutionary in a country where cash has ruled for generations."

Financial inclusion is the real prize. Pakistan has one of the lowest banking penetration rates in the region, yet mobile phone ownership is widespread. Raast bridges that gap — a farmer in interior Sindh with a basic smartphone and a mobile wallet can now receive payment for his crop the same day it is sold.

Challenges remain. Digital literacy is low, fraudsters target new users with fake messages, and many small traders still prefer the feel of cash. The State Bank and commercial banks have run awareness campaigns, but trust is built one successful transaction at a time.

Still, the direction is clear. Every month, more salaries, more merchant payments and more government disbursements move through Raast. The vegetable seller in Hyderabad may never read an economics column, but his phone buzzing with an instant payment is the sound of Pakistan's financial future arriving.
