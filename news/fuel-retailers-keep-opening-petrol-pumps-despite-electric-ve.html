Fuel retailers keep opening petrol pumps despite electric vehicle push

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Oil marketing companies in Pakistan appear more focused on opening new fuel retail outlets, a trend that seems at odds with the government's newly announced Auto Policy 2026-31, which aims to make 30 per cent of all new vehicles electric by 2030. The companies are upbeat about rising petrol demand, driven by strong sales of petrol-powered two-wheelers and 660-1,000cc cars, and by higher petrol prices pushing more buyers towards motorcycles as public transport remains ineffective. Pakistan State Oil completed 37 new retail sites in the first quarter of FY27, taking its network to 3,727 outlets, the largest in the country, and commissioned electric vehicle chargers in Faisalabad and Multan, expanding its charging network to 16 locations.

Wafi Energy Pakistan added 54 new Shell retail sites and nine electric vehicle charging locations in the nine months ending September 30. Attock Petroleum commissioned five new outlets in the same quarter while phasing out six underperforming sites, leaving its network at 816 outlets, and operationalised EV chargers at the Dandiwal service areas on the M-4 Motorway with Hubco Green. An oil industry executive said the government has issued licences to 50 oil marketing companies, of which 48 have established a presence, and noted that petrol demand will stay robust because the country has five million cars, 30 million bikes and 10 million rickshaws on its roads, with no rule requiring old vehicles to be scrapped.

Electric two-wheeler sales rose to 240,000 in FY26 from 60,000 in FY25, and overall bike assembly in July-August FY27 rose to 442,179 units from 321,788 in the same period last year.
