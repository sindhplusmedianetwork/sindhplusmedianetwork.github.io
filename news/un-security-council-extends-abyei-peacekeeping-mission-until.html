UN Security Council extends Abyei peacekeeping mission until November 2027

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The UN Security Council voted to extend the mandate of the UN Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA) and its support for the border monitoring mechanism until 15 November 2027. Abyei is an oil-rich, disputed administrative area claimed by both Sudan and South Sudan. Acting under Chapter VII of the UN Charter, the 15-member Council adopted resolution 2838 (2026) by a vote of 13 in favour to none against, with two abstentions, China and Russia.

Pakistan voted in favour of the resolution to reaffirm its support for UNISFA and its indispensable role in protecting civilians, preventing escalation and maintaining stability in Abyei, Ambassador Usman Jadoon, deputy permanent representative of Pakistan to the UN, told the Council while explaining his vote. He added that Pakistan wished to place on record its continued reservations regarding the linkage between the mission's future renewal and progress in the political process, which depends largely on decisions by Sudan and South Sudan, beyond UNISFA's control. Ambassador Jadoon also emphasised that the mission must be provided with adequate financial, logistical and operational resources to effectively implement its mandate, including for the deployment of all three authorised Formed Police Units.

Under the terms of the resolution, it also called for a prompt, impartial and thorough investigation into the drone attack against UNISFA personnel in Kadugli on 13 December 2025.
