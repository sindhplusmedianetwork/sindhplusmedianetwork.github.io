Republican Attacks on El-Sayed Over Muslim Brotherhood Links Aim to Sway Michigan Voters

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Republican US Senate candidate Mike Rogers and his allies, including President Donald Trump, are escalating efforts to link Democratic rival Abdul El-Sayed to the Muslim Brotherhood less than two weeks before the November 3 midterm elections, with Trump falsely branding him a jihadist. Pollsters and political strategists say the approach aims to dissuade Jewish and Black voters in the Detroit area who backed El-Sayed's primary opponent from supporting the Democratic nominee. El-Sayed and Rogers are vying for the seat held by retiring Democratic Senator Gary Peters in a race that could determine whether Democrats gain the four seats needed to take control of the Senate.

Republicans currently hold a 53-47 majority, and El-Sayed has built a narrow lead in opinion polls, averaging 2.6 percentage points according to RealClearPolling.com. Rogers has highlighted El-Sayed's criticism of Israel's offensive in Gaza and alleged longstanding family ties to the Muslim Brotherhood, citing old social media posts by El-Sayed's half-sister. El-Sayed has disavowed the group, denied any connection and said he was not raised by his mother.

During this week's televised debate in Southfield, Michigan, Rogers questioned El-Sayed's loyalty to the United States; El-Sayed, the son of Egyptian immigrants, called the question shameful and said he was proud to be an American. Independent analysts rate the race a toss-up.
