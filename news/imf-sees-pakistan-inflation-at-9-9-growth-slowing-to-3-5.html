IMF Sees Pakistan Inflation at 9.9%, Growth Slowing to 3.5%

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The International Monetary Fund has projected Pakistan's inflation at 9.9 percent for fiscal year 2026-27, up from 7.1 percent, in its latest World Economic Outlook released on October 13. The Fund expects the country's GDP growth to moderate to 3.5 percent from 3.6 percent over the same period. Pakistan's current account is forecast to slip into a deficit equal to one percent of GDP, compared with a 0.5 percent surplus previously.

Unemployment is projected to ease to 6.5 percent from 6.9 percent. Globally, the IMF sees growth slowing to 3.1 percent in 2026 before rebounding to 3.4 percent in 2027, with global headline inflation rising to 4.7 percent in 2026. The outlook notes that strong investment in artificial intelligence has partly offset the drag from the Middle East war, though the conflict continues to keep oil prices elevated.

It warns that further escalation of conflicts, renewed trade tensions or fiscal vulnerabilities could weaken growth, while stronger AI-driven productivity could lift it.
