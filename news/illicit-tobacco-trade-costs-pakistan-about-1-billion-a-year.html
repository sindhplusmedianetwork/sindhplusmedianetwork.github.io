Illicit Tobacco Trade Costs Pakistan About $1 Billion a Year, Study Finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Illicit tobacco trade continues to impose a significant fiscal burden across Asia-Pacific, depriving governments of billions of dollars in potential revenue, according to a report titled 'Illicit Tobacco Trade in Asia-Pacific: Taxation, Market Incentives, and Macroeconomic Costs'. The study estimates the illicit trade results in $14.85 billion in annual revenue losses across the economies covered, with Pakistan alone accounting for approximately $1 billion in estimated annual fiscal leakage. In Pakistan, the study estimates an illicit market share of 54 percent, with foregone revenue equivalent to 117.4 percent of tobacco tax collected.

The findings point to a large illicit market in Pakistan, where more than half of the tobacco market is estimated to be illicit. The report covers the illicit tobacco trade across Asia-Pacific economies.
