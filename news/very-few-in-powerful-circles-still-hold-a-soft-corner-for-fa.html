Very few in powerful circles still hold a soft corner for Fazlur Rehman, says source

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Amid the debate over the proposed 28th constitutional amendment and the creation of new provinces, rumours of a possible political reconciliation with former prime minister Imran Khan continue to circulate in political circles. However, a source familiar with the thinking of powerful circles says there has been no change in the current policy on the founder of the PTI, although the recent release of Imran Khan's two sisters and the debate over the proposed constitutional amendment have given the political discussion a new direction, given the need for parliamentary support for constitutional changes to provincial boundaries. According to Latif Khosa, the issue of the 28th amendment and the creation of new provinces had come up in a recent meeting between Imran Khan and his legal experts, and the PTI's possible stance on the proposed amendment has become a subject of political interest, carrying considerable weight given the party's parliamentary strength in any future constitutional move, which has also strengthened rumours that some political understanding involving Imran Khan may be reached at some point.

The source's analysis, however, gives no impression that any policy has changed in support of that idea, and the source said debates on constitutional matters, political speculation and possible backchannel efforts should not be seen as evidence that policy towards the PTI founder is changing. When the source was asked whether JUI-F chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman was once well regarded in powerful circles, the source said very few people still hold a soft corner for him, and whatever sympathy and political acceptance he once enjoyed has now markedly declined. The assessment comes at a time when Fazlur Rehman is speaking openly on political and constitutional matters, openly challenging the current political system and raising questions about the role of powerful institutions.

His son Asad Mahmood is also in focus, as the National Accountability Bureau has summoned him over alleged irregularities in recruitments in Pakistan Post.
