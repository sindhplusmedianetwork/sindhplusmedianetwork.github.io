Constitutional Court Rules Provinces Cannot Take Over Unexercised Federal Powers

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 21, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) has ruled that parliament's failure to exercise a fiscal power does not transfer that subject to the provinces. The 59-page judgement, authored by Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan, held that where a provincial law conflicts with a competent federal law, the federal law prevails under Article 142 of the Constitution and the provincial law is void to the extent of the conflict. A two-judge bench comprising the chief justice and Justice Aamer Farooq issued the detailed judgement on 568 challenges filed by taxpayers against the infrastructure development cess imposed by Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Allowing the taxpayers' appeals, the court set aside the judgements of the Sindh and Peshawar high courts and declared the cess charging provisions, together with dependent rules, notifications, assessments and recovery measures, unconstitutional and without lawful authority. The court said no demand, surcharge or penalty based solely on those charges could be enforced, and securities furnished for the invalid charges would be discharged after verification. It added that provinces remain free to levy constitutionally competent charges on goods manufactured and consumed within their own territories.

The judgement noted that the 18th Amendment expanded provincial competence but did not transfer subjects expressly retained in the federal domain, including imports, exports and interprovincial trade.
