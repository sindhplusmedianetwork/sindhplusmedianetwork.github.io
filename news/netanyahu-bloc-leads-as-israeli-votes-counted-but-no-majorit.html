Netanyahu Bloc Leads as Israeli Votes Counted, but No Majority

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's camp was ahead of his rival's opposition bloc but still short of a majority on Wednesday after two-thirds of the votes were counted in Israel's pivotal general election. With 66 per cent of ballots counted, Netanyahu's bloc was shown to have 54 of the 120 seats in parliament, while Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc had 48 seats and the country's non-aligned Arab parties 18. Both Netanyahu and his main rival Eisenkot vowed to form a government despite neither being projected to win a majority, and the partial results are likely to change as more votes are counted.

Voters turned out in the highest numbers in decades, with turnout reaching nearly 78 per cent, the highest since 1999, to give their verdict on Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving prime minister. Exit polls had shown Eisenkot, a former military chief and political novice, neck-and-neck with Netanyahu's hard-right camp at about 51 seats each, well away from the threshold for a majority. The strongest gains went to Arab Israeli parties, whose voters turned out in soaring numbers, and the partial results suggest Eisenkot's bloc could reach a majority if it entered a coalition with the Arab parties.

Yair Golan, head of Israel's main left-wing party in Eisenkot's camp, called for precisely such a partnership, saying there is a responsibility and an opportunity to form a government of change with Israel's Arab citizens. Eisenkot, speaking in Tel Aviv, called Netanyahu a caretaker prime minister without a mandate or the people's trust and said the public had voted for change, while Netanyahu told supporters in Jerusalem that the Jewish and Zionist majority must unite for a strong government of the right. Full results are expected by Thursday evening or Friday morning, and a lack of a clear majority could mean weeks of coalition negotiations, with possible new elections if talks fail.
