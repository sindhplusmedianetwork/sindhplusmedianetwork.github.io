Supreme Court Gives Key Verdict on Direct Enforcement of Foreign Court Decrees in Pakistan

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 21, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Supreme Court has delivered an important verdict regarding the direct enforcement of foreign court decrees in Pakistan. The court allowed a private company's appeal by a majority of two to one and annulled the orders of the Lahore High Court and the subordinate court. Justice Munib Akhtar and Justice Muhammad Shafi Siddiqui agreed to allow the company's appeal, while Justice Irfan Saadat Khan dissented from the majority opinion.

In the case, the legal status of the UAE's federal courts and Abu Dhabi's local courts was examined. In its verdict, the Supreme Court said the disputed decree verified by the Abu Dhabi court cannot be directly enforced in Pakistan under Section 44A, and the majority held that Abu Dhabi's local appellate court cannot be considered the UAE's federal appellate court, as a specific federal law is required for the transfer of local judicial authority to federal courts. The majority verdict said the Lahore executing court did not have the authority to enforce the decree under Section 44A.

After the difference of opinion in the two-member bench, the matter was sent to a third judge as referee, and Justice Muhammad Shafi Siddiqui, as referee judge, agreed with Justice Munib Akhtar's opinion. Justice Irfan Saadat Khan gave a dissenting opinion that the trial court's decree had merged into the appellate court's decree, the decree under enforcement should be considered the appellate court's decree, the UAE is a recognised reciprocal-enforcement territory, and the appellate court's decree can be enforced in Pakistan.
