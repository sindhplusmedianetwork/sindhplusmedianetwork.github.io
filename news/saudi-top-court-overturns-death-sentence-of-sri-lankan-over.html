Saudi Top Court Overturns Death Sentence of Sri Lankan Over Facebook Post

By Sindh Plus Media Network

Saudi Arabia's Supreme Court has set aside the death sentence handed to Sri Lankan migrant worker Anojan Sivarasa over a Facebook comment, Colombo announced on Wednesday. The 27-year-old was sentenced to death in September by an appellate court after being convicted of blasphemy against Islam and the Prophet Muhammad. The case began in late June when footage of a Muslim man kicking a dog following Hindu pilgrims in Sri Lanka circulated online, sparking a heated religious exchange in which Sivarasa responded in Tamil.

He later deleted the comment and posted an apology video, but a screenshot was sent to his employer and Saudi officials, leading to his arrest in July. The Al-Ahsa Criminal Court had initially sentenced him to five years in prison and a fine of three million Saudi riyals, but the sentence was raised to death on appeal. Sri Lanka's Deputy Foreign Minister Arun Hemachandra credited sustained diplomatic and legal efforts by both countries for the outcome.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake welcomed the decision, thanking Saudi authorities and all officials involved, while human rights groups had also called for Sivarasa's release.
