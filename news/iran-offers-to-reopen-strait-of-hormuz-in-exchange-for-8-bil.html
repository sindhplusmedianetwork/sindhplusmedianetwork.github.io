Iran offers to reopen Strait of Hormuz in exchange for $8 billion frozen in Qatar, report says

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 10, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Iran has proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for the return of its $8 billion in assets frozen in Qatar, according to a report in an Israeli newspaper carried by Jang. Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani carried the proposal to Washington after visiting Tehran and has met US President Donald Trump. Under the proposed interim arrangement, the United States would end its naval and economic blockade of Iran, while Tehran would reopen the strait and stop attacks on the commercial ships and oil tankers of Gulf countries.

In return, Iran's $8 billion in frozen assets held in Qatar would be restored. The report said Iran also appears to have dropped its demand to take full control of the strait and charge transit fees from passing ships, though Tehran is not prepared to include its nuclear programme in the negotiations. Efforts to advance the talks are being led by US Vice President JD Vance, special envoy Steve Witkoff and the president's son-in-law Jared Kushner, while senior officials including Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth oppose the proposal.

Neither government has confirmed the proposed deal, and American and Gulf officials have expressed doubt about the chances of any immediate diplomatic breakthrough. Following the report, Brent crude oil fell 3.6 percent to $94.76 a barrel, while US crude fell 3.1 percent to $88.05 a barrel.
