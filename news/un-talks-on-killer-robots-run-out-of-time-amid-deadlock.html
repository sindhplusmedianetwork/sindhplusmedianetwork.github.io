UN Talks on 'Killer Robots' Run Out of Time Amid Deadlock

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 21, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A United Nations conference on lethal autonomous weapons, often called killer robots, was running out of time on Friday after five days of talks failed to break a deadlock over whether to begin negotiating regulations. The meeting in Geneva was the review conference of the Convention on Conventional Weapons (CCW), whose 128 member countries spent the week deciding whether to launch talks on regulating such weapons, and on what terms. Any decision at the conference requires full consensus.

A majority of states favour a legally binding agreement, but countries possessing the technology, including the United States and Russia, are reluctant to accept limits. The International Committee of the Red Cross and several non-governmental organisations have pushed for a one-year negotiating timeline, while Washington has proposed stretching talks over five years, and France has sought support for a hybrid approach mixing binding and non-binding measures. Lethal autonomous weapons systems can select and attack targets without human intervention once activated, and campaigners say such systems have already been tested or deployed, including in the war in Ukraine.

Russia also triggered a procedural standoff at the start of the conference by objecting to observers, including the European Union and civil society groups, taking part in the debate.
