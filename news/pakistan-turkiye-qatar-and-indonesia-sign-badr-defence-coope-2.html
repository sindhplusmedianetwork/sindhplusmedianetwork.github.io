Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia Sign 'Badr' Defence Cooperation Accord in Doha

By Sindh Plus Media Network

Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia signed a quadrilateral defence cooperation agreement in Doha on Wednesday, October 21, to boost defence cooperation and strengthen their defensive capabilities. The 'Badr' multilateral memorandum of understanding was signed by Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Asif, Türkiye's Defence Minister Yasar Güler, Qatar's Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of State for Defence Sheikh Saud bin Abdulrahman, and Indonesia's Deputy Minister of Defence Yusuf Jauhari. The accord aims to foster cooperation in military production, boost military capabilities, and support collaboration in technical military training, research and development and joint exercises, while strengthening the countries' defence industries.

It also seeks to pool their industrial capabilities, encourage joint production and technology transfer, and develop defence capacity to meet the needs of allied and friendly nations. Pakistan's Defence Minister said the agreement reflected a shared commitment to regional peace and stability, adding that the countries would explore opportunities for industrial collaboration, technology transfer and greater self-reliance in defence production. Unlike a mutual-defence treaty, the Badr MoU does not establish a collective military response obligation.

The development comes months after Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye signed the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement in August, under which an attack against one of the countries is treated as an attack against all.
