Haiti Lawlessness: More Than 1,000 Killed in Three Months, UN Says

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Serious human rights violations continue in Haiti, where more than 1,000 people were killed between July and September in violence by armed criminal gangs and in operations against them. The head of the UN office in the country, Carlos Ruiz Massieu, said the killings are a reminder of the urgent need to strengthen the system for protecting civilians and to establish state authority in areas freed from armed gangs. According to the UN office's report, at least 1,167 people were killed and 459 injured in the three-month period up to September.

More than 45 percent of the incidents were attributed to armed gangs, while 5 percent involved groups targeting gang members and their alleged facilitators. Over half of the killings and injuries occurred during ground and air operations by security forces against armed gangs, which were sometimes supported by a private company using drones. The report also records mass killings in fighting for control of different areas, including at least 19 people beheaded in Cité Soleil in early August, among them 12 elderly women, and at least 105 people killed in two attacks on the town of Kenscoff in July and August, including 19 children, with more than 36 people shot dead in a church courtyard.

It further documents at least 137 kidnappings for ransom, including children who made up 10 percent of those abducted, and 344 victims of sexual violence by gang members, aged between eight and 75, with 77 percent of the incidents involving gang rape. The report also records 44 cases of extrajudicial killing and attempted killing by members of Haiti's national police, up from 14 in the previous quarter, and says at least 32 boys aged 14 to 16 who had been forcibly recruited by an armed gang were killed in clashes with security forces in Gressier in August, while the UN Security Council has approved the deployment of a special force against armed gangs in Haiti, but it has not yet been fully deployed, and the UN has urged the international community to maintain its commitment to help Haiti and ensure the force is fully deployed.
