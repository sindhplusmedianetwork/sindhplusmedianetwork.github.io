Indian Navy Frees Iranian Fishing Boat and 20 Crew from Pirates off Somalia

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 04, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Indian Navy has rescued the Iranian fishing boat Azini and its 20-member crew from armed pirates off the northeastern coast of Somalia, ending a standoff that lasted more than 48 hours. According to the Indian defence ministry, a naval warship, a maritime surveillance aircraft and special marine commandos took part in the operation. The Iranian vessel had been hijacked near the Somali coast on October 29.

Naval officials said the warship and aircraft kept the boat under continuous watch and tracked its movements to stop it from escaping. The marine commandos then reached the area by air aboard an Indian Air Force C-17 military transport plane, seized control of the boat and freed the crew. The defence ministry said none of the 20 rescued people was reported killed or injured.

A Puntland maritime police official told the Associated Press that the boat had carried 21 Iranian crew members and two Somali security guards, adding that Indian forces disarmed 12 suspected pirates and seized their mobile phones; three people were reported slightly injured, though it was not clear whether they were pirates or guards. Indian Defence Minister Rajnath Singh praised the operation, calling the navy-air force coordination a key factor in its success. The Iranian embassy in New Delhi thanked the Indian government, navy and air force, describing the mission as a symbol of cooperation and humanitarian spirit.

The rescue comes amid growing concern over a rise in piracy incidents in the Arabian Sea, the Gulf of Aden and the western Indian Ocean.
