US army soldier files whistleblower complaint over Iranian strike that killed 6 service members

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read

A US Army major who survived an Iranian strike on a makeshift operations centre in Kuwait that killed six service members has filed a whistleblower complaint alleging that the Pentagon ignored concerns about inadequate protection for troops. Army Major Stephen Ramsbottom called the March 1 attack a 'tactical failure' by the army, its leadership and himself during an interview with the CBS programme '60 Minutes' that aired on Sunday. The six US Army Reserve soldiers killed in the attack on Kuwait's Shuaiba port were the first American service members to die in the war with Iran, and dozens of others, including Ramsbottom, were injured.

Although senior Pentagon officials described the facility as fortified at the time, a source familiar with the site said its defences consisted mainly of concrete barriers designed to protect against car bomb attacks, but not necessarily drones or missiles. The strike occurred without warning or sirens to alert personnel to evacuate or seek shelter in a bunker, and the complaint also states that the facility had neither a physician nor a permanent medical treatment station when the drone strike occurred, with medical support limited to a single ambulance. Ramsbottom said he had repeatedly raised concerns with his superiors during several meetings about the site's vulnerability to aerial attacks, but was told there was no need to worry about additional protection, and a counter-drone system discussed by military officials never arrived.

The complaint was submitted last week to the House and Senate Armed Services Committees, and comes as Democrats prepare to launch wide-ranging investigations into the Trump administration's handling of the war with Iran when they take control of Congress in January.
