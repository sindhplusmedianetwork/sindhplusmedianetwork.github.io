UN report: Security Council veto power hinders efforts to end conflicts

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 25, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A new United Nations report has warned that the veto power of the five permanent members of the Security Council is one of the reasons multilateral mechanisms have been unable to prevent or stop the growing number of state-based conflicts. The resulting paralysis has weakened decision-making processes, prompting the UN secretary-general to call for Security Council reforms, including an expansion of its membership to ensure fairer representation, particularly for Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, and the Asia-Pacific region. The World Social Report 2026, prepared by the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs, says geopolitical fragmentation has considerably heightened global risks, with multiple centres of power competing for economic, military and political dominance.

According to the report, the number of countries exercising substantial influence in another country has risen from six in the 1970s to 34 today. The report notes that relations between neighbouring countries have substantially deteriorated since 2008, with 59 countries recording poor ties with neighbours in 2024-25, while only 19 improved. Rising conflicts and geopolitical tensions are weakening international cooperation, disrupting trade and supply chains, and diverting financial and political resources away from development priorities.

The report adds that more than half of countries increased defence spending as a share of GDP between 2020 and 2024, and all nine nuclear-armed states have expanded their arsenals since 2022.
