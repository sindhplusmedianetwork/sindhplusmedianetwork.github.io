Pakistan announces 13 mineral cooperation deals at Islamabad forum

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan on Friday announced over a dozen mineral cooperation agreements with foreign companies and countries, declaring that it would move away from exporting raw materials and unprocessed minerals as it seeks to reshape its economic and development priorities. At the concluding day of the Pakistan Mineral Investment Forum in Islamabad, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir both emphasised that Pakistan wanted investors to build complete value chains, starting from exploration to finished products, instead of just exporting raw materials. The prime minister said mineral resources must be developed alongside environmental protection, stressing that mining projects must follow international standards and use modern technology to safeguard water resources and workers' health and safety.

Pakistani companies finalised 13 joint ventures, memoranda of understanding and agreements with foreign companies on the last day of the conference, including copper and gold exploration in Chagai, Balochistan, a state-of-the-art mineral assay laboratory, and geological cooperation with Turkiye, the United Kingdom, Japan and Germany. Barrick Gold Executive Chairman John Thornton, whose company owns a 50 per cent stake in the Reko Diq project, said his company intends to continue to be a long-term partner of Pakistan. Pakistan's mineral reserves are estimated at around 8 trillion dollars and spread over 600,000 square kilometres, according to the Institute of Cost and Management Accountants, while the sector has historically contributed less than 3 per cent to gross domestic product.

With strong governance and security, annual mineral exports could rise from about 3 billion dollars to 8 billion dollars, and economic growth could climb from roughly 3 per cent to 6 per cent.
