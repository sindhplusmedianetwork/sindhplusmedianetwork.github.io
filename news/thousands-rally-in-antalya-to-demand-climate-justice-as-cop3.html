Thousands rally in Antalya to demand climate justice as COP31 talks hit midpoint

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 15, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Thousands of protesters took to the streets in Antalya, Turkiye, on Saturday to accuse the COP31 climate summit of failing those bearing the brunt of environmental destruction. Police estimated that more than 7,000 people attended the rally, the largest demonstration since the summit began on Monday. In downtown Antalya, under rainy skies, protesters dressed in costumes and shouting slogans marched under close police supervision.

Halime Saman, from the People's Climate Summit platform, said the voices of those suffering climate injustice were never heard at the COP, adding that Turkiye, the summit's co-chair, still had no roadmap for phasing out fossil fuels. Yasar Gokoglu, a 78-year-old demonstrator, travelled nearly 600 kilometres from Adana to join the protest. The negotiations hit the halfway point on Saturday, but countries remain far apart on financing adaptation measures for developing nations, defending climate science, and shifting from fossil fuels.

At the Pakistan Pavilion, Parliamentary Secretary Danyal Chaudhry presented five parliamentary recommendations for climate-vulnerable and debt-stressed nations. UN climate chief Simon Stiell told reporters there was no climate quick fix, but each COP must drive progress.
