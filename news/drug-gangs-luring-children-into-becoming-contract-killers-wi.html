Drug Gangs Luring Children into Becoming Contract Killers with Cars and Flats

Drug-dealing gangs are luring children into becoming contract killers by promising them expensive cars and flats, a BBC investigation reports. The BBC team spent months trying to enter the dangerous world of Marseille's drug gangs, centred on deprived streets and high-rise housing estates in the French port city. The city's most powerful gang, DZ Mafia, emerged victorious from a bloody turf war and is now one of France's most feared criminal organisations.

The gang recruits young people from across France, not only to sell drugs but also to carry out kidnapping, torture and murder. More than 10,000 children were involved in drug trafficking in some way during 2025, according to official figures. One young man, Adam, admitted the murder cases against him were 'pretty horrific', describing daylight attacks, shootings in traffic jams, assaults and abductions.

Another former member, Camil, said the gang offered recruits whatever they wanted, from nice cars and spacious apartments to drugs. The gangs also use social media to recruit hitmen, advertising roles ranging from courier to killer.
