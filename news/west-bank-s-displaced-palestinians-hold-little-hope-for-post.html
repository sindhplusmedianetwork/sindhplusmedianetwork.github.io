West Bank's displaced Palestinians hold little hope for post-Netanyahu era

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Displaced Palestinians from the West Bank refugee camp of Tulkarem hold little hope of returning home, even as the prospect of an end to Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's rule grows. After Israeli forces pushed him and thousands of others out of the camp last year, 64-year-old Adel Darsieh held onto his hope of returning home, but it is fading fast. Shortly before the election, Netanyahu announced that no one removed from Tulkarem would be allowed back, and he is now fighting for his political life after his party lost seats in Israel's general election last month.

Former general Gadi Eisenkot has been tasked with trying to form a new government, but Darsieh says a change of government would change little. According to United Nations figures, the Israeli operation launched last year displaced 12,100 people from the Tulkarem camp, which was set up in 1950 for refugees who fled or were forced out during the creation of the state of Israel. Darsieh now lives near the camp in a modest, makeshift home with a sheet metal roof, and receives food from a charity kitchen that prepares some 1,200 meals for 400 displaced families.

Even if residents could return, much of the camp has been destroyed, with houses razed, buildings emptied and businesses burnt down.
