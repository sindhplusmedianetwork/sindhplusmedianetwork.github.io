Short Stint on Low-Fat Diet Shows Lasting Anti-Inflammatory Effect, Study Finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Switching briefly to a low-fat diet at the onset of an inflammatory disease could significantly alter the course of that disease in both the short and long term, according to a new study by a University of California, San Francisco team. The research, published in Nature on October 28, found that even a short-term dietary change can substantially change how the body responds to skin irritation, at least in mice. When scientists fed mice a high-fat diet for just a few days, the animals reacted far more severely to an irritating cream, and their skin stayed on alert even after they returned to a low-fat diet and the inflammation had cleared, responding more strongly the next time it was exposed.

The findings suggest that short-term diet may matter just as much as body weight when it comes to inflammation. Corresponding author Sagar Bapat said long-term dietary habits absolutely matter for health, but the study uncovered a much more real-time influence of diet on the immune system, noting that breakfast, lunch and dinner can all have a rapid impact. The work grew out of laboratory experiments on obesity led by Zewen 'Owen' Jiang, who first raised mice on either low-fat or high-fat pellets and then applied imiquimod, a cream that triggers skin inflammation, to one ear of each mouse.

Obese mice on habitual high-fat feed had severe reactions, while low-fat-fed mice had mild ones, and crucially, obese mice switched to normal food for just four days experienced much milder inflammation.
