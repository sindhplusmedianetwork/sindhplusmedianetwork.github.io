Reza Pahlavi Receives Freedom Prize in Frankfurt Amid Cheers and Criticism

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Reza Pahlavi, son of Iran's former shah, received the Freedom Prize for the Iranian people in Frankfurt, Germany, on Saturday. Thousands of people welcomed him on the streets of Frankfurt, chanting slogans in his favour and waving the national flag from before the Iranian revolution. The prize was awarded by the Friedrich Naumann Foundation, considered close to the liberal FDP party, and the ceremony was held at St Paul's Church, where Pahlavi said Iran was not a country that needed to be taught democracy, but a country whose people were being stopped from practising it, adding that a nation ready to give its life for the right to vote was also ready to use that right.

However, some circles criticised the ceremony, calling it unrepresentative and illegitimate. His father, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, was Iran's last shah, an authoritarian ruler who left the country into exile before the monarchy ended in the 1979 revolution. Critics also object to his open support for Israel and his failure to clearly distance himself from his father's authoritarian era.

According to Sahar Sanaei, spokeswoman for the Society for German Iranians in Hesse, about 200 people imprisoned in Iran signed a letter protesting the award, seeing Pahlavi as a symbol of past dictatorship, while local branches of the Social Democratic Party and the Greens boycotted the ceremony and the local branch of Die Linke opposed holding it in the church, saying city rules did not allow political events in the place of worship. Pahlavi said on Saturday that he was accepting the prize only as a trustee until a democratically elected government was established in Iran, adding that he would one day hand it to its rightful owners in Tehran, not on foreign soil.
