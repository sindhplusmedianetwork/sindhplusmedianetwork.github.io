Powerful Pacific jet stream sends flights to destinations ahead of schedule

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A jet stream over the Pacific Ocean has been blowing at an extraordinary 330 to 370 kilometres per hour over the past 24 hours, driven by a strong El Nino pattern warming ocean temperatures. The El Nino pattern is making the Pacific jet stream far more powerful and wide than usual at the start of winter. At an altitude of around 30,000 to 35,000 feet, the jet stream's speed is considered extremely powerful, with winds in its strongest part reaching 180 to 200 knots, about 330 to 370 kilometres per hour.

Passenger aircraft flying west to east, from Asia or Japan towards America, are riding these fast currents, which push the planes from behind. The tailwinds are bringing flights to their destinations well ahead of schedule and saving an extraordinary amount of fuel, worth crores of rupees per hour on one route. Flights on the opposite route, from America towards Asia, are facing severe headwinds, which can take far more time and fuel than usual.
