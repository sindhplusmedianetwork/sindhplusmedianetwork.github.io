K-P Council Tasked With Persuading Armed Groups to Leave Emergency Districts

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Authorities in Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa have begun a peace process in the twelve districts where emergency was imposed on October 11, involving local tribal elders and jirgas to engage with militants and convince them to leave the areas. Under the process, the task has been assigned to a 48-member Area Representatives and Development Council, which will initiate talks according to tribal customs in its districts and convince armed groups to evacuate. Council chairman Shaukatullah Khan, who also served as Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa governor, said members will enlist support from jirgas, tribal elders and local people so that armed groups may be convinced to voluntarily evacuate.

The Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa governor's adviser on information, Mehmood Jan Babar, said the relevant council member in every district will be assigned to liaise with local jirgas, elders and people, and the process will move forward on local contacts and consultations. The districts are Dera Ismail Khan, Tank, Lakki Marwat, Bannu, Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, Kurram, Orakzai, North Waziristan, Upper South Waziristan and Lower South Waziristan. The federal government announced the emergency in these districts on October 11, a joint sitting of Parliament endorsed it on October 15, and a notification establishing the 48-member council was issued on October 17.

Khan said development would be the council's initial priority, while establishing peace in the districts was also essential and would require a separate strategy that took tribal traditions into account.
