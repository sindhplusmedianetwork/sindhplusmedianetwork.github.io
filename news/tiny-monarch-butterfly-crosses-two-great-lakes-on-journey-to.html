Tiny monarch butterfly crosses two Great Lakes on journey to Mexico

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A tiny monarch butterfly has achieved what scientists long thought impossible, flying more than 3,300 kilometres in 70 days from Manitoulin Island in Canada and crossing two Great Lakes to reach Mexico. The butterfly, known only by the code MAN26021, crossed Lake Huron and Lake Michigan on its way to the monarch wintering grounds about 100 kilometres northwest of Mexico City. According to Canada's national broadcaster CBC, it is the first time a monarch butterfly's crossing of the Great Lakes has been proven.

In late August, researchers from Environment and Climate Change Canada and the Canadian Wildlife Federation tagged dozens of butterflies on Manitoulin Island to understand their migration and flight routes. Each butterfly carried a tiny solar-powered radio transmitter on its back, weighing less than a grain of rice, while the butterfly itself weighed about as much as a paper clip. Retired Laurentian University entomologist Joe Shorthouse and his colleagues followed the butterfly's whole journey on an app called Project Monarch.

Shorthouse called it a spectacular achievement of the natural world, saying it was the first time in history they had learned that these butterflies could fly across the Great Lakes. The butterfly is now spending the winter with thousands of other monarchs on oyamel fir trees in the mountains of Mexico, and researchers hope it will fly back north to Texas in spring to lay eggs.
