Lahore once again ranks as world's most polluted major city as AQI crosses 400

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Heavy smog pushed air quality in several Punjab cities into the hazardous category on Friday, with Lahore recording an hourly AQI reading of 465 between 7am and 8am, while Gujranwala and Multan registered readings of 661 and 641 respectively, according to real-time air quality data. Lahore's historical hourly air quality graph classified the reading of 465 as hazardous, indicating extremely polluted conditions and serious health risks for residents. A separate ranking of Pakistani cities placed Gujranwala at the top with an AQI of 661, followed by Multan at 641 and Lahore at 426, while other Punjab cities also reported elevated pollution levels, including Sargodha at 289, Sialkot at 232, Rahim Yar Khan at 228 and Bahawalpur at 198.

Residents have been advised to minimise outdoor activities, keep windows closed, wear protective masks when going outside and use air purifiers indoors, as prolonged exposure to high concentrations of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) can aggravate respiratory and cardiovascular conditions. The deterioration in air quality comes despite intensified enforcement operations by the Punjab Environment Protection and Climate Change Department against sources of pollution, particularly non-compliant brick kilns, with departmental figures showing 84,370 inspections of brick kilns across the province between January 1 and November 18, demolishing 1,429 units, sealing 968 and registering 1,248 FIRs, besides fines totalling Rs178 million. Meanwhile, Pakistan's average PM2.5 concentration stood at 67.3 micrograms per cubic metre in 2025, nearly 14 times the World Health Organisation's annual guideline of 5 micrograms per cubic metre, corresponding to an average AQI of 156, according to the 2025 World Air Quality Report.
