Judicial Commission on Mir Raza's Murder Hands Sealed Report to Sindh Govt

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 16, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A sealed report prepared by the judicial commission formed to investigate the circumstances and possible negligence in Mir Raza Ali's murder investigation was handed over to the Sindh government on Thursday. The commission, headed by Justice Omar Sial of the Sindh High Court, concluded its proceedings last week after examining 49 witnesses during 15 hearings, including an inspection of the crime scene. The report and associated documents were handed over to the provincial government's focal person, additional home secretary Javed Ahmed Abro, by the commission's registrar Abdul Majid on the Sindh High Court premises, since Justice Sial was hearing cases at the SHC Hyderabad circuit court.

The handover included the executive summary, witness statements and supplementary documents, and Mr Abro told reporters it was now the government's prerogative to decide whether the report would be made public or kept secret. The provincial government had formed the commission in August, tasking it with recommendations for further investigation, forensic analysis or legal and departmental action within 30 days, but the commission sought and received a cabinet-approved extension till October 15. The commission had also sent questionnaires to Karachi police chief Azad Khan, DIG Amir Farooqui and DIG East Farrukh Lanjar after dismissing the family's application to summon the senior officers, and the three submitted written answers.

The 25-year-old businessman and Institute of Business Administration graduate, Mir Raza Ali, had gone missing on July 28 after leaving his PECHS home, and his body with gunshot wounds was found a day later in bushes in a Gulistan-i-Jauhar locality. Police had initially built the case around suicide on the basis of the first autopsy, which indicated a bullet entry wound on the chest, citing business losses and borrowed money, but police surgeon Dr Summaiya Syed disputed the first post-mortem conducted by medico-legal officer Dr Osama Sheikh.
