'A paradise beside the lakes': Nagar, where a 1,200-year tradition of hereditary rule still lives on

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The former state of Nagar in Gilgit-Baltistan is a land of snow-covered peaks, glaciers and green valleys, known for gems including ruby, topaz and emerald. Its hereditary rulers, the Mirs of Nagar, governed the valley for centuries, in a tradition said to stretch back 1,200 years. In 1974, the government of Zulfikar Ali Bhutto abolished the state system, ending the powers of the Mir of Nagar and bringing in a modern administrative system.

Even so, the tradition of hereditary rule is still alive in symbolic form today. Observers say Nagar offers around 25 different varieties of apricot, along with walnuts and traditional foods that reflect the valley's distinct culture. For visitors, spending a night under a sky full of stars near the glaciers is an experience in itself.

The valley, often described as a paradise beside the lakes, continues to draw people with its natural beauty and its long history.
