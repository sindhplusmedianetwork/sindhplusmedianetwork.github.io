77 Million Email Addresses Stolen in Arara Message Hack; 380 Japanese Firms Affected

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 04, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Hackers have stolen roughly 77 million email addresses in a cyberattack on Arara Message, an email service widely used by Japanese companies to contact their customers. Tokyo-based IT company PayCloud Holdings said on Monday that attackers gained illegal access to servers run by its subsidiary Arara and extracted customer databases. Up to about 380 companies' users are affected, and the compromised data may include names, phone numbers, birth dates and addresses — though the company says credit card numbers were never stored in the service.

The scale of the breach grew sharply: Arara first reported about 3 million leaked addresses on October 29, but a second official report on November 2 revealed that a third party had accessed 97 client-company servers between October 28 and 31. The attackers also abused the service itself, sending a maximum of about 7.13 million fraudulent emails in the names of credit card companies between October 28 and 30. Because those messages came from Arara's genuine addresses, many email filters found them hard to identify as fake.

The company shut the service down completely on October 31 and has given no date for its return, warning the public to stay alert for suspicious emails and phone calls. Arara says it will restore the service only once the attackers' entry route is found and security is confirmed, and a third report will give the final data-leak figures.
