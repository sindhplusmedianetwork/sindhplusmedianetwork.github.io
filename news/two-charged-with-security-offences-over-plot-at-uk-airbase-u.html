Two Charged With Security Offences Over Plot at UK Airbase Used by US

United Kingdom anti-terrorism police said on Tuesday that two men have been charged with national security offences after an incident last month at a British airbase used by the United States, linking the scare to Iran. The head of National Counter Terrorism Policing, Laurence Taylor, said two British men aged 24 had been charged: one with assisting a foreign intelligence service, believed to be in Iran, and another with entering a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the United Kingdom. This is the first time anyone has been charged over the incident at RAF Fairford, after police arrested seven suspects in the days afterwards before releasing them on bail.

Police were called to the Royal Air Force's Fairford base in southwest England in the early hours of September 27 after a local farmer reported a group of men with several white vans engaged in suspicious activity. Taylor said that while some circumstances remain unclear, police are confident the activity was conducted on behalf of a foreign intelligence service. Days after the incident, the United States withdrew all its B-1 bombers from the base.

Police charged 24-year-old Nouman Essghir with entering or approaching a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the interests of the UK, after he was arrested at the scene in Gloucestershire, released on bail, and re-arrested a week ago. The second man, 24-year-old Mohmed Ahmad Abdulkarim, was charged with assisting a foreign intelligence service, which police believe to be Iran, while six other men remain on bail, one of them a dual UK-Iranian national who had been re-arrested last week.
