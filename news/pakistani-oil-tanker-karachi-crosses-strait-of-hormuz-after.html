Pakistani Oil Tanker 'Karachi' Crosses Strait of Hormuz After Closure

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar has confirmed that a Pakistani oil-carrying ship has crossed the Strait of Hormuz and will reach a Pakistani port within the next 24 hours. The ship is named 'Karachi', and Dar said Pakistan's diplomatic channels had worked with their Iranian counterpart to secure its passage. After the American and Israeli attacks on Iran, Tehran closed the strait, through which 20 percent of global oil trade passes, sending crude prices up in world markets.

Bloomberg first reported on Monday that the Pakistani tanker carrying crude from Abu Dhabi had crossed the strait toward Pakistan. Ship-tracker MarineTraffic said the Aframax tanker is the first non-Iranian cargo to pass since the closure while broadcasting its AIS signal. BBC Verify confirmed the passage, noting the ship, owned by the Pakistan National Shipping Corporation, took an unusually long route close to the Iranian coast.

Analysts say the longer route may indicate Iranian instructions to the vessel, possibly linked to mine concerns in the waterway. On Sunday, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said many countries have contacted Iran seeking safe passage and that Iran is ready to talk, while India said direct talks with Tehran helped its own tankers pass on Saturday.
