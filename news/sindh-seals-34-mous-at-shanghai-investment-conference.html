Sindh Seals 34 MoUs at Shanghai Investment Conference

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 16, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Sindh government has secured 34 memorandums of understanding with Chinese and Pakistani businesses and government entities at the second Sindh Investment Conference in Shanghai, targeting investment in energy, manufacturing, agriculture and emerging technologies. Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah told the media after the day-long moot — held after a gap of 12 years and attended by around 400 delegates including investors, Chinese companies and financial institutions — that the agreements covered both government-to-government and business-to-business cooperation aimed at creating jobs, expanding industry and strengthening economic ties. A major focus was the Dhabeji Special Economic Zone, a CPEC priority project spanning about 1,530 acres in Thatta district, with proposed industrial clusters in automotive manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, electronics, textiles, steel and warehousing.

Shah announced that investors in special economic zones could benefit from a 10-year income-tax exemption and duty-free imports of capital goods, subject to applicable laws, and said a dedicated China Desk and Single Window Facility Centre had been set up, with each agreement assigned a focal person and reviewed at the highest level. "For us, the signing is the beginning of the work, not the end," he said. Key cooperation areas include Thar coal utilisation, coal-to-chemicals, renewable energy, electric vehicle manufacturing, charging infrastructure and mineral processing, while Sindh's wind-energy potential is estimated at around 50,000 megawatts.

The province is also courting investment in artificial intelligence, cloud computing and data centres, training around 1,500 young people annually under its People's Information Technology Programme, and highlighted the 435-acre Education City Special Technology Zone for software, financial technology, biotechnology, clean energy and electronics. Agriculture and food processing offer further openings in rice processing, mango-pulp production, date processing and red-chilli dehydration, backed by modern technology, cold-chain facilities and export logistics. Shah pointed to Karachi's commercial weight and Sindh's two major seaports — Karachi Port handled about 53.95 million tonnes of cargo and 2.651 million TEUs of containers in 2024-25, while Port Qasim has annual capacity of around 89 million tonnes — and cited existing Chinese-backed projects including Shanghai Electric's 1,320-megawatt Thar Block-I power plant, operational since 2023, and the Port Qasim coal-fired power project.
