South Korea's KOSPI falls 2.59% to nine-month low amid AI concerns

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

South Korean stocks sank for the fourth consecutive session on Tuesday, as lingering concerns over artificial intelligence dampened investor sentiment, and the Korean won weakened against the US dollar. The benchmark Korea Composite Stock Price Index lost 132.02 points, or 2.59 percent, to close at 4,968.48, falling below the 5,000-point mark to its lowest level in nine months. On Wall Street, the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped about 2.5 percent on Monday, while AI chip giant Nvidia shares slid 4.1 percent amid continued uncertainty following the departure of its CEO, Jensen Huang, last week.

Nvidia is scheduled to report its earnings on Tuesday US time, with investors closely watching its outlook for AI chip demand and infrastructure spending, and investors are also keeping an eye on AI giant Anthropic's planned Nasdaq debut on Thursday. The AI sector is also facing increased regulatory scrutiny after the US government issued new rules on Monday restricting remote access to advanced US computing chips and servers. Trade volume was light at 183.09 million shares worth 14.19 trillion won, or 10.61 billion dollars, with decliners far outnumbering advancers 709 to 182, and foreigners sold a net 1.44 trillion won, while institutions and individuals bought a net 543.56 billion won and 421.79 billion won, respectively.

Top market cap Samsung Electronics decreased 3.3 percent to 190,300 won, and chip giant SK hynix shed 1.96 percent to 1.1 million won, while the Korean won was trading at 1,338.2 won against the US dollar as of 3:30 p.m., down 1.7 won from the previous session's close. Kang Jin-hyuk, an analyst at Shinhan Securities, said the market came under pressure from continued fallout surrounding Nvidia, US restrictions on remote access to Nvidia's AI chips and concerns over the sustainability of AI demand, dampening investors' risk appetite.
