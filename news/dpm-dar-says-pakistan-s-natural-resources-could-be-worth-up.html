DPM Dar says Pakistan's natural resources could be worth up to $10 trillion

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said Pakistan's natural resources, including minerals, hydrocarbons and gemstones, were conservatively estimated to be worth between $8 trillion and $10 trillion, as he welcomed a Turkish seismic research vessel at Karachi Port on Friday. Addressing a ceremony marking the arrival of the Turkish vessel Oruç Reis in Karachi, Dar said people sometimes talk about Pakistan being a heavily indebted or debt-trap country, which he always negates, adding that the total debt of the Government of Pakistan is about $100 billion and $130 billion including the foreign exchange liabilities of private companies and public sector enterprises. He said the most conservative estimate of the mines and minerals, natural resources, hydrocarbons, gemstones and other God-gifted natural resources in the country is anything between $8 trillion and $10 trillion.

The deputy prime minister said the arrival of the vessel marked a new chapter of scientific exploration and strategic cooperation between Pakistan and Turkey, built on the two countries' longstanding friendship, and conveyed gratitude to Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan for their personal commitment to strengthening relations, while also welcoming Turkey's Minister of Energy and Natural Resources Alparslan Bayraktar. He said the Oruç Reis will bring together Turkish scientific expertise and the experience of Pakistan's national energy companies to conduct advanced exploration in the country's offshore domain, and that the data gathered by the vessel may deepen understanding of geological structures beneath the seabed and provide a stronger basis for Pakistan's future exploration plans. He acknowledged that offshore exploration is complex, capital-intensive and inherently uncertain, saying a seismic survey cannot guarantee a commercial discovery but no discovery can be made without seismic and exploration, and added that the prime minister had directed relevant federal ministries and institutions to strengthen coordination on a fast-track basis so that projects of strategic importance do not suffer from institutional fragmentation or unnecessary delay.
