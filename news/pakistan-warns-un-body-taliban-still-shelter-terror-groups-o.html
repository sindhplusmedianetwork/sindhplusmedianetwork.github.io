Pakistan Warns UN Body Taliban Still Shelter Terror Groups Operating Against It

By Sindh Plus Media Network

WASHINGTON: Pakistan has warned that the Taliban continue to allow terrorist groups to operate from Afghan soil, as a UN human rights expert urged member states not to normalise relations with the Kabul regime without verifiable improvements in human rights. Addressing the UN Human Rights Council's interactive dialogue on Afghanistan, Pakistan's representative Muhammad Jawad Ajmal said more than 4,700 Pakistanis had been killed in terrorist attacks originating from Afghanistan over the past three years. Ajmal accused the Taliban of maintaining close links with terrorist organisations and alleged these groups operated with impunity on Afghan soil with support from external actors targeting Pakistan.

He said the Taliban's failure to publicly denounce the groups demonstrated their complicity and active support. Separately, UN Special Rapporteur Richard Bennett told the General Assembly that five years after seizing power, the Taliban had shown no willingness to reverse systematic human rights violations, deepening restrictions especially on women and girls. Bennett urged member states to refrain from normalising relations with the Taliban until they reversed oppressive policies, citing recent decrees that further undermined women's rights and permitted child marriages.
