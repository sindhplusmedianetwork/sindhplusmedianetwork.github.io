Cyberattack on refrigeration systems hits supermarkets across Europe

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Grocery stores in at least five European countries saw temperatures in their cold cases soar on Monday, with several confirming they had been hit by a cyberattack similar to one that struck France three weeks ago. Supermarkets in Belgium, Estonia, Finland, Luxembourg and Sweden were among those affected in the latest attack, for which no one has claimed responsibility. In Belgium, Carrefour reported disruption in about 15 of its 700 stores, with spokesman Mich Vergauwen saying, "It's a cyberattack; there is no other reason," while Ikea's store in Mons also reported freezer temperatures rising suddenly in the early hours of Monday.

Chains Delhaize, Colruyt and Intermarche also experienced technical problems affecting their fridges in Belgium, and Delhaize reported that four of its stores in Luxembourg were hit as well. In Sweden, chain Coop confirmed six of its around 800 stores were hit early Monday, though the problem has since been resolved and the fridges and freezers are working normally; the affected stores had been connected to a 4G mobile network rather than the updated 5G system, which made them "more exposed" to hackers. Swedish chain Ica said a limited number of its stores experienced refrigeration problems, with all affected stores connected to an external refrigeration control system provided by Danish manufacturer Danfoss, the same supplier targeted in the October 12 attack on French retailers.

One Ica store in Sjobo posted on Facebook that its fridges and freezers had "been hacked and they set the temperature at 50 degrees" Celsius, while in Estonia at least one supermarket was attacked by hackers who raised refrigeration temperatures to 50 degrees Celsius. After the France attack, Danfoss had said most of the affected equipment were "legacy" products which "no longer receive software or security updates," and EU food and agriculture commissioner Christophe Hansen warned that food security required more attention at all levels.
