Punjab Insists Smog Is Not a Political Issue as Lahore and Delhi Gasp

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Punjab government insisted on Wednesday that there should be no politics over smog, environmental protection and public health, as these issues concern the lives, health and future of millions of people. “Smog is not a political issue. It is a scientific, environmental and public health challenge,” senior minister Marriyum Aurangzeb told a press conference on Wednesday, even as Lahore, Multan and other major cities remained blanketed under thick smog.

On Wednesday night, Lahore remained on top of the global ranking of major polluted cities, recording an air quality index reading of 358, while second-placed New Delhi recorded 248. The Punjab Environment Protection Agency found in its Diwali-period assessment that pollution-laden air from India travelled deep into Punjab, with around 50 per cent of the province affected, and that air released across the border on Diwali night could reach Lahore from Amritsar in about two hours, Faisalabad in 12 hours and Multan in around 11 hours. The agency said Lahore's overall AQI increased from 366 on Nov 8 to 440 on Nov 9, reaching its highest recorded value at 9am, and that the findings suggest cross-border pollution contributed significantly, though local emissions and weather also played a role.

Ms Aurangzeb said that on Nov 11, 2024, Lahore's AQI had exceeded 2,000 and reached about 2,400, while on Nov 11, 2026, it was below 450, which she said reflected the impact of scientific planning and enforcement. In India, toxic smog concentrations were so high that air pollution monitors were overwhelmed, with readings later hitting 879 micrograms per cubic metre in Delhi's Mandir Marg locality, more than 58 times the WHO daily limit.
