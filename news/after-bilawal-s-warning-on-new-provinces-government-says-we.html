After Bilawal's Warning on New Provinces, Government Says 'We Are on the Same Side'

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The federal government on Tuesday reminded PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari that they were part of the same government, after he launched a hard-hitting attack over the question of new provinces and called for a political and democratic reset. In one of his starkest warnings yet, Mr Bhutto-Zardari said any forced attempt to carve up Sindh into new provinces would lead to bloodshed, adding that he did not want to see any division imposed through force. Speaking at the Karachi Press Club after addressing a Meet the Press programme, he opposed a referendum on new provinces, saying that in the country's history a referendum had always been carried out for an undemocratic act and never a democratic one.

He said that up until now, neither the prime minister nor a single member of the provinces had brought up the issue with the president of Pakistan or with him, and that even the cabinet had not discussed the matter, let alone parliament. He also said the system had not collapsed but had been made to collapse, acknowledged his own role in the matter, and said mistakes must be fixed, while questioning the judiciary after the 27th Amendment, which he said was passed with brute majority and not through consensus. The PPP chairman openly supported the release of former prime minister Imran Khan and all other political prisoners, called for an end to the tradition of revenge in politics, and termed the return of reserved seats to the PTI a confidence-building measure.

Federal Information Minister Attaullah Tarar responded directly, telling Mr Bhutto-Zardari that they were part of the same government and should sit together to decide for the betterment of the people and the country. He said the people were the ultimate decision-makers, adding that if the people of Pakistan and Sindh wanted more provinces, they should be made, otherwise not, and that the debate would happen in parliament and outside it.
