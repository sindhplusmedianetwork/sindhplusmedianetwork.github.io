Karachi Gets Quaid-e-Azam Forest: 5,150 Trees Planted on Five Acres at Governor House

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A new urban forest named Quaid-e-Azam Forest has been established over five acres at the Sindh Governor House in Karachi, where 5,150 trees and plants are now taking root on land that once offered little relief from the city's concrete sprawl. The plantation uses Japan's Miyawaki technique, planting different species close together in a limited space so dense greenery can grow quickly, with fruit-bearing, flowering and shade-providing trees chosen to suit Karachi's local environment. According to Dr Saifur Rehman, Principal Secretary to the Governor House, the forest was created on the special instructions of Governor Nihal Hashmi and named after the founder of Pakistan as a tribute to Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah on the occasion of his 150th birth anniversary.

Indigenous trees were given priority, including neem, gulmohar, tamarind, jamun, guava, mango, moringa, coconut and lemon among many other varieties. The project aims to expand urban greenery, improve air quality, help moderate temperatures and provide a natural habitat for birds, butterflies and other wildlife, at a time when Karachi has lost much of its green cover to construction and tree-cutting over the past three decades. Dr Rehman said the initiative needed to be replicated across the city, with indigenous trees planted in neighbourhoods, schools, parks and government land, and warned that without regular care plantation drives would struggle to deliver lasting benefits.

A digital forest mapping system is also planned, using tools including Google Earth, QField, QGIS and PlantNet to document each tree, monitor growth and record biodiversity at the site.
