Villa Vie Odyssey shuts down in Cambodia, over 200 residents forced off ship

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

More than 200 residents of the Villa Vie Odyssey, the so-called 'endless' residential cruise ship, were forced to leave the vessel in Sihanoukville, Cambodia, in late October after the operator suspended operations. The Florida-based company blamed soaring fuel costs linked to regional conflict, which pushed monthly fuel expenses per cabin from about $1,800 to as high as $8,000. Many former residents say the collapse did not come as a complete surprise: Sharon Lane, a retired teacher who paid around $200,000 and left the ship in August, described long stretches without proper meals, with only cookies offered for hours, along with repeated requests for extra money to cover fuel.

In Singapore earlier, the ship risked detention over unpaid bills, and residents pooled cash — one person paid $400,000 from a personal account — to buy fuel so the voyage could continue. Internal documents later showed the company was millions of dollars in debt to suppliers, ports and crew, while town-hall meetings warned of possible layoffs, loss of insurance and service cuts including the internet. By September and October, appeals for more resident contributions continued as food quality dropped, itineraries slowed, the internet failed at times and crew pay fell behind.

On the morning of the shutdown, passengers were given roughly seven hours to pack and leave by 2 p.m.; many had sold homes and invested life savings in multi-year or lifetime residencies, and now describe shock, anger and uncertainty as they scramble for housing and flights.
