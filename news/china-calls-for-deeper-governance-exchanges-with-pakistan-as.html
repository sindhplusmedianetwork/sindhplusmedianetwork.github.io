China calls for deeper governance exchanges with Pakistan as CPEC 2.0 advances

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

China has called for deeper exchanges with Pakistan on party and state governance, saying the experience of the Communist Party of China could offer valuable lessons for developing countries pursuing modernisation and sustainable development. In an article, Chinese Ambassador to Pakistan Jiang Zaidong said the Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee of the party, held in Beijing from October 26 to 29, reaffirmed its commitment to strengthening governance, discipline and self-reform. He said the session adopted a resolution on advancing full and rigorous party self-governance, setting out guiding principles and measures to improve the party's governing capacity.

Jiang said the party's emphasis on self-governance had become a central pillar of party-building under President Xi Jinping and had helped the party steer China through economic development while maintaining social stability. He said China was willing to expand engagement with political parties around the world, particularly in developing countries seeking independent paths to modernisation. Highlighting Pakistan-China engagement, he said a party delegation led by Liu Haixing visited Pakistan shortly after the plenary and held meetings with Pakistani leaders, including an exchange meeting with Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, while President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif sent congratulatory messages.

Jiang said the upgraded CPEC 2.0 was progressing, with cooperation expanding into industry, agriculture and social development, and that Chinese companies Haier and Challenge are set to establish industrial parks in Pakistan expected to generate more than 35,000 jobs. In agriculture, he said China had donated 300,000 doses of veterinary vaccines, while bilateral agricultural trade reached 940 million dollars in the first nine months of the year, with Pakistan recording a 240 million dollar surplus, a 185.5 percent increase year on year.
