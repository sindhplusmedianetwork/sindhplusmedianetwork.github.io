Power producers and industry urge scrapping of fuel levies to cut electricity cost

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

ISLAMABAD: The manufacturing sector and the power division have joined hands in demanding that the petroleum levy on furnace oil and diesel used for electricity generation be removed or reduced, arguing that the taxes add roughly Rs10 per unit to power costs. The demand came during a Nepra public hearing on Thursday on the Central Power Purchasing Agency's plea for an additional Rs2.56 per unit fuel cost adjustment to recover about Rs33 billion for electricity consumed in September. Industrial representatives told the regulator that the government charges about Rs76,000 per tonne in levy on furnace oil and Rs80 per litre on diesel, and removing these could bring generation costs down from around Rs62 to between Rs44 and Rs50 per unit.

Power division officials confirmed that furnace oil and diesel-based generation made up 3.6 per cent of total output in September, as LNG and other cheaper sources were unavailable amid the regional conflict involving Iran. PPMC chief financial officer Naveed Qaiser conceded that the levies had a substantial impact on generation costs and said the division had long fought for their removal. The CPPA said actual fuel cost in September stood at Rs10.022 per unit against a reference rate of Rs7.46, and assured that the net increase in bills would not exceed Rs1.45 per unit as an older adjustment expires.

Nepra members also raised concerns about discrepancies in generation data and coal procurement at the Port Qasim plant, where industrialists alleged inflated prices through affiliated entities.
