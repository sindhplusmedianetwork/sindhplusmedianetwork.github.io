US blacklists Alibaba subsidiary and Megaspeed linked to Nvidia scandal

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The US Commerce Department on Monday added Megaspeed and Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary to its trade blacklist, following criticism from lawmakers and administration officials. Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner under investigation by US authorities since 2024 over whether it diverted AI chips to China, will now need approval from the Commerce Department to obtain US exports, which is likely to be denied. The company gained the spotlight about two weeks ago after news broke that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang had an affair with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom US authorities suspect of being a Chinese spy, while China has said the spy accusations are groundless.

The Commerce Department also added Apex Enterprise Solutions, Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary, to the Entity List, after Megaspeed leveraged its relationship with Alibaba to secure designation as an official Nvidia cloud partner. Apex paid around 4.2 billion dollars to Megaspeed and other companies as a prepayment to rent computing capacity in Southeast Asia, and in return secured some of their assets as a pledge. Alibaba said adding Apex to the Entity List was erroneous because Apex has never diverted advanced computing chips subject to US export restrictions, and said it would seek all legal avenues to protect its rights and reputation.

The blacklisting followed growing pressure on Commerce over its handling of Megaspeed, including a public complaint by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on November 13 that the company had not yet been placed on the list, and it coincided with Jensen Huang's reinstatement as chief executive of Nvidia.
