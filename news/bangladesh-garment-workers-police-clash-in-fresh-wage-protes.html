Bangladesh garment workers, police clash in fresh wage protest

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Bangladeshi police fired tear gas and stun grenades on Tuesday in clashes with thousands of garment workers who were protesting after a key wage board meeting was postponed. Protesters hurled bricks, torched vehicles and blocked the main highway between the capital Dhaka and the key financial district of Mymensingh as demonstrations demanding higher wages turned violent. Workers are demanding a minimum monthly wage of 30,000 taka, about 240 dollars, a 140 per cent increase on the current 12,500 taka, as they struggle to cope with rising food, housing, transport and healthcare costs.

Some 500 factories out of 4,500 are shut, which is further stoking unrest among workers. The wage board, comprising representatives of employers and workers, was set up by the government earlier this month to negotiate a pay rise, and its meeting scheduled for Monday was postponed after a member representing the garment workers said he was unwell. The next meeting will be held on November 23, and the new minimum wage will be announced in December and take effect in January.

The garment industry generates 38 billion dollars in revenues annually and employs more than four million people, making Bangladesh the world's second-biggest garment exporter behind China.
