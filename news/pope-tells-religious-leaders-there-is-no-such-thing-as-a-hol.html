Pope tells religious leaders: 'There is no such thing as a holy war'

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 29, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pope Leo XIV told religious leaders on Tuesday that there is no such thing as a holy war, saying in a video message to a gathering in Assisi that only peace is holy and war can never be waged in the name of God. The American pope, 71, had been due to preside over the gathering in person but cancelled his attendance while recovering from lung cancer surgery on October 13, sending a videotaped message instead. The delegates in Assisi, the birthplace of Saint Francis, included the Archbishop of Canterbury Sarah Mullally as well as Muslim, Buddhist and other Christian leaders, and were marking the 40th anniversary of a landmark interfaith peace meeting called by Saint John Paul II in 1986.

In his message, Leo denounced what he called the troubling revival of war as an instrument of international politics and its religious justifications by leaders, urging the world to rebuild peace everywhere rather than prepare for more wars. His remarks came after Russia's Orthodox Church justified Russia's invasion of Ukraine as a holy war, and after leaders on all sides of the war on Iran used religion to justify their actions, including United States officials who invoked their Christian faith. Leo has consistently rejected such justifications, saying in his first encyclical that fighting in the name of religion means attacking religion itself.

The Vatican also stepped up humanitarian efforts for Ukraine, with the pope's chief almsgiver, Monsignor Luis Marin de San Martin, beginning a three-day visit carrying winter clothes, blankets and food to Kyiv, after Leo appealed for a ceasefire in the city of Oleshky and spoke by phone with Russian President Vladimir Putin on October 16 and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy three days later.
