PCB Keeps NOCs of Pakistani Players on Hold Hours Before Nepal Premier League Opens

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 26, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Pakistan Cricket Board has kept the No Objection Certificates of Pakistani players on hold hours before the opening of the Nepal Premier League, leaving the tournament's franchises in limbo and searching for substitutes. The third edition of the league, featuring eight teams, begins on Monday and runs until November 21 at the TU International Cricket Ground in Kirtipur. The board has also asked Pakistani cricketer Sahibzada Farhan, who arrived in Nepal to play for the Karnali Yaks, to return to Pakistan after being called up to the national team, while the other Pakistani players named in the report are Haider Ali, Hunain Shah, Hussain Talat, Iftikhar Ahmed, Hasan Ali and Usama Mir.

According to Grihendra Ghimire, director of the Karnali Yaks, Farhan may return to the league after the national team's match concludes on October 31, and in the meantime the franchise is preparing to bring in a replacement player. Mr Ghimire said the board had requested an additional $25,000 to issue No Objection Certificates for Pakistani players who had arrived in Nepal to participate in the league, and expressed dissatisfaction with the demand, saying it was made only after the players had arrived in Nepal with their certificates. He said the objective in bringing Pakistani players to the league was not just to watch them play, but also to invest in the development of Nepali cricket, which is currently in a developmental phase, and that under these circumstances the franchise was disappointed by the last-minute demand for additional funds.

Mr Ghimire added that the Karnali Yaks had been considering paying the additional amount to ensure the players could participate, but Farhan's call-up to the national team meant he must return to Pakistan, and the franchise expects him to rejoin the team after the match on October 31. The report, citing ESPNCricinfo on Sunday, said individuals at the league also said the board required a fee in exchange for the certificates, believed to be in the region of $25,000 for each Pakistan player, but added that it could not be confirmed that such a demand had been made in writing to the league, and that it remained unclear whether the reported fee was the main obstacle or whether the board had formally demanded payment, and the board has not publicly commented on the claim.
