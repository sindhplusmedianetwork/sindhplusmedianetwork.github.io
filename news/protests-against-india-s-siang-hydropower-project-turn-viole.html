Protests Against India's Siang Hydropower Project Turn Violent

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Opposition to a proposed 11,200 MW hydropower project on the Siang River in Arunachal Pradesh, in north-eastern India near the China border, has turned violent, with protesters setting fire to a National Hydro Power Corporation office and a local police station and vandalising the homes of a local MLA and the ruling BJP's state president. The project, described as India's largest proposed hydropower project, spans the Siang, Upper Siang and East Siang districts, and across the border in Medog, Tibet, China is building what is described as the world's largest hydropower dam on the same river, with 60,000 MW capacity, making the Indian project strategically important. Local tribal residents fear villages and agricultural land will be submerged and residents displaced from ancestral land, with local organisations saying 27 villages would be submerged and more than 150,000 people displaced, losing their livelihoods.

The project was declared a national project in 2008 but work did not start then, and in September 2026 the Arunachal Pradesh government formed a high-powered committee of experts to conduct a preliminary feasibility survey, which riverside villagers and local people have resisted. After a sit-in lasting several days, the situation turned violent on the previous Saturday, with protesters setting several vehicles on fire, while protest leaders say police used tear gas on peaceful protesters, injuring several women, which caused panic and increased anger. Police arrested several leaders of anti-dam organisations, large numbers of central forces including the Rapid Action Force were deployed in the Siang Valley, and the East Siang district administration banned protests and demonstrations in Pasighat and other places.

The state government says it is only conducting a preliminary feasibility study, not approving construction, and Chief Minister Pema Khandu said he was ready to talk to leaders opposing the dam, while organisations resisting the project say they have only paused, not ended, their movement.
