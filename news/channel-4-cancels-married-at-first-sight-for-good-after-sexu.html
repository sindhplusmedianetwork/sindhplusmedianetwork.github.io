Channel 4 cancels Married at First Sight for good after sexual assault allegations

By Sindh Plus Media Network

British broadcaster Channel 4 has announced it is permanently cancelling the popular reality show Married at First Sight after allegations of sexual assault and abuse by female participants. According to Reuters, the decision followed the findings of investigations into allegations against male participants on the show. In May this year, three women told the BBC's Panorama programme that male co-participants had assaulted them, with two alleging rape and a third alleging non-consensual sexual activity; the accused denied the allegations.

Channel 4 held two investigations: a review by law firm Clyde and Company examined more than 31,000 documents and 20 hours of video, and a second review by former BBC official Lorraine Heggessey recommended stronger safeguarding measures. The Clyde and Company report found safeguarding was broadly in line with broadcast rules but was not always fully followed. Channel 4 chief Priya Dogra said implementing all the recommendations would fundamentally change the programme's structure, so the sixth season will not be broadcast and a planned spin-off has been halted.

Channel 4 said the show will not return, its budget will be spent on new television programmes, and it apologised again to the affected women. The investigations did not rule on the women's claims, which remain under consideration by the relevant authorities.
