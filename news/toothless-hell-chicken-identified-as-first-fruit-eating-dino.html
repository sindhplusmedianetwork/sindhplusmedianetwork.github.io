Toothless 'hell chicken' identified as first fruit-eating dinosaur

Scientists have identified a strange, toothless dinosaur with a parrot-like beak as the first-known fruit-eating dinosaur. The creature, named Infernogallus jamesi, was roughly the size of a turkey and lived about 66 million years ago. It roamed what is now Montana and North Dakota in the final chapter of the Cretaceous period.

Its jaw was specially adapted for mashing soft fruits, the researchers found. The findings were published in the journal Nature Communications. Researchers said the discovery shows that dinosaur diets were far more diverse than previously believed.
