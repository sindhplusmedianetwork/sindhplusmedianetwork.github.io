Pakistan Turns Down $490 Million Trade Loan Over High Interest Cost

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 25, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan has turned down a $490 million trade loan offered by the International Islamic Trade Finance Corporation (ITFC), the trade finance arm of the Islamic Development Bank Group, because of its high interest cost. According to finance ministry officials, the loan carried a cost of about 8 percent, while the government had expected to obtain the facility at around 6 percent. The ministry's Debt Management Office took the decision last week, and after rejecting the one-year facility, the finance ministry instructed the Economic Affairs Division to explore obtaining a new facility from the ITFC or another source at a competitive rate.

The development comes at a time when Pakistan's overall financing needs remain high. Under the finance ministry's annual borrowing plan for fiscal year 2026-27, the country's total financing needs are estimated at 28,647 billion rupees, equal to about 20 percent of gross domestic product. Those needs include a federal fiscal deficit of around 7,000 billion rupees and repayments of more than 21,000 billion rupees in domestic and foreign debt principal.

The ministry put net external financing needs at 813 billion rupees, or about 2.8 billion dollars, though new borrowing will be higher because of repayments. The International Monetary Fund, in its latest report, forecast Pakistan's current account deficit at 1 percent of GDP, or about 4.5 billion dollars, at least 1.5 billion dollars more than Pakistani officials' recent estimate of 2.5 to 3 billion dollars.
