UN's Western Sahara mission begins wind-down after Council fails to renew mandate

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The United Nations' 35-year-old peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara has begun winding down after the Security Council failed to extend its mandate. The mandate of the UN Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara expired at midnight on Saturday after a US-backed draft resolution was vetoed by Russia, while a rival Russian text failed to secure the nine votes needed for adoption. Pakistan abstained on both drafts, calling for dialogue and consensus among the parties.

Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said the mission's departure did not cancel the people's right to self-determination and thanked the mission's staff, including the 20 personnel who lost their lives over 35 years. A smaller team will now oversee the disposal of UN equipment during a liquidation period, while his personal envoy Staffan de Mistura continues to pursue a political solution. Guterres also expressed concern over the territory's security situation and urged all sides to exercise maximum restraint.
