World food prices edge lower in October as cereals, dairy and meat weaken

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

World wheat prices have declined following four consecutive monthly increases, largely reflecting subdued international demand and an improving production outlook. According to the Food Price Index, published by the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO) on Friday, expectations of easing constraints on export flows added to the downward pressure on prices, although continued disruptions to Black Sea logistics limited the decline. The FAO Food Price Index, a benchmark measure of world food commodity prices, edged lower in October, as weaker import demand contributed to month-on-month declines in the price indices for cereals, dairy products and meat.

The index, which tracks monthly changes in the international prices of a basket of globally traded food commodities, averaged 133.9 points in October, down 1.6 points (1.2 per cent) from its revised September level, as lower price indices across most commodity groups more than offset a sharp rise in sugar and a marginal increase in vegetable oils. Despite the monthly decline, the index stood 5.9pc above its level a year earlier but remained 16.4pc below its March 2022 peak. The index says world cereal production is forecast to retreat from its 2025 record in 2026, while remaining at its second-highest level on record (2,999 million tonnes).

Production is expected to decline across all major cereals, most markedly for wheat, while coarse grains are forecast to fall only marginally. Rice production is set to contract for the first time in four seasons, falling by 2.5pc to 552 million tonnes, largely reflecting poor producer margins and El Nino-related adverse weather conditions. World cereal utilisation in 2026-27 is forecast to increase marginally by 0.2pc to 2,971m tonnes, while global cereal stocks are forecast to rise by 17.2 million tonnes, or 1.8pc, by the close of seasons in 2027.

World trade in cereals in 2026-27 is forecast to contract by 3.3pc, to 507.7m tonnes, with a fall of 8.6pc in wheat trade as the main driver.
