Moon Is Shrinking: Scientists Find Over 10,000 New Fault Lines on Lunar Surface

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Scientists have released the most detailed estimate yet of how much the Moon has shrunk over geological time. A new study published on October 14, 2026 in The Planetary Science Journal identified more than 10,000 relatively new fault lines on the lunar surface. The team, led by Thomas R.

Watters of the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum, used images and observations from the camera on NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter. They mapped thousands of surface features known as lobate scarps and small mare ridges, which form from compressional forces like those that shrink Earth's surface. The researchers estimate the Moon's radius decreased by about 45 metres over the past 400 to 800 million years.

They further concluded that if the shrinking rate has been constant, the radius is decreasing by roughly 6 to 11 centimetres per million years. The structures were found across vast regions of the Moon, including its highlands and smooth maria plains, showing the shrinking is a global phenomenon. Scientists first found solid evidence that the Moon was shrinking more than a decade ago, but the new work reveals the extent of the change for the first time.
