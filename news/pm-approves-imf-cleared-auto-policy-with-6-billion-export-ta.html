PM approves IMF-cleared auto policy with $6 billion export target

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has approved the Autos and Auto Parts Manufacturing Policy 2026-31 after obtaining a go-ahead from the International Monetary Fund, setting an export target of $6 billion over the policy period. Sources told The Express Tribune that the policy draft was shared with the IMF, which raised no objection. The government will introduce a money bill in Parliament, to be termed a mini-budget, after seeking the federal cabinet's approval.

Adviser to the Prime Minister on Industries and Production Haroon Akhtar Khan said the policy marked a comprehensive shift away from import-dependent assembly towards export-oriented manufacturing, greater localisation and internationally competitive production. He said Pakistan currently had about 2,200 auto parts manufacturers, and a Vendor Upgradation and Technology Transfer Programme with an allocation of Rs2.1 billion would be run through the Engineering Development Board and SMEDA, targeting at least five anchor firms for investment. The policy requires existing manufacturers to raise domestic value addition by 2 per cent a year, a cumulative 8 per cent over the policy period, with minimum targets by 2030-31 of 40 per cent for internal combustion engine cars and SUVs, 45 per cent for light commercial vehicles, 80 per cent for tractors and 90 per cent for motorcycles and rickshaws.

It sets cumulative exports of about $6.01 billion during 2026-31, including $3.29 billion from auto parts makers and $2.72 billion from automobile manufacturers, and projects cumulative foreign exchange savings of about $17.7 billion. Manufacturers that fail to meet export targets face penalties of 1 to 3 per cent of annual factory-gate sales, and two consecutive years of failure may result in cancellation of the manufacturing certificate, except in cases of force majeure.
