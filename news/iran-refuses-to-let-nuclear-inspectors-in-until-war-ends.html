Iran refuses to let nuclear inspectors in until war ends

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Iran will not let UN inspectors visit its damaged nuclear facilities until the Middle East war ends and the United States meets its conditions, a senior Iranian official said. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi made the remarks in a televised interview on Sunday, days after the International Atomic Energy Agency urged Tehran to resume inspections, which have been suspended since last year's 12-day war with Israel, when the US also struck Iranian nuclear sites. Gharibabadi said Iran would not grant the IAEA access to the facilities until its conditions are met and the war has permanently ended, and he alleged the agency's inspection request was likely aimed at assessing the effectiveness of American weaponry.

While IAEA inspectors visited Iran's Bushehr nuclear power plant in September, they have not gained access to other bombed facilities, including Natanz, Fordow and Isfahan. Iranian nuclear sites were hit again during the current conflict, which broke out on February 28 when US-Israeli strikes killed Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. The standoff coincides with surging violence in the Strait of Hormuz, where two maritime monitors said an oil tanker was set on fire by an attack, and the Revolutionary Guards said six tankers had caught fire and sunk there since Sunday.

Amid the clashes, the USS Theodore Roosevelt aircraft carrier arrived in the Middle East on Sunday, bringing the number of US carrier strike groups in the region to three. Meanwhile, Tehran is demanding that Washington unfreeze Iranian assets and lift sanctions, among other conditions, in exchange for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, while Iranian officials have said talks on the nuclear programme would come at a later stage.
