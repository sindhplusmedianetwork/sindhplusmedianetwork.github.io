Parishae Adnan says fashion industry dismissed her ideas before New York recognition

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Parishae Adnan said Pakistan's fashion industry had been slow to embrace her unconventional ideas, recalling how senior designers dismissed her plans for a public beach fashion show before international audiences began taking notice of her work. Speaking to Frieha Altaf on her podcast, the designer discussed the resistance she had faced throughout her career, the reception of her experimental approach to fashion and the economic anxieties confronting younger generations. She also spoke about growing up as the daughter of renowned designers Amir Adnan and Huma Adnan.

'I have been doing this for years, and everyone looked at the clothes and thought, that's weird. Now that it's in New York and people over there think it's cool, people here think it's cool too,' she said. Adnan recalled attending a gathering of senior designers a week before her first public beach fashion show, where her mother was discussing the upcoming event and the senior designers responded, 'Haha, that won't happen.' She also discussed her recent international outing, where she closed the Flying Solo show at New York Fashion Week in September, debuting her brand House of Parishae's sustainable collection, Second Skin.

Adnan recalled launching Awami, a brand that championed co-ord sets at a time when the concept was unfamiliar to many consumers, saying store visitors used to laugh and call the clothes men's clothes. She believes younger consumers are increasingly willing to experiment with personal style, with thrift culture playing a significant role. Beyond fashion, she discussed economic pressures, recalling a brand that offered her reel work with payment after 200 days, and questioned the gap between wages and the cost of living for millennials and Gen Z.
