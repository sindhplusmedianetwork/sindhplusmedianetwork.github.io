Intel shares plunge 20% on weak Q4 outlook as PC slowdown deepens

American chipmaker Intel's shares have plunged nearly 20 percent after the company issued a weak outlook for the fourth quarter, despite reporting better-than-expected third-quarter results. Intel announced results on Thursday, October 29, showing July-to-September revenue up 21 percent to $16.5 billion from $13.7 billion a year earlier. Adjusted earnings per share came in at 52 cents, above the 40 cents analysts expected, and gross margin improved to 43 percent from 38.2 percent a year ago.

But the market's attention stayed on the coming months. For the October-to-December quarter, Intel guided revenue of $15 to $16 billion, well below the roughly $17.1 billion analysts expected, with adjusted EPS of just 25 cents against expectations of 43 cents. Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner said the PC slowdown looked set to continue at least into the first half of next year, and investor-relations head John Pitzer told MarketWatch the PC market had stayed weaker than expected, with the impact arriving 'earlier and deeper'.

The main cause is fast-rising memory and component prices, driven by enormous AI data-centre demand, which has pushed up laptop and desktop prices and hurt sales; research firm Counterpoint says global PC shipments fell 4 percent in the second quarter. Intel's PC chip business, its largest segment, saw revenue fall 5 percent to $8.1 billion, below estimates, according to CNBC. The other side of the picture is Intel's data-centre business, which is growing fast on AI demand — revenue there rose 78 percent to $7.3 billion, which Zinsner called the segment's fastest growth in 15 years.

Another drag on the outlook was an explosion earlier this month at Intel's chip factory in Kiryat Gat, Israel, which shut the plant for nearly two weeks; the company expects a roughly $600 million revenue hit across two quarters, about $300 million of it in Q4, with margin nearly 2 percentage points lower. In after-hours trading following the results, Intel shares fell more than 20 percent — notable because the stock had risen about 150 percent this year, though it was already down nearly 23 percent in October on concerns about heavy AI spending. On the same day, Alphabet shares also fell sharply after weak results, adding to pressure on technology stocks.
