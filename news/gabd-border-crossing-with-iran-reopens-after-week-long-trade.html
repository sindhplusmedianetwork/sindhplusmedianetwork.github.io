Gabd Border Crossing With Iran Reopens After Week-Long Trade Suspension

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan reopened its border crossing with Iran at Gabd-Rimdan on Saturday, restoring bilateral trade and allowing stranded trucks carrying LPG and essential food commodities to resume movement after a weeklong suspension that began on October 17. Border officials cleared commercial consignments holding a consignment note dated October 16 or earlier to cross at the Gabd-Rimdan point, known as Gate 250, in Gwadar. Mir Naveed Jan Kalmati, president of the Gwadar Chamber of Commerce and Industry, confirmed that trade operations resumed on Saturday morning and welcomed the decision, saying it would bring immediate relief to traders and transporters stranded on both sides.

He added that the restored supply chain was expected to ease a nationwide LPG shortage and reduce soaring energy and food prices across Pakistan. Saturday's reopening came after local administrators reviewed the security situation and deployed additional forces to protect trade convoys, following an attack earlier in the week in which unidentified gunmen targeted parked freight vehicles near the closed crossing, setting dozens of trucks ablaze and damaging several others. Those trucks had been stranded since October 17, when Pakistan's Ministry of Commerce abruptly suspended trade at both the Gabd-Rimdan and Taftan crossings, issuing a memorandum withdrawing an October 3 trade order with immediate effect without specifying a reason for the halt.
