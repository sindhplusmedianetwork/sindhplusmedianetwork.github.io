Oil falls over $1 as easing tensions and oversupply worries weigh on markets

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Oil prices fell more than $1 per barrel on Thursday as easing concerns over an escalation of conflict in the Middle East reduced the risk premium in crude markets, while rising OPEC+ output and growing US inventories fed worries about oversupply. Brent futures were down $1.47, or 1.58 per cent, to $91.29 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude futures slipped $1.24, or 1.43 per cent, to $85.29. The declines reversed Wednesday's gains, when Brent rose 1.4 per cent and WTI added 0.6 per cent after both benchmarks hit two-month lows the previous day.

Markets are falling on expectations that behind-the-scenes efforts are underway to secure a ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine and that it is unlikely the Iran war will escalate ahead of the US-China summit next week, Hiroyuki Kikukawa, chief strategist of Nissan Securities Investment, said. However, given the uncertainty surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, the markets are unlikely to suffer a significant collapse, he added. Russian and Ukrainian negotiators agreed to a major prisoner exchange at two days of peace talks in Abu Dhabi that ended on Wednesday, although US mediators said differences remained on other major issues, including territory.

Adding to bearish sentiment in oil markets, OPEC+ crude production rose in October, driven by higher Saudi Arabian output despite ongoing shipping disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz. US crude stocks rose by 9 million barrels in the latest week, market sources said, citing data from the American Petroleum Institute.
