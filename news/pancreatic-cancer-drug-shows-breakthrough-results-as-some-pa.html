Pancreatic Cancer Drug Shows Breakthrough Results as Some Patients' Tumours Vanish

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A daily pill called daraxonrasib has shown remarkable results in early trials for pancreatic cancer, one of the deadliest and most difficult cancers to treat. The drug, taken together with chemotherapy, was tested on 61 patients who had recently been diagnosed with pancreatic cancer that had spread to other parts of the body. Tumour growth stopped in almost all of the participants, while in just over 60 per cent of patients the tumours shrank or disappeared altogether.

In about eight per cent of patients — one in every 12 — scans showed no sign of cancer at all. Cancer Research UK has described the findings as the biggest advance for pancreatic cancer patients in decades, with its Dr Sam Godfrey calling the results extraordinarily promising. Doctors stressed, however, that it is not yet clear how long the drug can keep the disease under control, and it should not be regarded as a cure.

The drug works by targeting changes in a gene known as RAS, which are found in most pancreatic cancers and also occur in dozens of other cancers, including lung and ovarian cancer, raising hopes it could help many more patients. The research is being presented at the European Cancer Symposium 2026, and doctors say longer-term studies are needed to find out whether the cancer can stay away for good.
