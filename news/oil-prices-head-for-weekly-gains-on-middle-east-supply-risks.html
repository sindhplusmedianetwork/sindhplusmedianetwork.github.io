Oil Prices Head for Weekly Gains on Middle East Supply Risks

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 16, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Oil prices eased on Friday but were heading for weekly gains on uncertainty over Middle East supplies and a larger-than-expected crude stocks draw in the United States. Brent crude futures fell 71 cents, or 0.67 per cent, to 105.21 dollars a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude futures were down 77 cents, or 0.79 per cent, to 97.20 dollars, though for the week Brent was up 0.44 per cent and WTI up 5.81 per cent. WTI had settled up more than 3 per cent on Thursday after a big US crude stock draw on hurricane production shut-ins.

ING analysts wrote on Friday that uncertainty in the Middle East and concerns over oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz had the market pricing in a significant risk premium, adding that a US-Iran agreement was needed to restore confidence in regional flows. Supply jitters rose as attacks around the Strait of Hormuz surged over the past 10 days, with Iran saying it was stepping up strikes in response to the US blockade of its ports and tougher sanctions introduced last month, while ship tracker Kpler put Hormuz clearance at 11.62 million barrels per day over the seven days to October 13, about 68 per cent of the baseline. The Houthis fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in Aden on Thursday, killing two guards, while the Saudi-led coalition detailed dozens of attacks on Saudi infrastructure.

The International Energy Agency agreed on Thursday to release over 20 million barrels of diesel plus remaining oil from a record March emergency reserve release to cool soaring prices, though Haitong Futures said overnight gains showed investors were dissatisfied and only an easing of Middle East tensions would really cool the market. The Trump administration is considering lifting sanctions on some shadow fleet vessels carrying oil from Russia, Iran and Venezuela to cut record-high freight rates, days after Trump temporarily lifted sanctions on Russian diesel fuel drew criticism from Ukraine's president, European countries and some Republicans.
