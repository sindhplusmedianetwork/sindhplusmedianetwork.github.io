Fourth-Quarter Growth Slows to 2% as Industry Contracts

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's economic growth slowed to just 2% in the fourth quarter of the last fiscal year as the industrial sector contracted by 4.7%, the National Accounts Committee has approved. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics said the economy struggled with continued fiscal stabilisation and the impact of the Middle East conflict, coming against a high base growth of 6.1% in the same quarter a year earlier. Agriculture grew 3.7% in the quarter, with important crops up 2.2% on higher wheat, rice and sugarcane benchmarks, but the industrial contraction pulled the overall rate down to a mere 2%.

The committee also approved a major revision of import data for five fiscal years, adding a cumulative $14.8 billion to imports after reporting differences emerged between PBS and Pakistan Single Window data. Imports for 2024-25 are now officially $64.5 billion, $6.1 billion more than earlier reported, while 2023-24 imports stand at $59.9 billion. Due to the slower final quarter, the annual growth rate for the last fiscal year was revised slightly downward to 3.65%.

Within industry, mining and quarrying grew 8.9% on higher gas, oil and coal output, large-scale manufacturing grew only 0.41%, and the electricity, gas and water sector contracted 33.2%. The services sector grew 3.62% in the quarter.
