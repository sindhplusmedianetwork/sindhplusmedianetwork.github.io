Iranian Filmmaker Jafar Panahi Gets Five Years and One Month in Prison in Absentia

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A Tehran Revolutionary Court has sentenced internationally acclaimed Iranian filmmaker Jafar Panahi to five years and one month in prison in a separate case, in a ruling issued in his absence. The ruling also orders the confiscation of all of Panahi's movable and immovable assets and imposes a two-year ban on social media activity and a two-year ban on international travel. Panahi's lawyer, Mostafa Nili, said he was not notified of the hearing beforehand and that he and Panahi only learned of the verdict after the filmmaker's bank accounts were frozen last week, when they visited the enforcement office at Tehran's Culture and Media Prosecutor's Office.

Nili said the charges in the new case have not been publicly clarified, and the case file described Panahi as living outside Iran even though he had returned to the country on March 30, 2026. The new sentence adds to a separate one-year prison term imposed on Panahi in December 2025 over propaganda charges against the Iranian government, which a Tehran court upheld in June 2026 along with a two-year travel ban. According to his lawyer, Panahi currently has three open legal cases, he has not been imprisoned as a result of the latest ruling, and it remains unclear whether or when the sentences will be implemented.

Panahi, whose film It Was Just an Accident won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and was nominated for an Oscar as France's official entry, has faced repeated legal action from Iranian authorities over the past two decades.
