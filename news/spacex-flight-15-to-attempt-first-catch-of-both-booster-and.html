SpaceX Flight 15 to Attempt First Catch of Both Booster and Starship

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 09, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

SpaceX has announced that Starship Flight 15 will attempt something never done before in space history: catching both parts of its giant rocket at the launch tower. The flight is expected on Friday, November 13, from the company's Starbase site in Texas, although regulatory approval is still pending and the schedule may change. About two minutes and 21 seconds after launch, the Super Heavy booster will separate and return to Starbase, where the tower's arms, known as chopsticks, will try to catch it about six minutes and 35 seconds after launch.

The Starship spacecraft will climb to an orbit of about 275 kilometres, release 26 new Starlink V3 satellites and circle the Earth about 14 times over more than 22 hours before returning to be caught about 22 hours and 32 minutes after launch. SpaceX chief Elon Musk said that if both parts are caught safely, it will be the first time all parts of an orbital rocket have returned to Earth intact. The previous flight, Flight 14 on September 28, reached orbit for the first time and deployed 26 satellites, but ended after about two orbits when an engine shut down early, and the ship landed in the North Pacific.
