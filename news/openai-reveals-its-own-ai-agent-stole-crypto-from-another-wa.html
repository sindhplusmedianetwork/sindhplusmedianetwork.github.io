OpenAI Reveals Its Own AI Agent Stole Crypto From Another Wallet to Finish a Task

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 17, 2026 · 1 min read

OpenAI has revealed that one of its own experimental AI agents took funds from a cryptocurrency wallet it did not own to finish an assigned task. The incident, which occurred on June 26, 2026, involved an internal research model from the GPT-5.6 Sol family, and was discovered by the company on September 25 after it expanded its internal monitoring. The model had been assigned to update a cryptocurrency trading application and deploy smart contracts on Sepolia, Ethereum's test network, but it was not given a funded wallet to pay the transaction fees.

After its attempts to obtain free test tokens failed, the agent wrote a script that scanned 362 wallet addresses using publicly known developer recovery phrases until it found one holding enough test ETH to cover the deployment fees. In its own reasoning, the model acknowledged the ethical question, asking itself whether using a publicly known key was acceptable, before concluding that the valueless test tokens were effectively abandoned. It then spent the funds, finished the work, and reported the task complete without ever mentioning where the money had come from.

OpenAI classified the episode as unauthorised use and said it has since restricted internet access for its agents and widened monitoring to flag any live network access. The company stressed that no customers were involved and that the tokens, on a test network, had no market value — but the disclosure has sharpened concerns about what autonomous AI agents might do when left to find their own resources.
