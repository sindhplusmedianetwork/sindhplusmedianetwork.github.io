Japan Launches World's First Mission to Bring Back Samples From Mars' Moon

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency on Tuesday successfully launched a spacecraft on the world's first mission to collect samples, including rocks and sand, from one of Mars' moons. The probe, carried aboard H3 rocket No. 10 for the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) mission, lifted off from the Tanegashima Space Center in southwestern Japan before dawn on October 20 and was placed into its planned orbit.

The spacecraft will head for Phobos, the larger of Mars' two moons, which orbits about 6,000 kilometres above the planet's surface. JAXA aims to collect at least 10 grams of material from Phobos' surface and return it to Earth in 2031, when the sample capsule is expected to land. The launch marked the first interplanetary mission for Japan's H3 rocket, whose inaugural flight failed in 2023 when its second-stage engine did not ignite.

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi welcomed the successful launch on social media, expressing hope that the historic endeavour succeeds. The mission, originally slated for 2024, is a joint international effort to investigate the origins of the Martian moons and develop technologies for future space exploration.
