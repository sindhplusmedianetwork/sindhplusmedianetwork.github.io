14-province idea on the table, says planning minister

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal says he has heard of a plan for 14 provinces in Pakistan, with four in Punjab, four in Sindh, three in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and three in Balochistan. He told The News that there has also been discussion about giving Islamabad separate provincial status. Iqbal said he wrote to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in September, recommending the formation of a committee to examine the debate around creating additional provinces, but no progress had been made on the matter so far.

On Islamabad's governance, he said the prime minister had tasked him with developing an alternative administrative model for the capital, and after examining capital cities around the world, a hybrid model between a local government and a provincial assembly was presented. Under the proposed arrangement, Islamabad could have a chief minister or mayor and an assembly of around 25 members, comprising 21 general seats, three reserved for women and one for minorities, with an elected third tier of government in the form of union councils to follow at a later stage. Iqbal said he had also heard discussions about expanding Islamabad's boundaries to incorporate adjoining areas of Rawalpindi division, potentially giving the capital the status of an independent province.

He said the question of creating additional provinces would have to be resolved before the Senate elections scheduled for March 2027, because the 96-member upper house currently gives equal representation to each of the four provinces, and new provinces could otherwise complain of lacking proportionate representation for six years. Iqbal also dismissed speculation about a presidential system, saying he had not heard of any serious proposal to introduce one, and urged politicians to take the lead in advancing the debate, saying Pakistan needs administrative reforms because after the 18th Amendment, provinces did not devolve any functions, creating top-heavy provincial structures.
