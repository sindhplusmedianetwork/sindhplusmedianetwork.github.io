Online Attacks on Women Journalists Are Digital Violence, Not Mere Trolling, Say Senior Journalists

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Senior journalist and anchor Gharida Farooqi says calling the attacks on women journalists on social media merely trolling is not correct, because they include rape and death threats, character assassination and attempts to silence them. In her view, this is online crime and digital violence, which should not be ignored as ordinary social media activity. Farooqi says online harassment of women journalists is not new and she has faced such attacks since 2010, when social media was not as widespread and people did not fully grasp the seriousness of online violence, but over time the attacks grew in intensity and methods.

She says that earlier, indecent comments were made or photographs were altered to present women journalists wrongly, but now they are also targeted through artificial intelligence and deepfake technology, including fake material in which clothing and other visible aspects in videos are changed, while personal and family photographs unrelated to their journalistic work are also used. According to her, the purpose of these attacks is not only to defame a woman journalist but also to silence her and obstruct her professional work. She has approached the Federal Investigation Agency, the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency and the courts, filing more than two dozen applications and petitions, though only a few have seen partial action, and she says victims in Pakistan do not get swift justice because of the long legal process and delays.

Journalist Fahemeeda Yousufi describes how sharing a news story led to abuse, threats and false claims about her personal life, while Karachi Press Club governing body member Sheema Siddiqui says women are often judged on gender rather than ability and notes that the press club has 183 registered women members. The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency says a dedicated desk and women officers handle such complaints, including sub-inspectors, while a 2018 survey by the Digital Rights Foundation found that 72 percent of women journalists had experienced digital insecurity, compared with 61 percent of men.
