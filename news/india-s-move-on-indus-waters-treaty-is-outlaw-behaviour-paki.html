India's move on Indus Waters Treaty is 'outlaw behaviour', Pakistan tells UN

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan has described India's unilateral decision to place the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance as outlaw behaviour, warning that the move violates international law, threatens the water security of more than 240 million people and risks escalating tensions between the two nuclear-armed neighbours. Speaking at a UN Security Council meeting on natural resources and conflict prevention, Pakistan's representative said India was seeking unilateral authority to decide when the treaty applied and what conditions should govern its implementation. First Secretary Ansar Shah said in Pakistan's right of reply that weaponising the waters on which more than 240 million Pakistanis depended was not a reassessment of the treaty but an act of coercion that created conditions conducive to conflict.

Pakistan's position was initially presented by Ambassador Usman Jadoon, Deputy Permanent Representative of Pakistan, at an Arria-formula meeting convened by Liberia on November 18. Jadoon cited the August 31 award of the Court of Arbitration in the Indus Waters dispute, which found that the treaty remained fully in force and that India continued to be bound by its obligations. Responding to Pakistan's intervention, India's Permanent Representative, Ambassador Harish Parvathaneni, defended the use of existing international mechanisms for managing resource-related disputes.

In his right of reply, Shah said the 1960 treaty contained no provision allowing either party to place it unilaterally in abeyance, and he urged India to abandon its hubris and comply with international law and its obligations under treaties and Security Council resolutions.
