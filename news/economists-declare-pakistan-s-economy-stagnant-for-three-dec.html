Economists declare Pakistan's economy stagnant for three decades at Harvard conference

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A conference on Pakistan at Harvard University, titled 'From Potential to Performance', heard sharp criticism of the country's economic performance from leading economists. Nobel laureate Professor Daron Acemoglu said Pakistan's economy has been stagnant for the past three decades, pointing to weak institutions, insufficient investment in education and a low tax-to-GDP ratio as the fundamental causes. He warned that without digital infrastructure, Pakistan risks falling further behind in an AI-driven world.

Economist Professor Atif Mian said the country lacked strategic vision, suffered from elite dominance and unequal economic growth, and had failed to establish internal peace. Mian added that Pakistan could tackle its energy crisis by shifting transport to electricity, but powerful interest groups were resisting the change. Federal Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb acknowledged the concerns, saying political-economy obstacles had delayed difficult decisions for years.

He said the government was now taking steps long considered politically hard, including tariff reforms, a tax on agricultural income and the privatisation of state enterprises. Aurangzeb added that Pakistan approved its national AI policy in 2025 and IT exports had reached 4.6 billion dollars. According to the economists at the conference, Pakistan's real problem is not a shortage of policies or proposals but the failure to implement them.
