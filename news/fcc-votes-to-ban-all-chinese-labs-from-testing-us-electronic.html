FCC votes to ban all Chinese labs from testing US electronics

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Federal Communications Commission on Thursday voted to bar all Chinese labs from testing electronic devices, including smartphones, cameras and computers for use in the US, expanding a previous agency action targeting Beijing. The FCC said it will prohibit test labs and certification bodies in countries including China that deny reciprocal treatment to US-based test labs, with the prohibitions slated to take effect in December 2028. FCC Chair Brendan Carr said that in 2025, 82% of electronics were tested in China, which does not have an agreement with the US.

'These labs are a critical gateway into American communications infrastructure — and their privileged role gives them access to sensitive and confidential information about electronic products often months before the devices enter the market,' Carr said, adding they could 'allow insecure gear into the US market, disrupt communications networks, or even steal intellectual property.' The Chinese Embassy in Washington did not immediately respond to a request for comment. In 2025, the FCC banned testing of US electronics by labs owned or controlled by the Chinese government, and several dozen labs were barred. About 4% of all electronic devices are tested in labs in the US, Carr said.

Separately, the FCC streamlined its approval process for devices tested in US labs or in labs in countries that do not pose national security risks. This is the latest move by Washington to crack down on China over technology issues, following December's ban on imports of new models of Chinese drones and consumer routers and July's vote to bar sales of devices containing key hardware from Chinese firms deemed national security risks, including Huawei and ZTE.
