PM Shehbaz Sets Up Committee to Review Proposed LPG Import, Pricing Overhaul

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

ISLAMABAD: Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has constituted an eight-member ministerial committee to review a set of policy changes proposed by the Petroleum Division for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), amid soaring prices following supply disruptions. The proposed reforms envisage allowing state-owned enterprises (SOEs) to enter the LPG import business and take over price-setting from the regulator, with prices subsequently passed on to the private sector. The committee was formed after LPG stakeholders, including marketing companies and dealers, protested against finalisation of the new LPG policy without consulting them; the policy had been scheduled to take effect within a week.

Stakeholders warned that SOEs had limited LPG import experience and that a proposed levy on locally produced LPG would push prices higher, calling for a greater private-sector role. LPG prices in the market currently range between Rs6,000 and Rs7,200 per 11.57kg cylinder against the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority's notified price of Rs3,296, as operations at the Iran border came to a halt earlier this month and imports through sea routes declined. The committee, headed by the petroleum minister, has one month to review the proposed reforms, cylinder standards and the local LPG model and submit a comprehensive report.

Under the proposal, SOEs including PSO, Sui companies, PARCO and Pakistan LNG would import LPG in bulk and set monthly prices based on Saudi Aramco's rates plus actual import costs.
