2.5 million Muslim voters could shape US midterm battles in key states

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 03, 2026 · 1 min read

Muslim voters are gaining unusual prominence in the United States' midterm elections, with their ballots potentially deciding congressional races in several battleground states. According to the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR), about 8 million Muslims live in the US, including more than 2.5 million registered voters spread across 12 key states. A CAIR pre-election survey found 81 percent of Muslim voters determined to cast their ballots.

For 89 percent, a candidate's position on Gaza is extremely important, while 63 percent named inflation and rising costs as their top issue. Muslim voters in Michigan, Texas, Georgia, Pennsylvania and other states could tilt tight contests; in Michigan, Senate candidate Abdul Sayed's campaign is drawing special attention. A Muslim Network TV review found at least 51 Muslim American candidates contesting races for Congress, state assemblies and local offices.

Their priorities include religious freedom, health care, inflation, immigration, civil rights and US policy toward the Middle East. Most Muslim voters remain angry over President Trump's aggressive role in Gaza and now Iran, and view his immigration policy as rooted in hostility toward Muslims, tilting them toward anti-Trump candidates. The midterm results will decide not only the balance of power in Congress but also how much room President Donald Trump has to implement his policies over the next two years.
