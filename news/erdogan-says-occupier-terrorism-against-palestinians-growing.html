Erdogan says 'occupier terrorism' against Palestinians growing bolder

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 07, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan on Friday criticised the international community for failing to address Israel's actions in the occupied Palestinian territories, warning that the international system is failing to uphold peace and security. Speaking at the opening of the TRT World Forum 2026 in Istanbul, he said occupier terrorism aimed at forcibly displacing Palestinians from their ancestral homeland was growing bolder under the indifferent gaze of the modern world. He said more than 3,000 attacks and violations targeting Palestinians in the West Bank were recorded in October alone, and that the October 2025 Gaza Peace Agreement, which Turkiye helped broker, is not being implemented.

Erdogan said international institutions were watching from the sidelines as atrocities unfolded in Gaza, as previously happened in Rwanda and Srebrenica, and accused critics of double standards. Separately, Israeli forces and settlers carried out attacks across the occupied West Bank on Friday, including assaults on Palestinians harvesting olives, the burning of homes and farmland, and raids on several areas, according to the Wafa news agency. The agency reported that 15 foreign activists helping Palestinians harvest olives near Jabal al-Risan were assaulted and detained, while occupiers set fire to land in Burqa and residential tents in Duma.

The Palestinian Wall and Settlement Resistance Commission said it had documented 409 attacks on olive farmers, trees, land and equipment across the West Bank between October 1 and 21, of which 317 were carried out by settlers and 92 by the Israeli army.
