Saudi firm explores $30 billion Pakistan gas deal

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Saudi Arabian energy company Delta Energy Saudi has signed an agreement with Pakistan's Inter State Gas Systems (ISGS) to explore the development of energy infrastructure in the country, involving a proposed investment of about $30 billion, the companies said on Tuesday. The programme envisages assessing a natural gas pipeline entering Pakistan through its western border and extending towards Gwadar, and it would also examine a possible extension of the pipeline to the eastern border, alongside liquefied natural gas (LNG) facilities, gas processing and storage infrastructure, and export terminals. The proposed network also includes a pipeline from Chaman, on Pakistan's border with Afghanistan, across the country towards the Indian border near Fazilka, a route Delta Energy described as a 'core strategic commitment' that would require formal engagement with the Pakistani and Indian authorities.

The proposed investment comes as other pipeline projects intended to bring natural gas into Pakistan have faced delays for years. Delta Energy identified Afghanistan's Kashk gas concession and other Afghan resources as potential sources of supply, and said the programme was separate from the Turkmenistan-Afghanistan-Pakistan-India (TAPI) pipeline project and did not depend on Turkmenistan. The agreement provides a framework for evaluating the proposed projects and does not constitute a final investment decision or a commitment to finance or build the infrastructure, the companies said.
