Tensions rise over proposed deal at COP31 as draft draws criticism

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Climate negotiators warned on Tuesday that a sprawling and contentious draft deal at COP31 has set the stage for tough negotiations before the UN climate talks in Turkiye end later this week. The 18-page draft 'cover decision', published by co-host Australia, includes a broad range of demands, from calls for a phaseout of fossil fuels to a defence of climate science and long-running battles over finance. The European Union's climate envoy, Wopke Hoekstra, said the draft could make the process 'untenable' and described it as having 'many of the characteristics of a shopping list' that reopens old issues.

Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen, who is steering the negotiations, acknowledged that the text included elements 'that not all parties agree on' and said there would be 'at least one more draft' before the end of the week. The text includes a call for wealthy nations to achieve net zero emissions by 2030, a target few could realistically meet. The draft's wording on phasing out fossil fuels has long been resisted by oil-rich countries, and Saudi Arabia is also at the centre of a separate battle over the timeline for publishing the UN climate science panel's next reports.
