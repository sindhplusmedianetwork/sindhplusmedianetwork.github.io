Netanyahu Denies Clash With Army Chief Over Iran-Linked Operation Before Election

October 26, 2026 · 1 min read

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Sunday denied reports of a clash with Army Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir over a proposed military operation that could trigger fighting with Iran ahead of Israel's parliamentary election on October 27. Channel 12 had reported that Zamir had asked for a detailed discussion and signed legal authorisation from political leaders before carrying out their instructions, without disclosing the nature of the operation. The report cited military concerns that political leaders could exploit tensions with Iran for electoral gain, and a security official warned that the operation could rapidly escalate into a confrontation with Iran at a sensitive time.

Netanyahu told Channel 14 that there was no confrontation, and political officials also rejected the claim that the Iran issue was being used for electoral purposes. In the same interview, Netanyahu said he had instructed the Mossad intelligence agency and the military to work towards toppling Iran's government, though Channel 12 did not establish whether the undisclosed operation was connected to those instructions. Defence Minister Israel Katz said on Monday that reports of a rift were entirely baseless, insisting there was complete coordination between the political echelon and the army, and that Israel had no intention of initiating an attack on Iran before the elections.

Opposition leader Yair Lapid accused Netanyahu of seeking to set off an escalation to postpone the vote, a claim Netanyahu's camp rejected.
