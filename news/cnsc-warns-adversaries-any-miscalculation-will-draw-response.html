CNSC Warns Adversaries: Any Miscalculation Will Draw Response 'Beyond Imagination'

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Commander National Strategic Command General Aamer Raza on Saturday issued a stern warning to Pakistan's adversaries, declaring that 'any miscalculation will be met with a response far beyond the imagination or the calculation of the initiator'. Addressing the passing-out parade of cadets of the 154th Long Course at the Pakistan Military Academy in Kakul, he said there was 'no space for conflict or war in a nuclearised environment' and reiterated that the armed forces stood ready to 'defend every inch of the motherland' at all costs. The warning came in response to provocative remarks by Indian Defence Minister Rajnath Singh, who had warned Pakistan on Thursday that any 'fresh terrorist activity from across the border would invite a strike even bigger than the last time', according to Indian news agency ANI.

Gen Raza said that in the 'post-truth era', adversaries were trying to drive a wedge between the Pakistani people and the armed forces through 'calculated disinformation and narrative warfare', and that after the failure of Operation Sindoor they sought to fracture Pakistan's social cohesion through proxy terrorism. He urged the newly-graduated cadets to remain steadfast and 'immune to malicious propaganda and narrative warfare', and said safeguarding the country's unity and territorial integrity was 'an absolute imperative'. The CNSC commander also pointed to Pakistan's 'decisive role' in restoring regional stability through the Islamabad Accord and the Makkah Accord, and expressed solidarity with the Palestinian people, condemning violence against non-combatants as violations of international law.

A day earlier, the Foreign Office had condemned Singh's 'provocative remarks', with spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan warning that Pakistan's restraint should not be mistaken for weakness and that any aggression would be met with a 'forceful response'.
