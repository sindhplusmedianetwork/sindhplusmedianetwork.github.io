Gallup: One in Four Young Americans Now in Treatment for Depression

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 20, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A new Gallup survey on life satisfaction shows that young Americans feel less happy and more anxious than in the past. According to the Gallup Life Evaluation Index, 27 percent of people aged 18 to 29 are receiving treatment for depression, an 11-percentage-point rise since 2019. Only 44 percent of that age group said their lives were moving toward thriving, compared with 64 percent in 2010.

Young people pointed to distance from friends and partners, declining happiness, and uncertainty about the future, and fewer than one-third said they achieved most of their goals over the past year. Connecticut-based psychologist Dr. Katrina Furey noted that Generation Z has grown up with constant access to smartphones and social media while also enduring the Covid-19 pandemic and financial pressures such as rising housing costs, adding that the loss of social interaction during formative years can have lasting effects.

The figures show the thriving share fell from 64 percent in 2010 to 57 percent in 2019, to 39 percent during the pandemic in 2020, recovering to 51 percent in 2023 before declining again. Relationship results were also worrying: only 47 percent said their bond with a spouse, partner, or close friend was stronger than before, versus 65 percent in 2019, and just 64 percent reported feeling happy the previous day — 17 points lower than in 2019. Dr.

Furey added that Gen Z is among the first generations to spend large parts of childhood and adolescence online, and research has linked social media use with eating disorders and depression. The trend is not limited to the young: among millennials aged 30 to 44, only 45 percent reported thriving, and among Generation X it was 46 percent — while baby boomers aged 65 and above rose from 38 percent in 2008 to 53 percent. The survey data alone do not prove that social media, the pandemic, or financial stress are the sole causes.
