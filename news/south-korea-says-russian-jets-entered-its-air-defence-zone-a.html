South Korea says Russian jets entered its air defence zone after false North Korea alert

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

South Korean authorities said on Tuesday that several Russian military aircraft had entered the country's air defence identification zone, correcting an earlier alert that had wrongly reported a North Korean plane. An emergency alert was issued on Ulleungdo island in North Gyeongsang Province at 8am on Tuesday, saying a suspected North Korean aircraft had left the danger zone, and the alert was lifted at 7:58am. Around 40 minutes later, provincial authorities issued a correction, saying it was Russian aircraft that had violated the airspace, without explaining the cause of the error.

South Korea's military later clarified that the Russian aircraft had not violated the country's airspace and that no North Korean aircraft were involved. According to Seoul's defence ministry, the Russian aircraft entered the air defence identification zone over the East Sea, also known as the Sea of Japan, and departed as South Korean forces were taking military measures, including deploying fighter jets. The ministry later said the Russian aircraft had flown right up to the edge of South Korean airspace and the alert had been issued as a proactive measure.

The ministry said it planned to summon the Russian defence attache in the afternoon to lodge a stern protest, its spokesperson Chung Binna told reporters. The inaccurate alert came amid heightened tensions along the heavily fortified border, after a recent mine blast injured three South Korean soldiers in the Demilitarised Zone.
