Faaz: why the test of Pakistan's first locally developed air-to-air missile is seen as significant

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 06, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan has successfully tested Faaz, its first locally developed air-to-air missile, the military's media wing said. The ISPR described Faaz as the country's first indigenously developed air-to-air missile, fitted with a modern terminal guidance system and capable of striking enemy aerial targets at long range. Defence analyst Bilal Khan said Faaz is a beyond-visual-range missile platform, with two main versions: one with an imaging infrared seeker and another with an active radar homing system.

He said the Air Weapons Complex has given the missile's range as about 100 kilometres, while the infrared seeker's target-detection range is about 40 to 45 kilometres. The test carried out on November 3 used the imaging infrared version, which builds a thermal image of its target and is less affected by decoys such as flares than conventional infrared missiles. Weapons researcher Hammad Waleed said Faaz should be seen as a missile family, with different versions for different targets, and that a Faaz-II with a range of 180 kilometres or more is expected.

Analysts say the missile could give the Pakistan Air Force greater flexibility in choosing and integrating weapons for its fighter aircraft, including the JF-17. The development is also being seen in the context of the aerial arms race with India, which fields the European-made Meteor missile and is extending the range of its Astra programme.
