Brewers Sweep Dodgers, Reach First World Series Since 1982

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 16, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Milwaukee Brewers are headed to the World Series for the first time since 1982 after completing a stunning four-game sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers in the National League Championship Series. On Thursday night at Dodger Stadium, the Brewers rallied from a 4-0 deficit to win Game 4 by 6-4, after Shohei Ohtani's three-run homer in the second inning — his first of the postseason — had given the Dodgers an early lead. Jake Bauers sparked the comeback with a two-run double in the fourth and drew a bases-loaded walk to tie the game in the fifth, before Garrett Mitchell's go-ahead solo home run in the sixth off Tanner Scott put Milwaukee ahead for good.

David Hamilton added insurance with a solo shot in the ninth, while relievers JoJo Romero and Aaron Ashby held the Dodgers scoreless down the stretch. Bauers was named NLCS Most Valuable Player after driving in six runs across the series. Milwaukee out-homered the power-hitting Dodgers 8-1 over the four games and became the first team in MLB history to sweep the same opponent that had swept them the previous postseason.

The Brewers, who finished the regular season with a league-best 103 wins, will host Game 1 of the World Series on October 23 at American Family Field against the winner of the ALCS between Cleveland and Tampa Bay. For the two-time defending champion Dodgers, the bid for a historic three-peat is over.
