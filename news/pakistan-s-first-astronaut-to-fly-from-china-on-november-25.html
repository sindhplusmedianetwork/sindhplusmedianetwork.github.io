Pakistan's First Astronaut to Fly From China on November 25, Says Ahsan Iqbal

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal announced on October 18 that Pakistan's first astronaut will undertake a space mission from a Chinese space centre on November 25, 2026. Addressing the concluding ceremony of World Space Week 2026 in Narowal, held in collaboration with the Pakistan Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (Suparco), the minister said cooperation with China would help Pakistan reach new heights in space exploration. He also announced plans to establish Pakistan's first Space Museum at the University of Narowal to promote scientific awareness among young people.

The astronaut will be one of two Pakistani pilots, Khurram Daud and Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, who were selected in April and have been undergoing intensive training in China; the selected astronaut will serve as a payload specialist conducting Pakistani scientific experiments aboard China's Tiangong space station. China has not said which of the two will fly, though a Suparco official said a final selection decision was expected in mid-to-late October. The minister also set an ambitious national goal of achieving a lunar landing by 2035, expressing hope that Pakistan's cooperation with China would help it reach new heights in space science.
