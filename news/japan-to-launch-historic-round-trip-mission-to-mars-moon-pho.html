Japan to Launch Historic Round-Trip Mission to Mars Moon Phobos on Oct 20

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Japanese scientists are preparing to launch the unmanned Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe aboard the H3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Centre on October 20, in what aims to be the first round trip to the Martian region. The spacecraft will orbit Mars's moon Phobos for up to three years, land to collect at least 10 grams of surface material, and return it to Earth, with the sample capsule expected to land in Australia in 2031. Scientists say the samples could unlock how the solar system formed and how life emerged.

The mission, built with France, Germany, NASA and the European Space Agency, also carries the small French-German IDEFIX rover, which will conduct surface observations on Phobos. JAXA officials say the mission lays groundwork for eventual crewed exploration of Mars by establishing the technology required for a round trip. Multiple Russian attempts to land on Phobos, including one in 2011, have failed, making MMX the most ambitious attempt yet at the mysterious Martian moon.
