India probes website blocks based on forged court orders

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

India has launched an inquiry into how telecoms officials blocked websites based on forged court orders, while police have sought ministry records as part of a criminal investigation, officials said on Thursday. An arm of the telecoms ministry this year directed internet providers to block 129 illegal betting websites, but Reuters revealed on Wednesday that the directives were based on forged orders issued in the names of two state high courts and emailed to officials by alleged fraudsters posing as lawyers. The findings have raised questions about safeguards in India's content-takedown system and prompted concerns that legitimate websites could be blocked using falsified court documents.

The telecoms ministry's inquiry will review its procedures and consider additional checks before officials act on court orders seeking website takedowns, a government official with direct knowledge of the matter said. The ministry plans to revoke the blocking directives issued on the basis of the forged orders and remove related documents from its website, the official added. Police opened a criminal investigation after checks by the Allahabad High Court found that one of the purported orders in its name had not been issued by the court, and the document contained a fabricated court logo and seal as well as a fake QR code.

A cybercrime official in Uttar Pradesh said police had asked the ministry for details of its content management system and digital logs, and had sent 24 questions to the ministry to help identify those responsible.
