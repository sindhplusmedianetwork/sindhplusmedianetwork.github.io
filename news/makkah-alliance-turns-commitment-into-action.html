Makkah Alliance Turns Commitment Into Action

By Sindh Plus Media Network

Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye on Friday reaffirmed their collective defence commitment under the Makkah pact, strongly condemning continued Houthi attacks on the kingdom, as Islamabad and Ankara pledged to accelerate efforts to support Saudi security, sovereignty and its territorial integrity. The commitment was made at the second meeting of the high-powered Strategic Political and Defence Committee of the Makkah Alliance for Defence in Islamabad, where the three countries expressed solidarity with Saudi Arabia over attacks targeting civilians and civilian infrastructure. In a joint statement, the three sides described the attacks by the Houthi militia as persistent and heinous, reaffirming their collective resolve to step up efforts to support the Kingdom in safeguarding its security in accordance with the collective defence provisions of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement.

The committee reviewed the evolving regional security situation and reaffirmed its commitment to collective defence, mutual solidarity and respect for the sovereignty and territorial integrity of all three countries. It also reviewed progress on implementing decisions taken at the alliance's inaugural meeting in Istanbul on August 31 and an emergency meeting in Riyadh on October 5, particularly those concerning its institutional structure and the practical implementation of collective defence commitments. Significantly, the three countries expressed satisfaction over the pace of progress towards establishing and operationalising the alliance's secretariat in Saudi Arabia, describing the secretariat as an important component in supporting the alliance's institutional work and ensuring effective coordination among its members.

The three sides also agreed to strengthen cooperation in defence industries, joint technological development, military training and capacity-building to enhance the collective capabilities and cohesion of their armed forces. The meeting did not announce any new military deployment or joint operational command, with the focus remaining on institutionalising the alliance and advancing practical arrangements for implementing its collective defence commitments, under the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement signed on August 7 by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, which established a framework for closer military cooperation centred on the principle that an armed attack against one member would be considered an attack against all.
