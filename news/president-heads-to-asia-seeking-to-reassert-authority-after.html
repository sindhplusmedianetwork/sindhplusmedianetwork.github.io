President Heads to Asia Seeking to Reassert Authority After Election Setback

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

After his party suffered a heavy defeat in the midterm elections, the US president faces a new challenge on his Asia tour: proving he is still in charge of foreign policy on the global stage. The 80-year-old president heads to the ASEAN summit in the Philippines and the APEC summit in China from a radically transformed political landscape at home, after the Democrats seized both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Constitutionally barred from seeking a third term in the 2028 presidential race, he enters the remaining two-year lame-duck period of his White House tenure.

Analysts say that after bad midterms, this is how presidents reset, and the tour offers an arena where he is unlikely to be pressed publicly on domestic politics. The president told reporters this week that he expects to meet the Chinese and Russian leaders at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, scheduled for November 18 and 19, with denuclearisation and ending the war in Iran on the agenda. But analysts say the bigger goal may be a single photograph of him standing with the two other leaders he views as his great-power equals.

The Democrats will not get an easy ride in undoing his policies, though, as the president enjoys broad authority over trade and holds veto power over congressional legislation, which he has vowed to use.
