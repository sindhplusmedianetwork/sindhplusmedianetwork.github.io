9pm closure cuts retail sales by 42%, study finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The early closing rule for shops in Pakistan has cut daily transactions at retail outlets by 42 per cent, while evidence that it has saved energy remains thin, according to a new study by research firm Ipsos presented by the Chainstore Association of Pakistan. Retailers in provinces where the 9pm closing time applies said their shops now open 1.6 hours less each day, with average operating hours falling from 11.2 to 9.6, and across the top 10 product categories the average number of transactions per shop fell from 86 to 50 a day. The share of retailers rating their overall sales as good fell from 79 per cent before the rule to 34 per cent now, while those reporting high profitability dropped from 67 per cent to 26 per cent, and the share seeing heavy evening footfall between 4pm and 8pm halved from 63 per cent to 32 per cent.

The rule was meant to save energy, but only 41 per cent of retailers saw their own utility bills fall, and in Punjab, Islamabad and Balochistan, where the 9pm rule is still in force, the figure was just 31 per cent. Half of the 300 retailers interviewed oppose the rule and 32 per cent support it, and among retailers who believed energy goals were met, only one in five wanted the rule to continue unchanged, while 57 per cent wanted the timings changed and 21 per cent wanted it removed. Consumers are divided, with 35 per cent of the 1,046 people surveyed supporting early closing, 33 per cent neutral and 32 per cent opposed, while 37 per cent said their shopping experience had worsened and 53 per cent said finding time to shop had become harder.

Sindh and Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa lifted the rule on 16 May 2026, the fieldwork was conducted between August and September 2026 across 53 malls and markets in 10 cities, and the study found that among 15 benchmark countries only Bangladesh shuts shops early to save energy.
