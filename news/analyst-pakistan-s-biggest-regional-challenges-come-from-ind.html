Analyst: Pakistan's Biggest Regional Challenges Come from India and Afghanistan

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 03, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Analyst Salman Abid has argued in an Express Urdu opinion column that Pakistan's most pressing challenges today come from its troubled relationships with India and Afghanistan. He writes that terrorism, border clashes, the Durand Line dispute, the suspended Indus Waters Treaty and blocked trade have made the country's internal problems, from a weak economy to governance gaps, harder to manage. According to the column, the Afghan Taliban government is effectively backing the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan despite repeatedly assuring the world that Afghan soil would not be used for terrorism against Pakistan.

Mediation attempts by China, Qatar, Turkiye and Saudi Arabia have not yielded any positive outcome so far, the writer notes, blaming the Taliban's hard line on the TTP issue. On India, he lists the Kashmir dispute, the suspension of the Indus Waters Treaty, trade restrictions and military confrontations as unresolved flashpoints, and accuses New Delhi of supporting separatists in Balochistan. The column concludes that Pakistan must run military operations and political strategy side by side, activate diplomacy at regional and global forums, and connect its diplomatic successes to economic growth.

The views expressed are the columnist's own.
