K-P hospitals still struggle despite 13 years of reforms, residents say

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 09, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The previous provincial government introduced the Sehat Card programme, Boards of Governors in public-sector hospitals and the Institution-Based Practice system, presented as efforts to improve healthcare delivery and strengthen hospitals financially. Residents, however, questioned how far ordinary patients benefited. Javed Khan, a resident of Gulberg in Peshawar Cantonment, said the poor had received little benefit and alleged that public hospitals had been privatised to benefit individuals close to the former ruling party.

He also alleged that billions of rupees had been embezzled under the Sehat Card programme. Abbas Khan, a resident of Mardan, said district hospitals still faced shortages of basic facilities and patients with cancer and other serious diseases struggled to obtain treatment and medicines. He said the situation was particularly difficult in remote districts including Mohmand, Bajaur and Kurram, where patients had to travel long distances for treatment.

Governor Faisal Karim Kundi said he had received numerous complaints about the healthcare system and would meet the health adviser to discuss hospital governance, the Sehat Card and improvements to medical facilities, and criticised the previous government for failing to establish new hospitals or basic health units. In response, the PTI's provincial information secretary Shaukat Yousafzai defended the party's record, citing hospital upgrades and the Sehat Card, rejected the corruption allegations and challenged the government to present evidence.
