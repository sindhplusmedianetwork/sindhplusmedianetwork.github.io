Fire badly damages Hitler's Eagle's Nest retreat in Bavaria; suspect held

By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 25, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A major fire has broken out at the historic Eagle's Nest retreat in Bavaria, southern Germany, badly damaging the mountain building once used by Adolf Hitler. Around 200 rescue workers were deployed to bring the blaze under control, but difficult mountain roads hampered the operation, and the fire was still smouldering on Saturday morning. Police arrested a 33-year-old German man near the scene as he fled on a motorcycle, and said no evidence of any other suspect has emerged so far.

Prosecutors said an initial psychiatric assessment showed possible signs of mental illness in the suspect, so the nature of his criminal responsibility is being examined, while the possibility of political motives is also under investigation. The Eagle's Nest stands at an altitude of about 1,800 metres near Berchtesgaden and was built in 1938 during the Nazi era as part of a complex of buildings used by Hitler and his associates during World War II. After the war it survived destruction and later became a popular tourist site and mountain restaurant.

Today it is seen as a reminder of the Nazi dictatorship, its atrocities and the importance of learning lessons from history. Bavaria's Interior Minister Joachim Herrmann said the building would not be left in ruins under any circumstances, and that the process of historical remembrance and learning from the past must continue.
