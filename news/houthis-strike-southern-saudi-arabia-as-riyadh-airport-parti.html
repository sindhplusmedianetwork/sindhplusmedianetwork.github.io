Houthis strike southern Saudi Arabia as Riyadh airport partially reopens after two-week closure

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 25, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Yemen's Houthi rebels launched wide-ranging strikes across southern Saudi Arabia on Sunday, as the kingdom announced the partial reopening of Riyadh's international airport despite repeated attacks by the group. Alerts warning of possible attacks were activated across the south throughout the day, with the Saudi-led military coalition saying at least 10 ballistic missiles were intercepted. Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport said flights would resume on Sunday after a two-week closure following the Houthi strikes, though it remained unclear to what extent the airport was operational.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree warned international, regional and local airlines, staff and travellers against using the airport, describing it as unsafe and a military target. The group has carried out waves of strikes on the airport, including an attack this month that killed 13 people and wounded more than 300. The coalition also said it downed a ballistic missile launched towards Riyadh and a cruise missile fired at the southern city of Khamis Mushait, with warnings issued for Abha, Taif, Namas and Jazan.
