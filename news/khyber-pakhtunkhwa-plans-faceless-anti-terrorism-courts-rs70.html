Khyber Pakhtunkhwa plans faceless anti-terrorism courts, Rs70 billion police upgrade

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa has decided to set up faceless anti-terrorism courts to try terrorists, as part of a plan to spend around Rs70 billion to address rising terrorism threats and shortages of police manpower, funds and modern technology. The province also plans to fill 17,000 vacant police posts, raise the Counter Terrorism Department's capacity from 12 per cent to 37 per cent, and take overall police capacity to 75 per cent. Under the proposed system, the identities of judges, witnesses, police personnel and others involved in terrorism cases will be kept secret during hearings, to protect them from possible threats from terrorists or their networks.

Legislation is being prepared for this purpose, while the concerned party will retain the right to appeal against a decision in the high court. The details were shared in a briefing by the provincial home department, attended by the governor, advisers, the chief secretary, departmental secretaries and senior police officers. Home Secretary Amjad Ali Khan said decisions have also been taken to convert several cities into safe cities, set up special centres in 10 districts and speed up intelligence-based operations.

Governor Faisal Karim Kundi said a zero-tolerance policy will be adopted against terrorists, and action will be taken if the JUI tries to use madrasas or their students for political purposes.
