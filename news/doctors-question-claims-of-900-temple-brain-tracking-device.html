Doctors Question Claims of $900 Temple Brain-Tracking Device

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A tiny $900 wearable device developed by Indian billionaire Deepinder Goyal claims to show how well the brain is being supplied with blood in real time, but doctors say the claims are exaggerated and are not backed by peer-reviewed research. The device, called Temple, is less than one inch wide, weighs under two grams and sticks to the side of the head between the forehead and the ear. Its makers say its BrainFlow feature tracks blood flow to the brain, while its Entropy feature detects how hard the body works to shed heat.

Prominent doctors have challenged those claims, with hepatologist Cyriac Philips calling Temple the biggest health scam and paediatrician Sanjeev Bagai describing it as a gimmick with no formal approval or clearance. Goyal has hit back, saying Temple is a fitness tracker and wellness device, not a medical one, and the company says the metrics involve emerging science, with more research to follow and some metrics eventually needing regulatory approval. India's health ministry did not respond to questions about the device.

Temple, which has professors from the University of Ottawa and Harvard on its advisory board, raised 54 million dollars in February at a valuation of about 190 million dollars, and Goyal says it has received orders from more than 62 countries since November 1.
