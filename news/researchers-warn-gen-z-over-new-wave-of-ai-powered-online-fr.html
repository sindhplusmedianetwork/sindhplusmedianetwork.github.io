Researchers Warn Gen Z Over New Wave of AI-Powered Online Fraud

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 21, 2026 · 1 min read

Researchers have warned America's young people that new and more sophisticated methods of cryptocurrency fraud are spreading rapidly on TikTok, Instagram, Twitch and online gaming platforms, where fraudsters use artificial intelligence and deepfake technology to grab people's money within minutes. California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued the warning on Monday, saying criminals are targeting Generation Z — people born between 1997 and 2012 — through social media, livestreams and various digital investment schemes. According to figures presented by Bonta, American citizens lost a total of $20.9 billion to internet fraud in 2025, of which $11.4 billion went to crypto-related deception.

He said these are the same old frauds authorities have always fought, but widespread access to cryptocurrency has given cheats a new and dangerous way to take people's money. Officials identified several common methods, including sexual blackmail — where criminals threaten to publish a victim's private photos or personal information unless money or cryptocurrency is paid — and the use of AI-generated voices and deepfake videos in which fraudsters impersonate relatives, celebrities or trusted figures to deceive people.
