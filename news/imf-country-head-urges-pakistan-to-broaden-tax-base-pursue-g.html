IMF country head urges Pakistan to broaden tax base, pursue growth-oriented reforms

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The International Monetary Fund has called for a broader, simpler, fairer and more predictable taxation system in Pakistan to generate resources for human capital development, infrastructure and investment. The IMF's Pakistan representative, Dr Mahir Binici, was speaking at a session titled 'Where Reforms Matter the Most for Growth?' at the Sustainable Development Policy Institute's 29th Sustainable Development Conference, organised by the Ministry of Finance. He praised Pakistan's efforts to maintain economic stability amid global and domestic challenges, but stressed the need for deeper reforms to put the economy on a sustainable growth path.

Citing IMF research, he said emerging developing economies need revenue of around 15 per cent of GDP for a strong, well-functioning state, and Pakistan remains far from that level. He identified five key areas for growth: preserving macroeconomic stability, fiscal and external sustainability with adequate buffers, fiscal discipline, governance, and the national tariff policy and cost of doing business. On human capital, he noted that spending on health and education had risen from around two per cent of GDP and was targeted to reach 2.8 to 3 per cent in the current fiscal year, though it remains far below what Pakistan needs.

He said Pakistan had done the hard work of stabilisation and now needed to turn it into more investment, economic activity, exports, employment and better living standards.
