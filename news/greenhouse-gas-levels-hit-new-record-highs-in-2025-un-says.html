Greenhouse Gas Levels Hit New Record Highs in 2025, UN Says

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Greenhouse gas concentrations in the atmosphere rose to new record highs in 2025, locking in climate impacts that will persist for centuries to come, the United Nations said on Tuesday. Levels of the three main greenhouse gases, carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide, all rose to unprecedented figures in 2025, the UN's weather and climate agency said. The World Meteorological Organisation said the rise would lead to more long-term temperature increases for the planet, in its 22nd annual Greenhouse Gas Bulletin, released ahead of the COP31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, running from November 9 to 20.

In 2025, carbon dioxide concentrations were at 426.8 parts per million, methane at 1,948 parts per billion, and nitrous oxide at 339.1 parts per billion, respectively 153 per cent, 267 per cent and 126 per cent of pre-industrial levels before 1750. Carbon dioxide accounts for about 66 per cent of the warming effect on the climate, while methane accounts for about 16 per cent. From 2024 to 2025, the global average concentration of carbon dioxide rose by 2.8 parts per million, smaller than the record surge from 2023 to 2024 of 3.6 parts per million, but still above the average growth rate for the past decade of 2.6 parts per million.

The bulletin said the past 11 years have been the warmest on record, and warned that concentrations can be expected to rise further in 2026 because of fossil fuel burning and the development of a powerful El Nino.
