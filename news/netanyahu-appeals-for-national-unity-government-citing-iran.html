Netanyahu Appeals for National Unity Government, Citing Iran Nuclear Threat

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has appealed for the formation of a national unity government amid the continuing political deadlock over forming a new government. He said such a government would work to end the nuclear threat from Iran permanently, preserve Israel's status as a Jewish state and strengthen unity within the country. In a video address issued on Saturday, Netanyahu invited all parties of the national camp and the Zionist parties to join a broad government that, in his words, wants to work for the country's security.

The appeal came after the 27 October parliamentary elections, in which no political alliance won a clear majority in the 120-member parliament, where 61 seats are needed to form a government. In consultations with President Isaac Herzog, 67 members of parliament backed former military chief and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, while 46 proposed Netanyahu's name. Netanyahu's Likud party emerged as the largest party with 27 seats, but its allies together hold 53, while Arab parties won 19 seats.

President Herzog has indicated he will choose a candidate to form the government on Sunday, and the nominee will initially be given 28 days, extendable by a further 14 days under the law. The opposition party Yesh Atid rejected Netanyahu's appeal, calling it a political tactic. Netanyahu also criticised opposition leaders who are trying to form a government with the support of Arab parties, accusing those parties of supporting terrorism and opposing Israel's identity, though these are his political allegations rather than established facts.

He announced no date or plan for any new military action against Iran, and Iran maintains that its nuclear programme is for peaceful purposes.
