Pakistan calls for fairer, more representative global order at UN

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan has called for a more representative international order and consistent application of international law during a series of debates at the United Nations this week. Its interventions covered Security Council reform, military artificial intelligence, weapons of mass destruction, radiation safety, human rights and conflicts in the Middle East, highlighting the need to balance international security with accountability and the interests of developing countries. Addressing the General Assembly on Thursday, Pakistan's acting permanent representative, Ambassador Usman Jadoon, opposed creating additional permanent seats on the Security Council, arguing that this would perpetuate existing inequalities.

He said the question is not whether the Council should be reformed, but how to make it more representative, democratic, transparent and accountable. Pakistan favoured expanding elected, non-permanent membership through regular elections and fair regional rotation, while seeking adequate representation for Africa and other underrepresented regions. On military AI, Mr Jadoon called for inclusive, UN-led governance, warning that the technology could accelerate military decision-making and increase escalation risks, particularly in situations involving nuclear weapons.

He also stressed the need for meaningful human control over autonomous weapons systems. At the General Assembly's First Committee, Counsellor Syed Atif Raza warned that advances in AI, synthetic biology and the life and chemical sciences were creating new security risks, and he called for safeguards against the misuse of scientific developments without restricting developing countries' access to peaceful applications. Pakistan reaffirmed its commitments under the biological and chemical weapons conventions, stressing the importance of verification and international cooperation.
