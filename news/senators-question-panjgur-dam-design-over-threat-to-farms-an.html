Senators Question Panjgur Dam Design Over Threat to Farms and Karez

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A Senate subcommittee on problems confronting underdeveloped areas of the country reviewed progress on uplift projects for Balochistan at a meeting in Islamabad on Tuesday and expressed concern over the proposed design of the Panjgur dam and its potential impact on local communities, agricultural land and traditional irrigation systems. The meeting, held at Parliament House and presided over by its chairman Senator Jan Muhammad Buledi, also reviewed implementation of the province's reserved quota in federal government jobs and the Aghaz-i-Haqooq-i-Balochistan Package. A major part of the meeting was devoted to the proposed design of the Panjgur dam, its canal system and its possible impact on local communities, date palm orchards, agricultural land and existing irrigation infrastructure.

Senator Buledi wondered why the existing irrigation system and its potential vulnerability had not been properly assessed before planning the project, and suggested the government consider building small dams as an alternative to large dams. Briefing the committee, the project director said Panjgur previously had 118 traditional Karez, which irrigated about 6,000 acres of agricultural land, but solar-powered water extraction had affected the network, with 72 Karez already affected and water flows in 34 Karez down by more than 50 per cent over the past two years. According to the director, the Panjgur dam has been designed to irrigate about 25,000 acres, will be 33 metres high, and will have a 30-kilometre canal on its left side and a 9.8-kilometre canal on its right side.

To protect thousands of date palms and farmland, the original open-canal design has been revised to a pipeline-based water supply system, and a cost estimate from China is awaited. In their reply, planning division officials said the dam's design would be reviewed and suggestions sought from the irrigation department, while the committee also sought a report from Wapda on the Gish Kaur dam project and its possible impact on downstream agriculture and water reaching the Mirani dam.
