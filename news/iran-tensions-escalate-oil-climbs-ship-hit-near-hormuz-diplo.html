Iran tensions escalate: oil climbs, ship hit near Hormuz, diplomacy stalls

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 21, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Tensions around Iran intensified on Wednesday as Brent crude futures rose 2.06 dollars, or 1.9 percent, to 110.85 dollars a barrel, with WTI at 99.95 dollars, after Iran said there was little reason to expect diplomatic progress with the United States. Yemen's Houthis targeted a military base at Al Rayyan airport in Al Mukalla, controlled by Yemen's Saudi-backed government, using missiles and drones. In a separate incident, a ship was struck by an unknown projectile eleven nautical miles off Bukha near the Strait of Hormuz, sparking a fire; the UKMTO said the crew is safe.

French President Emmanuel Macron said in Doha that he had secured the equivalent of two terawatt-hours per month of LNG from Qatar for winter. The United States is pressuring Iraq to close more border crossings after Baghdad shut the Sheeb crossing to passengers, vehicles and commercial trucks. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree denied launching drones toward Madina after the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted five drones.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio welcomed mediation efforts and reiterated that Washington will not support any deal allowing Iran to possess a nuclear weapon.
