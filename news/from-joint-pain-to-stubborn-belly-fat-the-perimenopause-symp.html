From joint pain to stubborn belly fat: the perimenopause symptoms in South Asian women that are often ignored

Difficulty concentrating, forgetting words during conversations and trouble remembering things are common mental symptoms of perimenopause, but in South Asian women they are often dismissed as stress, a busy lifestyle or simply part of ageing. The BBC report, written by Alia Nazki, describes how the author herself began experiencing symptoms near the age of 40, including a racing heartbeat, joint pain, digestive problems, numb feet and forgetting ordinary words mid-conversation, while her medical tests kept coming back normal. Perimenopause is the phase before menopause, which can last from four to ten years, during which periods continue but hormonal changes bring major physical and mental changes.

Menopause itself is the stage when periods have stopped for twelve months in a row, and it usually arrives between the ages of 50 and 55. Dr Nighat Arif, a specialist attached to Britain's National Health Service, told the BBC that in South Asian women perimenopause can begin eight to ten years earlier than in white women, at around 38, while menopause can arrive at around 45. She said joint pain is the most prominent symptom among the South Asian women who come to her, and it is often mistaken for ageing, stress or a vitamin deficiency.

She explained that oestrogen acts like a lubricating hormone in the body, and when its level falls during perimenopause, the renewal of cells in the joints slows down and the immune system can become overactive, causing mild swelling and pain in the joints and muscles. She advised regular exercise, especially resistance or weight training, along with vitamin D, calcium and omega-3 for bone, joint and muscle health, and said paracetamol or ibuprofen can be used if pain persists, while hormone replacement therapy can be discussed with a doctor for severe symptoms; for brain fog, she said cognitive behavioural therapy can help, pointing to research at University College London showing that properly delivered therapy can improve brain fog and mood-related symptoms by 60 to 70 percent.
