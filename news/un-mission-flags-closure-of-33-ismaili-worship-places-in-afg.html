UN mission flags closure of 33 Ismaili worship places in Afghanistan

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan has flagged growing restrictions on religious freedom in Afghanistan, saying Taliban authorities have closed dozens of Ismaili places of worship in the north-eastern province of Badakhshan and restricted Shia religious practices. According to the mission's latest quarterly human rights report, at least 33 Ismaili jamaat khanas in Badakhshan were closed, including 17 in Zebak district, 14 in Ishkashim and two in Wakhan. The report said worshippers were told to stop using the jamaat khanas, with the authorities citing the presence of mihrabs in the buildings and arguing that the sites should instead be used as mosques.

It also reported that a Shia imambargah in Kabul was seized and turned into a madrasa, with Shia followers barred from entering it. In Parwan and Logar provinces, Shia communities were told to limit their Muharram ceremonies to designated mosques, and the mission confirmed that 45 Shia people were detained in Kabul for hoisting Ashura flags. The United Nations and international human rights organisations have repeatedly expressed concern that such closures and restrictions violate religious freedom, deepen sectarian differences and expose different Muslim communities to discrimination and social exclusion.
