ICJ report sees 'judicial capture' in Pakistan after 26th, 27th Constitutional Amendments

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A report released by the International Commission of Jurists on Wednesday says the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments have led to systemic judicial capture in Pakistan. According to the report, the judiciary no longer enjoys the institutional guarantees of independence that international law requires, including those flowing from Pakistan's obligations under the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. The Commission describes the two amendments as a decisive break from the model of judicial independence that was built after the judiciary's restoration in 2009.

It says the changes altered the balance between the judiciary and the other branches of the state in ways that weaken the courts' ability to act independently. The report calls on the government to begin a comprehensive review of the amendments in light of international law. It also urges that any such review be carried out through transparent public consultation, so that the process itself commands public confidence.

The findings add to the debate in Pakistan over the amendments, which have been defended by the government as reforms and criticised by lawyers and opposition parties as an attack on judicial independence.
