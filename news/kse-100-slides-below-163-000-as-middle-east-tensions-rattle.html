KSE-100 slides below 163,000 as Middle East tensions rattle investors

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Pakistan Stock Exchange extended its losses on Friday as mounting tensions in the Middle East dashed hopes of a swift resolution to the regional conflict. The benchmark KSE-100 index shed 1,549.44 points, or 0.95 per cent, to close at 162,374.78, falling below the 163,000 level in its second consecutive bearish session. Escalating tensions between the United States and Iran pushed crude oil prices higher, raising inflation concerns for Pakistan, which is already facing a sharp surge in LPG prices.

Investors also stayed cautious ahead of the State Bank of Pakistan's monetary policy decision on October 26, with the market expecting a 50-basis-point rate hike. Adding to the unease, the PSX website remained inaccessible throughout the day with a maintenance message, and the exchange issued no explanation. The index swung between an intraday high of 163,987 and a low of 162,292, while around 384 million shares changed hands worth Rs18 billion.

Meezan Bank, Engro Holdings, Lucky Cement, Hub Power and Habib Bank were among the major laggards that dragged the index down. Analysts expect the market to stay volatile within the 160,000 to 168,000 range until the central bank's decision.
