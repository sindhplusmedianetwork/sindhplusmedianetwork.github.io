Big Pharma Says AI Is Cutting Drug Discovery Timelines by Up to Half

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Artificial intelligence has sped up early-stage drug development work at some of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies this year, cutting by as much as half the time it takes to produce new molecules and move them into the clinic, according to their top scientists. GSK, Bristol Myers Squibb, Novartis, Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk, AstraZeneca and Sanofi all say they have moved beyond less glamorous tasks for AI like preparing regulatory filings and are now using the technology for designing molecules, sorting out the most promising ones, and identifying safety risks. Sanofi said it saved at least a year developing asthma treatment lunsekimig using an AI-enabled approach that let the company skip a dose-selection study, while Novartis said AI tools helped it find five targets it prioritized for kidney disease treatments.

Bristol Myers said one of its sickle cell anemia candidates would not have been developed without the company's AI systems, and Novo Nordisk said AI has cut the number of cycles of designing, manufacturing and testing new molecules from around eight to three and slashed the time of each cycle from eight weeks to four. Some scientists say decades of drug discovery experience and data give them an advantage over AI companies like Anthropic, which is gearing up to be a rival, partner or both, with a $1.5 trillion initial public offering this week. The number of AI-enabled drugs in clinical testing has climbed incrementally to 129 drug candidates across 69 companies in March, up from around 117 eight months earlier, with most programs remaining in early-stage development.

Anthropic is working on its own and with partners like Bristol Myers to develop new drugs, launched scientific research-focused AI software called Claude Science, and added Novartis CEO Vas Narasimhan to its board.
