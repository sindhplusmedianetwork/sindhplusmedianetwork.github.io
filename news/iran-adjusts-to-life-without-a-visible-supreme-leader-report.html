Iran adjusts to life without a visible supreme leader, report says

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

More than eight months after an Israeli strike killed much of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei's family, Iran is governed without a visible supreme leader, with a six-man security council filling the vacuum, according to the report. Mojtaba Khamenei, the ayatollah's son, is said to have survived the opening-day strike of the US-Israeli war and formally rules in his father's place, but he has neither been seen nor heard since. Iranian officials and Western intelligence agencies insist he remains alive and underground for fear of assassination.

Sanam Vakil of Chatham House, a London-based think-tank, said what has principally changed is that there is no clear, final arbiter. Decision-making is now dominated by six men from the Supreme National Security Council: President Masoud Pezeshkian, Major General Ahmad Vahidi, commander-in-chief of the IRGC, Mohsen Rezaei, the council's secretary and a former IRGC chief, Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf, speaker of parliament, Ali Abdollahi, chief of the general staff of the armed forces, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. The biggest decisions require the collective's consent, including negotiations on ending or restarting the war with America, nuclear policy, and the appointment of a non-cleric as intelligence minister.

Domestic control remains largely in the hands of Hossein Taeb, an IRGC figure, former intelligence chief and clerical ideologue.
