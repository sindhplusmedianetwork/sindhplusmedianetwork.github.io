Federal intermediate board introduces on-the-spot printing of question papers

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Federal Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education (FBISE) has introduced an on-the-spot printing system for question papers at examination centres, in a major step towards preventing paper leaks that will also reduce transportation and bank storage costs. During the ongoing second annual examinations, the facility has been introduced at 35 examination centres, and the board plans to phase out the manual, bank-based distribution of question papers across all centres within the next year. FBISE Chairman Dr Ikram Ali Malik told Dawn that question papers were shared online just 15 minutes before the start of the examination, and only the superintendent and the head of the school, through their separate passwords, could access them and print the required number of copies.

He said the board would pay the respective schools for the number of question papers printed, adding that the online paper distribution system had already been operating successfully at the board's foreign examination centres since 2018. The board is also planning to introduce a computer-based testing system for the multiple-choice questions portion of examinations, with exams comprising 50 per cent descriptive questions and 50 per cent MCQs. Dr Malik said the aim was to introduce the system in the annual examinations of Class 9 and first-year students in 2027, initially covering MCQs in five subjects — Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Mathematics and Computer Science — with students receiving their results on the same day.

Meanwhile, the Higher Education Commission, through its Education Testing Council, successfully conducted the 35th Law Admission Test, for which 42,547 candidates registered and more than 35,523 appeared at 103 examination centres in 28 cities.
