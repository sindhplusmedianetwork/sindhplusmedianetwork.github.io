Australian Court Rules Mushroom Killer Erin Patterson Must Never Be Freed

Australia's Victoria Court of Appeal has thrown out mushroom killer Erin Patterson's appeal against her murder convictions and replaced her sentence with life in prison without any chance of parole. The 51-year-old was found guilty of murdering three relatives and attempting to kill a fourth after serving them a meal laced with death cap mushrooms at her home in Leongatha, Victoria, in July 2023. Her victims were her former parents-in-law Don and Gail Patterson, along with Gail's sister Heather Wilkinson, while Heather's husband Ian Wilkinson survived after hospital treatment.

She was originally sentenced to life imprisonment with parole possible after 33 years, but the court set aside that non-parole period after prosecutors argued the original punishment was too lenient. Justice Stephen McLeish said the seriousness of her carefully planned crimes meant release could never be an option. Patterson had challenged her convictions on seven grounds, including a hotel booking error that placed jurors in the same accommodation as the prosecution, a key police witness and journalists, as well as the admission of mobile phone location data and Facebook posts criticising her in-laws.

The judge described her conduct as an elaborate series of deceptions involving a false cancer claim, the destruction of evidence and a cruel breach of trust with her own relatives.
