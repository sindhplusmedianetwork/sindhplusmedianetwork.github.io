Critical PHP Flaws Put Millions of Websites at Risk; UK Shuts 32 Government Services as Precaution

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A severe cybersecurity flaw discovered in PHP, one of the most widely used programming languages for building websites and online services, has put millions of websites and computer systems worldwide at risk. Two critical vulnerabilities, detected with the help of artificial intelligence, could allow attackers in certain circumstances to run their own instructions on a web server without authorisation and gain access to sensitive information. A senior official at the UK's National Cyber Security Centre described the flaw as exceptional.

PHP administrators released security updates for the flaws on October 21, but nearly 90 percent of the systems at risk had not yet been patched, and no evidence has yet emerged of systems being breached through the flaw. Cloudflare recorded millions of attempted attacks within hours of the details becoming public, and reports indicate that exploitation attempts had already begun as early as October 17. The British government said 32 public services were temporarily shut and four partially disabled as a precaution, clarifying that the shutdown does not mean they were hacked; some online services in Canada were also affected, and Finland temporarily closed its government web services as a precaution.

Microsoft chief Satya Nadella said he had not seen a situation of this kind in a long time. Cybersecurity agencies have instructed website administrators to install the security updates immediately and step up monitoring of their systems.
