US-backed consortium proposes $4.4bn LNG terminal and pipeline project for Pakistan

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A private consortium of US companies, backed by the Trump administration, is pushing for an investment of up to $4.4 billion in an integrated liquefied natural gas (LNG) project in Pakistan, covering imports, storage, re-gasification, gas transmission and sales to large consumers on a merchant basis. The consortium comprises Global Vision Energy Partners and Khanjee Holdings, along with strategic co-investors and General Mediterranean Holding, a Luxembourg-based US financial conglomerate. The development follows the withdrawal of Energas, a joint venture of Lucky Group and QatarEnergy, which abandoned a similar project citing fundamental changes in Pakistan's energy policy and longstanding unresolved policy and fiscal issues.

The sponsors have secured consent from the Port Qasim Authority for the proposed site and filed an application with the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority for a provisional licence. The project would be developed in two phases: first, an LNG terminal and storage facility with four tanks holding a total of 560,000 cubic metres and annual throughput of about 35 billion cubic feet at a cost of $1.7bn; and second, a 1,100km, 42-48-inch pipeline to transport gas upcountry with a capacity of around 1.2 billion cubic feet per day. Proposed tariffs are $4.93 per mmBtu for the terminal and storage and $1.91 per mmBtu for transmission over the first decade.

The project has been approved for consideration under the US Trade and Development Agency's project preparation programme and may receive partial financing from the US Development Finance Corporation.
