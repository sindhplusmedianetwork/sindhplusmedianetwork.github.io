James Webb Telescope Finally Finds the 'Forbidden' Light Sought for 40 Years

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The James Webb Space Telescope has solved a mystery that has puzzled scientists for four decades. It has detected the faint 'forbidden' light of the triangular hydrogen molecule H3+, a glow first predicted by calculations in 1986 but never observed in a laboratory or in space until now. A team led by David Neufeld of Johns Hopkins University made the find near the newborn star HH 168 in the Cepheus A region, using Webb's MIRI instrument.

The researchers identified 26 spectral lines matching the molecule's signature. The discovery has been accepted for publication in The Astrophysical Journal Letters. It gives astronomers a new tool for studying the gas in the violent outflows that surround young stars, opening a fresh window on how stars form.
