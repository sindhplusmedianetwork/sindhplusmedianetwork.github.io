Sonia Gandhi's memoir tells of love, loss and refusing the prime minister's post in 2004

Former Congress president Sonia Gandhi has released her memoir, 'Belonging: A Journey of Love', a 544-page book published by HarperCollins India and launched on November 10. The book covers her childhood in a small town in Italy after the Second World War, her meeting with Rajiv Gandhi at Cambridge in 1965, their marriage in 1968, and the assassination of Rajiv Gandhi in 1991. It also describes her entry into politics in 1998, when she took over the leadership of the Congress party, and her decision after the 2004 elections not to accept the post of prime minister, nominating economist Manmohan Singh instead.

Sonia Gandhi, who is 79, has said the book is a personal and family story rather than a political one, covering love, duty, separation and her search for belonging in the country she made her home. The book also writes about her mother-in-law, former prime minister Indira Gandhi, and about her conversation with Rajiv Gandhi on the Shah Bano case. The publisher Alfred A.

Knopf had announced in August that the memoir would be published on November 10.
