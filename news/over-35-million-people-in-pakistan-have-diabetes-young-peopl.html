Over 35 Million People in Pakistan Have Diabetes, Young People Increasingly Affected

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Diabetes is rapidly becoming a serious disease in Pakistan, and young people are now also at risk from it, health experts warned at a media session organised by Aga Khan University Hospital in Karachi. Around 34.5 million adults in the country are living with diabetes, while 9.3 million of them remain undiagnosed. Experts warned that if effective measures are not taken, the number of diabetes patients in Pakistan could rise to 70.2 million by 2050.

Consultant endocrinologist Dr Asma Ahmed said type 2 diabetes is no longer limited to older people, and young people are also being affected. She said people with a family history of diabetes, extra fat around the abdomen or little physical activity need special care, and even people who appear to be of healthy weight should not consider themselves safe. She stressed that medical check-ups should not wait for symptoms to appear, and that regular use of medicines, medical examinations and positive lifestyle changes are essential to prevent complications.

Assistant Professor Dr Fareed Ahmed Sheikh said diabetes affects blood flow and the nervous system, and minor wounds on the feet can go unnoticed and later turn into serious infections. Senior dietitian Farah Syed said a balanced diet, vegetables, lentils and whole grains, and avoiding sweet drinks can help keep blood sugar under control. Experts also shared that under Aga Khan University's diabetes prevention research project, blood sugar levels returned to normal within a year in 61 percent of people with pre-diabetes who adopted lifestyle changes.
