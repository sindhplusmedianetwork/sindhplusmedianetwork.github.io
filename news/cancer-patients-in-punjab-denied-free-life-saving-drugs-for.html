Cancer Patients in Punjab Denied Free Life-Saving Drugs for Five Months

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 11, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A programme launched 12 years ago to provide free, life-saving medicines to blood cancer patients in Punjab has fallen into crisis. Thousands of people suffering from Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia have been without essential medication for the past five months. The programme was launched in 2014 during Shehbaz Sharif's tenure as Punjab chief minister, and thousands of patients have benefited from it over the years.

Under the initiative, the Punjab government signed a memorandum of understanding with Swiss pharmaceutical company Novartis Pharma Pakistan to ensure the supply of specialised medicines, including Nilotinib. Medicines were distributed through designated centres at Mayo Hospital and Jinnah Hospital in Lahore, Nishtar Hospital in Multan, Holy Family Hospital in Rawalpindi, Allied Hospital in Faisalabad and a government hospital in Bahawalpur. Last year, the Punjab government decided to centralise the procurement of medicines and started purchases from Oncongen after a tender process.

However, supplies have remained disrupted since the beginning of the current fiscal year, and the supply of the medicine to designated hospitals was discontinued in June and has yet to be restored. As a result, patients have been advised to approach Pakistan Baitul Mal for financial assistance to buy the medicine.
