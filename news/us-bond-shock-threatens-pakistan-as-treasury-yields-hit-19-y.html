US bond shock threatens Pakistan as Treasury yields hit 19-year high

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A shock in the American bond market is reaching Pakistan's doorstep as US 30-year Treasury yields climb to 5.36 percent, a 19-year high. The surge has been driven by heavy borrowing to fund artificial-intelligence infrastructure and by Brent crude pushing above $110 a barrel since the Iran war began, lifting long-term borrowing costs worldwide. For Pakistan, the timing could hardly be worse: the country imports about 90 percent of its energy, which means global fuel prices pass through to local consumers quickly, fueling inflation.

External borrowing will also grow costlier just as large foreign repayments loom on the horizon. The analysis advises Islamabad to protect its foreign-exchange reserves, allow flexibility in the rupee, pass fuel prices through to consumers honestly while shielding the poor through targeted support, and secure longer-dated financing while it still can.
