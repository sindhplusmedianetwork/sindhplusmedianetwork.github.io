Exit Polls Give Opposition Narrow Lead as Israelis Vote in Record Numbers

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Israelis voted on Tuesday in the country's first election since the October 7, 2023 attacks, with turnout reaching 66.7 per cent by 6pm, the highest at that point in the day since records began in 1973. The first exit polls gave the opposition bloc a narrow lead, projecting 52 or 53 seats against 50 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's camp in the 120-seat Knesset, although his Likud party was set to remain the largest single party with 24 or 25 seats. Netanyahu spent the day urging his supporters to vote, warning that Arab leaders wanted to steal the election, while his party unsuccessfully asked election authorities to keep polling stations open past 10pm.

His main rival is Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief and political novice, who called on voters to replace what he described as the worst government since the state was established. Turnout was unusually high among Israeli Arabs and in east Jerusalem, and United Arab List leader Mansour Abbas could emerge as a kingmaker in a close result. Police also arrested a man with a knife who moved threateningly towards opposition leader Yair Golan in Tel Aviv.

Netanyahu, who has served as prime minister on and off for more than 19 years, campaigned on his security record and his endorsement by US President Donald Trump.
