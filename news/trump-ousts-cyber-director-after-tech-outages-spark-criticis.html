Trump ousts cyber director after tech outages spark criticism: sources

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

White House national cyber director Sean Cairncross was fired from his job, a person close to the White House said on Thursday, days after technology outages disrupted US air travel, banking and national security systems. The ouster comes as US President Donald Trump and his administration face mounting scrutiny over their response to the series of disruptions over the past week, including a global outage affecting hundreds of millions of Microsoft users and a nationwide halt to air travel that disrupted more than 18,000 flights. The dismissal of Cairncross is the administration's first major personnel move since the outages, and Cairncross oversaw cybersecurity policy and strategy across the federal government, with Department of Defence Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies to assume the position temporarily, according to the source.

The person, who spoke on condition of anonymity, said the president fully supported firing Sean Cairncross from his position, while Cairncross said in a statement on Thursday night it was the honor of a lifetime to serve in Trump's administration. Cybersecurity experts told Reuters the recent technology disruptions are likely linked to corporate efforts to rapidly patch newly discovered software vulnerabilities in the AI age, while the top US intelligence official said on Wednesday the disruptions were separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes and not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack. Republican Senator Josh Hawley of Missouri told reporters in Washington on Tuesday that the Trump administration should brief lawmakers on the outages, and Cairncross's successor will need to be confirmed by the US Senate, which will be controlled by Democrats beginning in January.
