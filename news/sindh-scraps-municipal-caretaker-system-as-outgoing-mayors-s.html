Sindh Scraps Municipal Caretaker System as Outgoing Mayors Stay On

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Sindh government has formally done away with caretaker or independent municipal set-ups after notifying changes to the Sindh Local Government Act. Under the amended law, outgoing mayors and chairmen will continue to exercise the powers of the council as administrators until their newly elected successors assume office. The change follows the provincial assembly's passage of the Sindh Local Government (Amendment) Bill 2026, which was assented to by the governor and published in the gazette on October 9.

On Wednesday, the government formally informed stakeholders across the province, including mayors, municipal commissioners, town chairmen and district and union council officials. The amended law requires the government to send a request to the Election Commission 120 days before a council's term expires, so that fresh elections are held within 120 days. It also allows deputy mayors and vice chairmen to act as conveners of their councils, requires local bodies to set up three-member reconciliation forums within 90 days, and makes annual performance inspections and quarterly meetings mandatory.

The law further places new responsibilities on local councils for women's empowerment, the welfare of persons with disabilities, solid waste monitoring and reporting illegal constructions to the Sindh Building Control Authority.
