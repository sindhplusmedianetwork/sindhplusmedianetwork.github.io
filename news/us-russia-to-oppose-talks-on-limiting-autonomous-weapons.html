US, Russia to oppose talks on limiting autonomous weapons

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Major arms producers the United States and Russia look set to oppose a push at a global meeting in Geneva this week to launch negotiations on legally binding rules for autonomous weapons, diplomats and rights groups said. The UN-hosted meeting, which began on Monday, is due to decide whether to start talks for a legally binding pact on prohibitions and regulations for Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems, often called killer robots. The rise of artificial intelligence has boosted the development of drones, robots, tanks, submarines and other military hardware capable of killing without human involvement, and semi-autonomous, AI-assisted weapons have already been used in Ukraine, Sudan and the Middle East.

While most of the 128 states party to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons support negotiations, major military nations including the US, Russia and Israel oppose the move, arguing that existing humanitarian law is sufficient. Russia stymied the start of talks on Monday with its objection to a procedural rule allowing observers, including the EU and non-governmental organisations, to take the floor, a move most delegates opposed. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres urged states in a video message to agree to move towards negotiating a legally binding instrument with clear prohibitions and restrictions, and the International Committee of the Red Cross has also warned of the dangers.

The United States favours guidelines over binding rules and has proposed a five-year mandate for talks towards a non-binding agreement, while diplomats said a compromise may be found by the end of the week, with all options on the table.
