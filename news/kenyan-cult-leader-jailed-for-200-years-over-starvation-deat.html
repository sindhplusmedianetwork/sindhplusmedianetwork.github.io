Kenyan cult leader jailed for 200 years over starvation deaths of 190 children

A Kenyan court sentenced cult leader Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison on Friday for the murders of 190 children who were starved to death on his orders and buried in a forest. Justice Diana Kavedza, reading the judgement at the Mombasa High Court, said the sentence comprised 100 years for murder by starvation and 100 years for direct homicide, in one of the deadliest cult-related mass killings of modern times. Prosecutors said Mackenzie, who led an apocalyptic Christian church in a forest near Kenya's Indian Ocean coast, had told his followers to starve themselves to death, along with their children, to reach heaven before the end of the world.

His accomplices threatened and beat victims to ensure they followed his orders, and the emaciated bodies were found scattered in shallow graves and dumped in latrines, with some victims buried alive. Twenty-eight accomplices received sentences ranging from 10 to 160 years, and defence lawyer Lawrence Obonyo said he would appeal the ruling. The case shocked Kenya in April 2023, when police began uncovering bodies in Shakahola Forest near the coastal town of Malindi, and the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights says 448 bodies were ultimately found in the forest.

Mackenzie is also accused of ordering the deaths of 52 people found at a separate location about 30 kilometres away, bringing the suspected total toll to 500, and he has pleaded not guilty in that case.
