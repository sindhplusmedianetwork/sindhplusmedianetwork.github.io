Clifton residents protest high-rise boom and crumbling civic services

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 09, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Residents of Karachi's Clifton neighbourhood took to the streets to protest against alleged illegal high-rise construction, crumbling civic infrastructure and threats to the Nehr-e-Khayyam waterway. The protest, held outside Cross Court near Do Talwar, saw residents question how 40- to 46-storey buildings were being allowed to rise in an area already grappling with inadequate water supply, sewerage, drainage and parking. Protesters said Clifton, despite being one of Karachi's posh residential areas, lacked an effective sewerage system, while roads were in disrepair and garbage collection remained woefully inadequate.

They pointed out that even moderate rainfall left parts of the neighbourhood submerged, while sewage water stagnated in some streets as new high-rises kept coming up. Round-the-clock construction, the commercialisation of residential streets and the spread of padel courts were adding to the pressure on already strained infrastructure, they warned. The protesters also described Nehr-e-Khayyam as vital for rainwater drainage and the area's environmental balance, and demanded an independent technical assessment of work around the waterway and protection of its boundaries from encroachment.

They demanded accountability from the authorities issuing construction approvals and an immediate halt to illegal building, saying the poor state of civic services in an area hosting diplomatic missions raised serious questions about the performance of the relevant authorities.
