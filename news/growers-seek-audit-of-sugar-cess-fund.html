Growers seek audit of sugar cess fund

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Sugarcane growers in Rahim Yar Khan district have demanded an audit of the sugar cess fund, questioning why rural link roads remain in poor condition despite billions of rupees being collected every year. Sugarcane has been sown over 673,000 acres in the district this year, where eight sugar mills operate, and the crop area has grown alongside the number of mills. Deputy Director Agriculture Dr Masood Saleem confirmed the cultivation figure, while farmers expressed concern that the government has not announced the per-maund price of sugarcane as the crushing season approaches.

Kissan Ittehad central president Mazharul Haq Bukhari said the government had not fixed the price last year either. He explained that the government collects Rs2.50 per maund from growers and an equal amount from sugar mills, and based on an average yield of 800 maunds per acre, the annual cess from growers alone comes to around Rs1.35 billion, rising to about Rs2.69 billion with the mills' share. Farmer leader Abdul Samad Chaudhry asked why rural roads had remained damaged for nearly two decades despite the collections, and grower Zahid Sharif Mahar said roads damaged by heavily loaded sugarcane trailers were not being repaired.

The growers demanded that the government disclose how the cess money is being spent, and Bukhari added that farmers' organisations are not consulted on the utilisation of the fund or the fixing of sugarcane prices.
