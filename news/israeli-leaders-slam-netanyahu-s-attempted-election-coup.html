Israeli leaders slam Netanyahu's 'attempted election coup'

Israeli opposition leaders denounced on Monday what they called an attempted coup following reports that outgoing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office tried to frame members of an Arab-Israeli party ahead of recent elections. Channel 12 reported on Sunday that on the eve of the October 27 election, Netanyahu's chief of staff, Ido Norden, phoned in a complaint to police about members of the Arab-Israeli Raam party, directing police to a purported intelligence source, a former employee of Netanyahu's office, whose information led to the arrest of three Raam members. The three men were later released after police determined the allegations were unfounded, and Netanyahu subsequently accused Arab parties of electoral fraud after they won a record number of seats.

The newspaper Haaretz also reported that David Zini, head of the Shin Bet domestic security service, sought to wiretap leaders of the Joint List, the other Arab grouping, but the attorney general denied the request for lack of evidence. Yair Lapid, who headed the opposition in the previous legislature, wrote on X that as more details came to light it became clear that this was an attempted coup, while Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen called it an attempted coup by Netanyahu and Raam's number two, Yoav Segalovitz, demanded an urgent criminal investigation. Netanyahu's Likud party called the reports utterly absurd and claimed they were aimed at blocking a national unity government, while his main rival, former military chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, demanded an investigation and said the caretaker prime minister was a threat to Israeli democracy.

Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Eisenkot's opposition secured the 61-seat majority needed to govern after last month's vote, and both Raam and the Joint List have indicated they support Eisenkot.
