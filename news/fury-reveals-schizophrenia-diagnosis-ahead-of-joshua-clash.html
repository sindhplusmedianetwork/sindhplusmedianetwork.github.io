Fury reveals schizophrenia diagnosis ahead of Joshua clash

By Sindh Plus Media Network

Former world champion Tyson Fury has said he was diagnosed with schizophrenia soon after his world title win over Wladimir Klitschko in 2015. "Words can't explain the darkness I was in," the heavyweight boxer said. "It was like a living version of the pits of hell." Fury said he feared he would end up in a padded room with the door locked, and that crippling anxiety led him to see a psychiatrist in London.

He made the revelations in a documentary which will be released by Netflix on Friday. The 38-year-old is preparing for the long-awaited all-British heavyweight fight against Anthony Joshua, a fellow former world champion, in Cardiff on December 11, a bout that will be broadcast live on Netflix. Fury recalled hearing the radio talking to him and hearing voices in his mind, and said that after his win over Klitschko he gained weight and struggled with alcohol and drug addiction.

He said a turning point came on a night out for Halloween in 2017, when he decided to change his life, and he channelled that resolve into a comeback that led him to return to training, focus more on his family and claim another world title when he beat Deontay Wilder in 2020.
