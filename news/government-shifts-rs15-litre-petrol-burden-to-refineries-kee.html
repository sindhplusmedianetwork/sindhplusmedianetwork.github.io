Government Shifts Rs15/Litre Petrol Burden to Refineries, Keeps Prices Unchanged

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The federal government announced on Monday that local oil refineries would shoulder an additional financial burden of approximately Rs15 billion every month to shield consumers from rising global fuel prices. Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik told a joint press conference in Islamabad, alongside Information Minister Attaullah Tarar, that petrol was due to become Rs15 per litre costlier on Friday, but on the prime minister's directive the increase was not passed on to the public. Malik said refineries had raised their throughput over the past three months and had agreed to absorb the Rs15-a-litre gap so fuel could be sold below the price dictated by international markets.

Tarar said the government had also launched a fuel subsidy scheme for motorcycles, Ching Chi rickshaws and small vehicles, under which more than 15 million people had registered and Rs27 billion in relief had been disbursed so far. He added that tensions around the Bab-el-Mandeb strait and the Strait of Hormuz had raised insurance and logistics costs, but Pakistan's macroeconomic indicators had held up. Malik assured the public that the government would step in and protect citizens if fuel prices came under further pressure.
