Israel election commission rejects Netanyahu's fraud claims

By Sindh Plus Media Network

Israeli electoral authorities on Wednesday rejected allegations promoted by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of fraud by Arab parties, who could hold the key to removing him from power after a divisive vote. The Central Elections Committee said it had conducted an audit of 9,069 polling stations following complaints and was confident that the announced results 'accurately reflect the will of the voters'. It said all complaints submitted by political factions had been reviewed and there were no findings indicating harm to the integrity of the election.

Netanyahu had repeatedly alleged fraud by Arab-majority parties, pointing to the arrests of several people found with envelopes of cash on election day and later claiming lawmakers received money from Qatar. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said she would not question anyone without evidence, after reports that the head of the Shin Bet security service, a Netanyahu ally, had asked to interrogate members of Arab parties. President Isaac Herzog will officially receive the results of the October 27 election as he decides whether to give Netanyahu or his rival Gadi Eisenkot the first chance to form a government, with neither commanding enough votes alone.

Netanyahu faces corruption charges and the risk of jail if he loses power and, with it, immunity.
