Legal community calls for repeal of 26th and 27th Amendments after Bilawal's 'brute majority' remarks

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

One hundred members of the legal fraternity and civil society have called for the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments to be repealed, in an open letter issued on Tuesday. The letter was a reaction to remarks by PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari, whose party had helped make the enactment of both amendments possible, that the 27th Amendment was passed neither through political consensus nor mutual agreement but with a brute majority. The 26th and 27th Amendments were enacted in October 2024 and November 2025 respectively, and were passed by both houses of Parliament amid outcry from the PTI-led opposition.

Bilawal later clarified that he was not disowning the 27th Amendment, but noting that it did not enjoy the same unanimous consensus as the 18th Amendment and the 1973 Constitution. The letter, also signed by retired Supreme Court Justice Mansoor Ali Shah, said the constitutional order belongs to the people and cannot be seized by a majority that its own members admit was never theirs to wield. It demanded that the constitutional order that existed before the 26th Amendment be restored and that any future reform of the judiciary come through an open and consultative process.

The letter also called for reserved seats in the legislative houses to be returned to their lawful claimants, noting that the PTI was deprived of its reserved seats in the 2024 elections after its electoral symbol was revoked, and that a Supreme Court bench had declared the party eligible for the seats before the order was overturned by the constitutional bench created through the 26th Amendment.
