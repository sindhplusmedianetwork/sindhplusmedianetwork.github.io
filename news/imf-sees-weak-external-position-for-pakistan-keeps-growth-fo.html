IMF Sees Weak External Position for Pakistan, Keeps Growth Forecast at 3.5%

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The International Monetary Fund said on Thursday that Pakistan's external financial position may weaken due to rising imports and moderating remittances, urging exchange rate flexibility to preserve foreign exchange reserves and tighter monetary policy to deal with rising inflation. The global lender made the observations in its Middle East and Central Asia region report, released a week after the World Bank-IMF annual meetings. It said that over the medium term, external positions should strengthen in Egypt, Jordan and Morocco but weaken in Pakistan as imports rise and remittances moderate.

The remarks came days after the IMF reached a staff-level agreement for disbursement of the $1 billion fifth loan tranche, subject to the government fulfilling prior actions. The government has projected imports reaching $70 billion this fiscal year and remittances growing to $45.5 billion, while exports have struggled to grow. The IMF projected Pakistan's current account deficit at 1% of GDP, or around $4.5 billion, far higher than official estimates of $2.5 billion, and sees gross financing needs at 22-25% of GDP, well above peer countries.

The report also pointed to a deep nexus between the government and commercial banks, estimating banks' 63% exposure to public debt. On inflation, the IMF noted pressures rose to 11.1% year-on-year in August and projected inflation rising to 9.9%, urging countries including Pakistan to stand ready to tighten monetary policy. The IMF kept its economic growth forecast for Pakistan unchanged at 3.5%, lower than the official 4% target, after Pakistani authorities had tried to convince the Fund the higher target would be achieved.
