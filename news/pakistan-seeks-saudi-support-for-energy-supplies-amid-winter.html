Pakistan Seeks Saudi Support for Energy Supplies Amid Winter LPG Crisis

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan has sought Saudi Arabia's support for liquefied petroleum gas supplies and invited investment in oil storage facilities and refinery upgrades as it grapples with supply disruptions linked to the Iran-US conflict and instability along key regional shipping routes. Pakistan has relied heavily on LPG imports from Iran through the land border, and the closure of the crossing has raised concerns about supplies ahead of winter, when domestic gas demand typically increases. Islamabad has approached Riyadh for dedicated LPG shipments, as disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz and security risks in the Red Sea have added to concerns over regional energy supplies.

The proposals were discussed at a meeting between Federal Minister for Petroleum Ali Pervez Malik and Saudi Arabia's Ambassador to Pakistan Dr Rajeh Tami Albugamy. Malik told the ambassador that Pakistan had approved a tax-neutral bonded policy under which Saudi companies could establish commercial oil reserves in Pakistan without local tax liabilities. The minister also outlined plans to develop a large petroleum storage and refining complex at Hub in Balochistan and thanked Saudi authorities at Yanbu port for prioritising crude oil shipments to Pakistan.

The Saudi ambassador announced that the Saudi embassy in Islamabad had introduced same-day processing of visa applications for Pakistani citizens.
