Capacity charges drain billions as Pakistan's solar exodus leaves grid to pay

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's power sector is bleeding billions through capacity charges, with an estimated $40 billion paid since 2019. The payments flow from take-or-pay agreements signed when new generation capacity was added far faster than electricity consumption grew. The 2015 net-metering rules then triggered a mass migration of consumers to rooftop solar, and the country now hosts more than 10,000 megawatts of off-grid solar capacity, equivalent to about 42 percent of average summer grid demand.

As large numbers of consumers leave the grid, the shrinking pool of remaining grid users is forced to absorb the capacity-charge burden. The analysis urges the government to handle the solar transition with care and to renegotiate the power purchase agreements so their terms can be rebalanced more fairly.
