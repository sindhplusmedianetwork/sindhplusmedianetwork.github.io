Experts urge early diabetes screening and healthy habits

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Health experts have stressed the need for early diabetes screening, balanced nutrition, regular physical activity and timely medical treatment to prevent serious complications, as more than 35 million people in Pakistan are living with the disease, while millions remain undiagnosed. Speaking in connection with World Diabetes Day, Principal Ameer-ud-Din Medical College (AMC) Prof. Dr.

Jodat Saleem on Friday said diabetes was increasing at an alarming rate globally and in Pakistan, citing global estimates of nearly 580 million patients. He identified obesity, unhealthy dietary habits, physical inactivity and genetic predisposition as major risk factors, and urged people to adopt balanced diets, make daily walking or exercise part of their routine and undergo regular blood glucose screening, adding that early detection through a simple blood test could help prevent serious complications. Medical Superintendent Lahore General Hospital (LGH) Prof.

Dr. Faryad Hussain said uncontrolled diabetes could lead to serious health complications, including kidney failure, cardiovascular diseases, nerve damage and foot ulcers, and advised patients to strictly follow prescribed medication and insulin schedules and take special care of their feet. Head of Ophthalmology Prof.

Dr. Tayyaba Gul Malik warned that persistently high blood sugar levels could damage retinal blood vessels, leading to diabetic retinopathy, blurred vision, progressive visual impairment and even permanent blindness, and recommended that diabetic patients undergo comprehensive eye examinations at least once a year. Assistant Professor of Medicine Dr.

Muhammad Maqsood expressed concern over the increasing incidence of childhood diabetes, particularly Type 1 diabetes, and urged parents to seek immediate medical attention if children showed symptoms such as excessive thirst, frequent urination, sudden weight loss, increased hunger and persistent fatigue. Consultant Gynaecologist Dr. Laila Shafiq highlighted the importance of timely screening and proper management of gestational diabetes, warning that uncontrolled blood sugar during pregnancy could endanger both the mother and the baby, and stressed regular testing and continuous medical follow-up.

The experts called for sustained public awareness, early diagnosis, healthy dietary habits and regular exercise to become part of everyday life, emphasising that preventive measures combined with proper medical treatment could significantly reduce diabetes-related complications and disabilities.
