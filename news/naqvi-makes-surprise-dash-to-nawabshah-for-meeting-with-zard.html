Naqvi makes surprise dash to Nawabshah for meeting with Zardari

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi made an unscheduled visit to Nawabshah on Thursday night and held a nearly hour-long meeting with President Asif Ali Zardari at Zardari House, amid speculation over discussions on key political developments. According to sources, the meeting focused on proposals for the creation of new provinces, the possibility of holding a referendum on the issue and potential action against the Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F). Sources claimed Naqvi had travelled urgently carrying an important message from a senior national figure and sought to take the president into confidence over the matters discussed, though no official statement has been issued about the meeting.

Naqvi arrived at Nawabshah airport from Islamabad aboard a special aircraft, where he was received by Sindh Home Minister Zia Ul Hassan Lanjar, Minister for Works and Services and Prisons Haji Ali Hassan Zardari, Deputy Commissioner Abdul Samad Nizamani and SSP Dr Sameer Noor Channa, and was escorted to Zardari House under tight security before departing for Islamabad after about 59 minutes. The visit came hours after Naqvi said he and PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari were largely on the same page regarding a reset of the system, after Bilawal had said on Wednesday that the system needed resetting but the country should be run by the people rather than traders and businessmen. Addressing a press conference at the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency earlier on Thursday, Naqvi said Bilawal had spoken about resetting the 'political system', whereas his own proposal concerned an 'overall reset', and maintained that no decision would be taken without the consent of the parties concerned.

He also urged political parties to seek public opinion through a referendum on creating new provinces, a debate that gained momentum on July 31 when he called for additional administrative units, though the Sindh Assembly passed a resolution on September 4 rejecting attempts to divide the province and legal experts have pointed out that a referendum alone cannot create a new province.
