Newborn Death Rate at SICHN Drops to Lowest Level in Four Years

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The neonatal mortality rate at the medical centres of the Sindh Institute of Child Health and Neonatology (SICHN) has dropped to its lowest level in four years, following significant improvements in neonatal intensive care facilities, medical equipment and staff training. According to a survey conducted at the Mother and Child Health Centre established at Jinnah Postgraduate Medical Centre (JPMC), the neonatal mortality rate has declined to 15 deaths per 1,000 live births. The institute aims to further reduce the rate to 12 per 1,000 by next year, potentially achieving the United Nations' global target ahead of the 2030 deadline.

This was stated by Dr Syed Rehan Ali, Head of the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) at SICHN Korangi, while speaking at a ceremony held to mark World Prematurity Day. Dr Rehan Ali said that approximately 37 out of every 1,000 live-born babies in Pakistan die within the first 28 days of life, but SICHN has achieved a significant reduction over the past four years, with the rate falling from 37 per 1,000 when the institute began working in this specialised field to 15 per 1,000 now. He added that inadequate maternal and child healthcare facilities, particularly in underdeveloped areas of Pakistan, remain a major obstacle to achieving the target.

According to Dr Rehan Ali, more than 10 million babies are born prematurely worldwide every year, and timely treatment, trained healthcare professionals and well-equipped neonatal intensive care units are essential to saving their lives. He urged parents not to lose hope if their babies were born prematurely or with low birth weight, stressing that modern medical facilities and timely intervention could save even extremely low-birth-weight infants.
