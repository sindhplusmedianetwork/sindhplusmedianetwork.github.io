Nuclear science strengthening Pakistan's groundwater tracking, says IAEA

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 01, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan has significantly strengthened its ability to track groundwater resources through nuclear science, according to an International Atomic Energy Agency report released on Saturday. The agency said isotope-based techniques under its Regional Cooperative Agreement programme for Asia and the Pacific have enhanced the country's capacity for continuous groundwater monitoring. Scientists can now identify recharge sources, determine groundwater age and trace contamination pathways, linking research more closely with water governance.

Studies under the programme have produced reliable estimates of groundwater recharge rates, aquifer interconnections and pollution pathways in key regions including the Indus Plain, the Quetta Valley and the Cholistan Desert. The Pakistan Institute of Nuclear Science and Technology now hosts one of the region's most advanced isotope analysis facilities, supported by 12 laboratories nationwide. Pinstech and the Pakistan Council of Research in Water Resources are working together to feed isotopic data into national groundwater management models.

The report said this integration supports early detection of over-extraction, salinity intrusion and other aquifer stress, contributing to more sustainable groundwater use. It noted a shift from reactive to preventive, science-informed groundwater management, with policymakers increasingly relying on isotope data for regulation and long-term planning.
