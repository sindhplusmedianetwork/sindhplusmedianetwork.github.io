How Indian gangsters are running crime networks from behind prison bars

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Despite being behind bars, several of India's most dangerous gangsters continue to control their criminal networks from inside prison, according to an analytical report published in Indian media. The question resurfaced after a recent firing incident at actor Tiger Shroff's house, in which the Bishnoi group is alleged to have played a role. The report says Indian prisons have become not only safe havens for some gangs but also hubs for recruiting members and directing criminal operations.

Lawrence Bishnoi is lodged in Gujarat's Sabarmati Jail, while his brother Anmol Bishnoi is in Delhi's Tihar Jail; both face accusations of planning and ordering multiple shooting incidents in Mumbai over the past three years. Restrictions imposed by India's Home Ministry have made it difficult for investigating agencies to gain custody of the two. The report notes this is not a new phenomenon: Mumbai underworld figures like Arun Gawli and Ashwin Naik also faced charges of running their groups from jail, collecting extortion money and ordering killings.

Naik was accused of ordering the murder of his wife Neeta over suspected links with a police officer, but police could not prove the charge in court and he was acquitted. Gawli was accused of plotting the murder of Jayant Jadhav, a close aide of Shiv Sena's leadership, who had allegedly helped move him between jails. A special article on NDTV identified corruption as a key enabler, saying gangs maintain contacts with jail staff and obtain special facilities and outside contact through bribes and gifts.

Messages are also passed through lawyers, legal visits, and prisoners released or taken out for court hearings. Smuggled mobile phones have made these communications even easier. In March 2023, Lawrence Bishnoi gave two interviews to a Hindi TV channel while in custody, and in mid-2024 a video surfaced of him making a video call from Sabarmati Jail.

A fellow inmate reportedly told police that Bishnoi had a phone and was in contact with the outside world. Maharashtra jail officer Swati Sathe raided several prisons and seized phones from inmates. In the Tiger Shroff firing case, Mumbai police are now trying to take Anmol Bishnoi into custody from Tihar Jail, and have written to the Home Ministry for permission, which has not yet been granted.
