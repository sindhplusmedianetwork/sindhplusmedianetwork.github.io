291 teachers of Afghan refugee children protest 'abrupt' UNHCR layoffs in Peshawar

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 04, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Hundreds of Pakistani teachers who spent decades educating Afghan children in refugee camps protested in Peshawar on Tuesday against their 'abrupt' dismissal by the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees. As many as 291 teachers, who had been providing education to Afghan children under UNHCR auspices for the past 35 to 40 years, were laid off without prior notice or any financial relief. The affected teachers, including 100 men and 191 women from Peshawar, Mardan, Timergara, Nowshera, Swabi, Haripur, Kohat, Dera Ismail Khan, Bannu, Lakki Marwat, Hangu, Tall, Chakdara and Jalala Shergarh, were reportedly dismissed on October 31.

The protest was led by Tufail Ahmed and Farhana Durrani, who heads the women teachers. Ahmed said they had devoted nearly four decades to educating Afghan children but were dismissed through a single administrative decision, having already been deprived of medical allowances, financial benefits and gratuity. Durrani said that after 35 years of dedicated service, the UNHCR sent them home 'through a single phone call'.

The protesters complained that UNHCR officials refused to hear their grievances, closed the office doors to them and deployed police outside with warnings of a possible baton charge. They appealed to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi to intervene and secure relief for the affected families.
