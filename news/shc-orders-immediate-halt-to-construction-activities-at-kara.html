SHC Orders Immediate Halt to Construction Activities at Karachi's Nehr-i-Khayyam

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Sindh High Court on Thursday directed the authorities concerned to immediately stop the ongoing construction and development activities at Nehr-i-Khayyam in Clifton, Karachi. A two-judge constitutional bench comprising Justice Riazat Ali Sahar and Justice Muhammad Hasan Akber also asked its office to communicate the order to the Sindh chief secretary, the Local Government Department secretary and the director general of the Karachi Development Authority for compliance. Residents of the area, along with local leaders of a political party, petitioned the court challenging construction and development in and around Nehr-i-Khayyam and raising serious concerns about preservation of the natural waterway.

The bench noted these were pro bono petitions by citizens of Karachi bringing to the court's attention alleged illegal construction over Nehr-i-Khayyam, which served as a storm-water drainage channel, and stated that according to the petitioners' lawyers the Supreme Court had already considered the issue in a detailed judgement reported as 2020 SCMR 513, but despite specific Supreme Court directions illegal construction was still being carried out over the waterway. The bench ordered the secretary of the local government department and the KDA to immediately stop such illegal construction till the next date of hearing, and adjourned the hearing till January 18, 2027. Initially, Ava Ardeshir Cowasjee approached the court, submitting that as a resident of Bath Island she was directly affected, and argued that the waterway and the abutting roads were required for maintenance and servicing of Nehr-i-Khayyam.

The other petition was filed by City Council Opposition Leader Advocate Saifuddin, along with deputy parliamentary leader Taimoor Ahmed and two residents, who asserted they were aggrieved by construction in and around the waterway, including adjacent amenity plots. They pleaded to declare illegal the alleged conversion of the storm-water drain into a recreational park or commercial venture, sought directives to halt all construction and remove structures already erected within the lawful boundaries of Nehr-i-Khayyam, and asked the court to preserve it for its lawful purpose as a storm-water drainage channel.
