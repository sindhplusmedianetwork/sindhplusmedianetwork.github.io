UNDP chief: act on El Niño now or pay twice as much later

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The head of the United Nations Development Programme warned on Friday that acting now against the devastating effects of El Niño would cost $36 billion, but delay would push the bill to $73 billion. Speaking in an interview at the COP31 climate talks in the Turkish resort of Antalya, UNDP chief Alexander De Croo said the cost of inaction would be twice as high. El Niño heats Pacific Ocean waters every two to seven years, disrupting global wind patterns and rainfall, and this exceptionally strong cycle has already battered economies worldwide.

De Croo called inaction the most expensive climate policy available and urged major players such as the International Monetary Fund to help vulnerable developing nations limit the damage. He said those countries had been given no time to recover from a series of compounding crises, including the Covid-19 pandemic, the war in Ukraine, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and now El Niño and rising financing costs. A UN report this week cited African Development Bank estimates of $10 billion to $20 billion in regional damage, while the UN Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean projected cumulative GDP losses of at least 2 per cent over three years, pushing 4.8 million people into poverty.

Separately, sources said China has decided against hosting the 2028 COP33 climate summit, leaving the door open for another Asia-Pacific nation, after India withdrew its offer in April and South Korea expressed interest.
