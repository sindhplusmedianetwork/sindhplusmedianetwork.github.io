Supercomputer simulation reveals how the universe's dark ages ended

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Scientists from MIT, Harvard and other institutions have built a supercomputer simulation called Lumina tracing the first two billion years of the universe. The simulation covers a region about 1.6 billion light-years across and uses the Frontier supercomputer at Oak Ridge. It shows how early stars, galaxies and black holes ionised hydrogen and helium and moved the universe from darkness to light.

The research explains how the universe emerged from its dark ages into the bright cosmos seen today. The findings have been published in the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society.
