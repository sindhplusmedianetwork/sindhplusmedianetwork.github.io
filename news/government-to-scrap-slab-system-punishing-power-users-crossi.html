Government to Scrap Slab System Punishing Power Users Crossing 200 Units

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Power Minister Awais Leghari has told the Senate that the government has decided to scrap the electricity slab system that punishes consumers for crossing the 200-unit threshold. Under the current system, consumers using up to 200 units a month fall in the protected category and receive a subsidy of around Rs13 per unit, but crossing the limit by even one unit means paying up to Rs33 per unit and losing the facility for six months. The minister was responding to a calling-attention notice by PML-N Senator Abid Sher Ali, who said the tariff jumps from Rs15 to Rs30 per unit when consumption rises from 200 to 201 units, forcing a consumer to pay bills under the 400-unit slab for just one extra unit.

Leghari said work is being speeded up to abolish the system, with two options under consideration: raising the threshold from 200 to 250 units, or abolishing the six-month penalty so the protected benefit can be availed in any month when consumption is restricted to 200 units. He said it will take one and a half months to finalise the plan, and the new system will provide relief on the basis of income rather than consumption. The number of protected consumers has risen from 10 million to 20-25 million over the last two years because of solar energy, with many consumers using 700 to 800 units limiting their grid consumption to 200 units to avail relief meant for lower-income households.

The minister said the 65 per cent subsidy for protected consumers is being availed by rich and poor alike, and the new mechanism will restrict it to those below the poverty line and lower-middle income groups.
