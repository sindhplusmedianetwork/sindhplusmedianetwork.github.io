Daily Omega-3 Dose May Lift Five-Year Survival in Advanced Colon Cancer, Study Finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A new research trial has found that a higher daily dose of an omega-3 supplement may improve long-term survival among some patients with advanced colon cancer. The findings, presented at the European Society for Medical Oncology meeting in Madrid, focused on eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), a type of omega-3 fatty acid. The study involved 418 patients in the UK who were scheduled for surgery to remove liver tumours caused by colon cancer that had spread.

Participants were randomly assigned to receive either 4 grams of EPA or a mineral oil placebo every day. Around 62% of those taking EPA survived for five years, compared with 45% of those given the placebo, though the researchers found no significant improvement in progression-free survival, which stood at a median of 13.5 months for EPA patients against 12.9 months in the placebo group. Lead researcher Professor Mark Hull of the University of Leeds said the survival benefit could be clinically meaningful if confirmed in further studies, suggesting EPA may influence how the body responds to cancer, particularly in patients suffering weight loss, fatigue and reduced appetite.

However, experts urged caution, with Dr Chiara Cremolini of the University of Pisa saying more confirmatory research is needed before EPA could become part of routine cancer treatment, and the findings remain preliminary until published in a peer-reviewed journal.
