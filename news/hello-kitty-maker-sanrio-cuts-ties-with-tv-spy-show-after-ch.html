Hello Kitty maker Sanrio cuts ties with TV spy show after China backlash

By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 2 min read

Hello Kitty maker Sanrio has cancelled a collaboration with a Japanese TV spy show after a backlash in China, the second incident in days to highlight the tightrope foreign firms and celebrities must walk in the lucrative market. The Wednesday announcement by Sanrio's Chinese subsidiary came just after French soccer star Kylian Mbappé apologised for remarks about late Chinese leader Mao Zedong that saw several Chinese sponsors cut ties with him. The hit show VIVANT, produced by Japanese broadcaster TBS and also available on Netflix, centres on Japanese spies and a terrorist organisation called Tent; its latest season, currently being aired, depicts a fictional Chinese intelligence agency capturing and torturing some of the Japanese spies.

Chinese anger over Sanrio's range of soft toys, including Hello Kitty, dressed as spies from the show has simmered for days on social media and came to a head this week when Chinese state media ramped up the condemnation. 'We deeply apologise for the concern this has caused everyone,' Sanrio's Chinese subsidiary said in a statement, adding that it believed the collaboration contained expressions inconsistent with the corporate philosophy of the Sanrio Group and that it had therefore decided to terminate the collaboration along with all subsequent projects and promotional activities. TBS President Masamine Ryuho said at a regular press conference on Wednesday that the drama was 'entirely fictional and has no intention of disparaging any specific country,' local media reported.

Relations between China and Japan have plunged since Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi late last year said that a hypothetical Chinese attack on democratically ruled Taiwan could trigger a military response from Tokyo. Sanrio booked sales of 9.8 billion yen ($62 million) in China in the first quarter, up almost 25% on the same period a year earlier, underscoring why the market matters to Japanese firms.
