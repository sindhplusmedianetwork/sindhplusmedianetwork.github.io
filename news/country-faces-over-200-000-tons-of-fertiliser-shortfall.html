Country faces over 200,000 tons of fertiliser shortfall

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 01, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The government has acknowledged a fertiliser shortfall of more than 200,000 tonnes against the country's demand, blaming the Middle East conflict and gas shortages. The disclosure came during Question Hour in the National Assembly, where it was stated that disrupted gas supply from the Middle East situation had temporarily shut two domestic urea plants, creating shortage issues in the market. Parliamentary Secretary Shaista Khan told the House that agricultural production depends on fertiliser availability, so any change in its price and supply is a matter of concern for the government.

She said about 50 per cent of the country's fertiliser needs are met through imports, and prices have also risen amid the Middle East crisis. The secretary said the Ministry of National Food Security and Research is giving priority to farmers' needs, with projects under way including easy loans so farmers can meet their agricultural needs. She added that research is being conducted at NARC on new seed varieties, including seeds compatible with wheat and water-scarce conditions, to help Pakistan's agriculture cope with climate change.
