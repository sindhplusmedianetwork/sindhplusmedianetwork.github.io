Air Pollution Cutting 3.9 Years Off Pakistanis' Lives, Report Finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Air pollution reduces life expectancy in Pakistan by an estimated 3.9 years and contributes to around 128,000 premature deaths every year, according to a new report by the Pakistan Air Quality Initiative. The report, titled 'Unveiling Pakistan's Air Pollution', estimates that pollution imposes an economic burden equal to 6.5 per cent of the country's gross domestic product, while prolonged exposure in Lahore could cut life expectancy by up to seven years. It presents Pakistan's first harmonised, multi-sector emissions inventory alongside a multi-city assessment of air quality across Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad-Rawalpindi and Peshawar.

Lahore's annual average PM2.5 concentration exceeds 110 micrograms per cubic metre, more than 22 times the World Health Organisation's annual guideline of five micrograms. Transport is the largest source of primary PM2.5 emissions in Lahore at 35 per cent, followed by industry at 28 per cent and brick kilns at 17 per cent, while in Karachi industry accounts for 49 per cent of emissions. Winter temperature inversions make the problem worse by trapping pollutants near the ground, and the report outlines measures that could reduce emissions by up to 50 per cent.
