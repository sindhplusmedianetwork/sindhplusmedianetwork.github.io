France Confirms First Known Human Case of Shmonda Virus; Patient Dies

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 25, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

France's public health agency, Sante Publique France, has confirmed the first known human case of the Shmonda virus, a pathogen spread by tiny biting insects known as Culicoides. Authorities were informed on October 7 about a patient under treatment at a hospital in Lyon, who was already suffering from a neurological illness and had a severely weakened immune system. The patient later died, and tests on various biological samples confirmed the presence of the virus.

Officials said the case may have resulted from a bite by an infected Culicoides insect, and they are examining the patient's surroundings, travel history and possible contact with animals. Health authorities stressed that no evidence has yet been found of transmission from one person to another or of any danger to the wider population. The World Health Organization said more research is needed to understand how the virus spreads, and its chief, Tedros Ghebreyesus, said the agency is working with French officials to determine the cause of infection and any public health risk.

The Shmonda virus belongs to the Orthobunyavirus group and was first identified in Nigeria in the 1960s, when it was believed it could not pass to humans. French officials suggested the case came to light because of the patient's unusual medical condition and the detailed testing carried out at the Lyon hospital.
