Punjab plans Pakistan's first film royalty law to protect artists

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

LAHORE: Punjab is planning Pakistan's first film royalty law alongside production rebates, cinema restoration and a major investment in filmmaking infrastructure, as the government seeks to revive the province's cinematic heritage and establish Lahore as a competitive centre for film, television and screen tourism. The initiative comes amid renewed debate over artists' rights after filmmaker Sarmad Khoosat questioned the absence of royalty payments when his popular television drama 'Humsafar' was announced for Spanish-language release. 'If only there were royalties,' Khoosat wrote on social media.

Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz Sharif has approved the formulation of the proposed royalty law and directed that it be presented at the next meeting of the Punjab Film Authority. The legislation, if enacted, would seek to address the absence of continuing payments to creative professionals, although its precise provisions have yet to be announced. Pakistan previously had a royalty system for television actors, but the practice was largely abandoned during the 1990s when prominent performers agreed to relinquish residual rights in exchange for higher upfront payments from state-run Pakistan Television.

Alongside the proposed legal framework, the Punjab government has approved financial incentives: Punjabi-language feature films produced in the province will be eligible for a 35% rebate, while Urdu-language feature films will qualify for a 20% rebate. The wider strategy centres on Lahore Film City, a proposed complex with a target launch date of December 15, 2026, which will include a studio facility, modern sound stages, post-production facilities, a film and music school, a museum, a film trading house and a convention centre described as Pakistan's largest. The first meeting of the Punjab Film Authority, chaired by Senior Provincial Minister Marriyum Aurangzeb, also approved measures to restore older cinemas, install solar power systems and introduce online cinema booking.
