Sri Lanka Invites Pakistani Exporters to Tap $19 Billion Import Market

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Sri Lanka's Consul General in Karachi, Sanjeewa Pattiwila, has urged Pakistani exporters to take full advantage of the free trade agreement to access Sri Lanka's import market of about $19 billion. Addressing members of the SITE Association of Industry in Karachi, he said both countries had benefited from GSP and GSP+ facilities and have complementary capacities in textiles, garments, construction and pharmaceuticals, with opportunities for joint ventures and value addition. He said Pakistani woven cloth has been a key raw material for Sri Lankan garment manufacturers exporting to the European Union, and several Pakistani companies have set up production units in Sri Lanka.

The envoy said Sri Lanka allows imports of up to 6,000 metric tonnes of rice, with Basmati rice being popular among consumers and tenders issued every six months. He invited the Pakistani business community to participate in the Sri Lanka Expo in January 2027 and suggested that the SITE Association send a trade delegation to explore business opportunities. SITE Association President Abdul Hadi said textiles remain the largest share of bilateral trade and Pakistan is Sri Lanka's second largest SAARC trading partner, stressing the need to increase bilateral trade beyond $500 million.

SITE Vice President Nadeem Polani said new traders face difficulties in the tendering process and high freight charges despite the FTA, while Majeed Aziz called for a review of the FTA, which came into effect in June 2005, and proposed making Colombo a sister port of Karachi and Port Qasim.
