Houston mayor orders police to curtail cooperation with immigration officers

Houston Mayor John Whitmire has ordered the city's police department to curtail its cooperation with federal immigration officers, days after elections swept Democrats to statewide leadership in Texas. Mayor Whitmire said he was taking the action, after consulting with the state's newly elected governor, lieutenant governor and comptroller, to reduce fear and anxiety across the most populous city in Texas and because elections matter. Under the new policy, police officers conducting routine traffic stops of motorists or detaining someone for some other reason would be barred from alerting US Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials solely on the basis of a civil immigration warrant against that person.

Police would be permitted to notify ICE only if the person was also being arrested and charged with committing a jailable offence, the mayor said. This is the right thing to do at the right time, Whitmire, a Democrat who voted against the Texas statute banning so-called sanctuary city policies as a member of the state Senate in 2017, said at a news conference announcing his executive order. I do this to begin a healing process, Whitmire said, adding he hoped his executive order would become a model for other Texas cities.

Joining Whitmire at the press conference, Houston Police Chief J. Noe Diaz said he supported the change and that his department had already begun to implement the new policy. Whitmire indicated he was no longer concerned about potential retribution from the lame-duck political establishment of Republican Governor Greg Abbott, who lost his bid for a fourth term to Democrat Gina Hinojosa on Tuesday.
