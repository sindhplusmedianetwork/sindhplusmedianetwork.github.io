Sindh Lab Declares Six Medicines Counterfeit After Tests Find No Active Ingredients

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 26, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Sindh Drug Testing Laboratory in Karachi has declared seven samples of six widely used medicines counterfeit after laboratory tests failed to detect their required active ingredients. The medicines are Augmentin 625mg, Velosef 500mg, No-Spa Forte 80mg, Mucon Suspension, Brexin tablets and Lexoberon Liquid, according to laboratory reports issued by the laboratory's director, Syed Adnan Rizvi, who is also the provincial government analyst. The samples were declared counterfeit under the Drugs Act, 1976, and the reports set out the test findings, batch numbers and responses from the manufacturers named on the packaging.

No detectable amoxicillin trihydrate or clavulanic acid was found in the Augmentin 625mg sample, and GlaxoSmithKline Pakistan Limited, whose name appeared on the packaging, disowned the product in a letter dated September 24, 2026. The Velosef 500mg sample also failed testing, with no cefradine detected, and GlaxoSmithKline Pakistan denied manufacturing it in a letter dated September 9, 2026. Similarly, no detectable drotaverine hydrochloride was found in the No-Spa Forte 80mg sample, which Hoechst Pakistan Limited disowned in a letter dated September 7, 2026, while the Mucon Suspension sample showed no oxethazaine, aluminium hydroxide or magnesium hydroxide, and Lucky Core Limited Pharmaceuticals denied making it on the same date.

In the case of Brexin tablets, no detectable piroxicam beta-cyclodextrin, the stated active ingredient, was found, and GlaxoSmithKline Pakistan disowned the product through a letter dated September 7, 2026, while two samples of Lexoberon Liquid, from batches numbered 46292 and 43445, failed to show the presence of sodium picosulfate monohydrate.
