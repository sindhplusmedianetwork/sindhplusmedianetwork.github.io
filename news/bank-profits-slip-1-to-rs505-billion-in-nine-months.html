Bank Profits Slip 1% to Rs505 Billion in Nine Months

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The banking sector recorded a marginal decline in profitability in the first nine months of calendar year 2026 as higher interest expenses and operating costs offset growth in non-interest income and lower taxation, an Arif Habib Limited report showed. Banks posted a consolidated profit after tax of Rs505 billion, down 1% year-on-year. Total interest earned rose 6% while interest expenses climbed 9%, leaving net interest income flat at Rs1.6 trillion.

Non-interest income grew about 18% to Rs504 billion, driven by fee income up 11%, foreign exchange income up 36% and capital gains up 30%. The sector recorded a net provisioning reversal of about Rs21 billion, with National Bank of Pakistan posting the largest at Rs6 billion. Operating expenses reached Rs1.1 trillion, up about 15%, while Meezan Bank recorded the lowest cost-to-income ratio at 32%.

On a quarterly basis, listed banks recorded a combined profit of Rs164 billion, down 3% year-on-year. Deposit growth slowed sharply, rising just 0.02% quarter-on-quarter, though some major banks continued to expand market share.
