Peshawar High Court drops 105 petitions after constitutional court's restraining order

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Peshawar High Court on Saturday removed around 105 petitions from its November 10 cause lists, including those challenging the imposition of emergency and governor's rule in the province as well as the alleged illegal detention of Chief Minister Sohail Afridi and several other PTI lawmakers, leaders and workers. The move comes a day after the high court's administrative committee, acting in compliance with a restraining order issued by the Federal Constitutional Court, decided not to proceed with petitions filed under Article 199 of the Constitution on or after October 12. On November 5, a two-member bench of the constitutional court declared that since the armed forces had been called to act in aid of civil power in the entire province under the October 11 order, proceedings before the high court under Article 199 on or after October 12 shall remain suspended.

The constitutional court also decided not to entertain fresh petitions and directed the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa advocate general to produce within seven days a report identifying areas where the armed forces are no longer required to act in aid of civil power. The most significant of the deleted petitions were those challenging the imposition of emergency in the province under Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution, filed by PTI chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan, leaders of Tehreek Tahafuz-i-Aeen Pakistan and the National Democratic Movement, and the KP Bar Council. Petitions challenging the alleged illegal detention of the chief minister, provincial assembly Speaker Babar Saleem Swati, PTI provincial president Junaid Akbar and scores of lawmakers were also deleted, along with petitions on missing persons and those filed by Afghan nationals in the government's repatriation drive.
