Afghanistan Hunger at Highest Level Ever Recorded, Monitor Warns

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 16, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Acute malnutrition and hunger in Afghanistan have reached the highest levels ever recorded, with 15.9 million people facing acute food insecurity and conditions expected to worsen in the coming months, according to a global hunger monitor report released on Thursday. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) found record acute malnutrition for the August-October period, with more than 32 per cent of the population at crisis level — phase three or above — including 3.4 million people in emergency, phase-four conditions. The deterioration is driven by disease outbreaks and inadequate water, sanitation and hygiene conditions, compounded by sharply reduced access to nutrition services.

The situation is projected to worsen during the November-to-April winter lean season, when nearly 19.2 million people are expected to face phase three or above, including 5 million in emergency food insecurity, while 4.04 million children under five are projected to need malnutrition treatment between January and December 2027. Although improved rainfall and better agricultural production have supported food availability, declining humanitarian assistance is reducing households' ability to cope. The World Food Programme and the Food and Agriculture Organisation issued a joint appeal on Thursday for urgent funding to scale up food assistance, emergency agricultural support and malnutrition prevention and treatment.

"We still have a window to act, but it is closing fast," said WFP country director John Aylieff, adding that with urgent support families can get through the winter with dignity, while FAO representative Richard Trenchard said families need dependable livelihoods to withstand the next shock. Separately, the UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan reported continued erosion of basic human rights between July and September, with women and girls bearing the brunt of Taliban restrictions: girls remain barred from formal education beyond grade six, UNAMA documented more than 50 hijab-related cases mostly in Herat and Kabul, and women were blocked from entering health clinics and markets without a mehram. The mission recorded at least 443 cases and 97 incidents of ill-treatment by virtue and vice inspectors over offences including hijab violations, trimmed beards, skipped prayers and listening to music, alongside public lashings of 22 men and six women, the arrest of 14 former security force members, suspended media outlets and a new police law banning all demonstrations.
