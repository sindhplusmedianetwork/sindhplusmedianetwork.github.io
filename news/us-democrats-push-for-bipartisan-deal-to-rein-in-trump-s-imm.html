US Democrats push for bipartisan deal to rein in Trump's immigration crackdown

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Democratic lawmakers have called for a bipartisan immigration deal, seeking changes to President Donald Trump's enforcement policies and legal status for long-settled immigrants. Members of the House and Senate, including lawmakers elected last week, held a news conference outside the US Capitol, where Representative Veronica Escobar of Texas said she wanted far-reaching reform but recognised that legislation could not pass without Republican support. Senator Ruben Gallego of Arizona urged both parties to pursue an agreement before the current congressional session ends or continue negotiations in the next one.

Representative Sylvia Garcia of Texas proposed starting with the Dream and Promise Act, saying it could provide a basis for broader changes after Democrats assume control of Congress in January. The appeal coincided with Democratic state and local administrations narrowing the role of their police and detention agencies in federal civil immigration enforcement. In Houston, Mayor John Whitmire issued an order barring city police from contacting Immigration and Customs Enforcement about administrative immigration warrants during routine traffic stops and minor, nonviolent violations.

Virginia Governor Abigail Spanberger ordered state law-enforcement agencies to end their 287(g) agreements with ICE, while Maryland Governor Wes Moore signed legislation ending formal 287(g) partnerships. In January, Democrats are expected to use their new congressional committee leadership to scrutinise the Department of Homeland Security, which oversees ICE, and to investigate enforcement practices and detention conditions.
