Oil Prices Ease but Stay Above $100 as Middle East Tensions Linger

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Global oil prices retreated on Monday but remained above 100 dollars a barrel as markets weighed Middle East tensions against fresh moves by Washington. Brent crude futures fell 1.64 per cent to 108.06 dollars a barrel after settling on Friday at 109.86 dollars, the highest level since May 19. US West Texas Intermediate crude declined to 100.88 dollars a barrel.

Brent briefly touched 110 dollars on Friday for the first time since May, as tensions in the Middle East raised fears of disruptions to global oil supplies. The pullback followed US President Donald Trump's signing of an executive order on Friday suspending key sanctions and tariffs under the Graham Act, a move that could pave the way for higher Russian fuel exports. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the president would take up to 60 days to assess the economic impact of the waiver.

Over the past week, Brent gained 4.9 per cent and WTI rose 10.4 per cent, reflecting the market's sensitivity to geopolitical risks. Analysts said continued attacks on shipping and uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz were keeping supply concerns alive, while Gulf stock markets traded subdued.
