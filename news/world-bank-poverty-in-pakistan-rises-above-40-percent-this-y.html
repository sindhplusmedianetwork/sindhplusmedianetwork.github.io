World Bank: Poverty in Pakistan Rises Above 40 Percent This Year

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A World Bank report has revealed that Pakistan's poverty rate has risen to 40.5 percent this year, while political consensus and implementation of the IMF programme are necessary for economic stability. According to the report, the poverty rate was 40.2 percent in 2023, and slow economic growth, high inflation and a decline in wages are behind the rising poverty. The report said job opportunities are insufficient for the 1.6 million new young people entering the workforce in Pakistan every year.

In the next two years, 3.5 million new people will enter the job market. The report estimates that the country's debt ratio will rise from 72.4 percent this year to 73.8 percent, with a further increase to 74.7 percent feared next year. Pakistan needs $22 billion in external financing every year for the next two years, while economic growth this year is expected to remain the lowest among regional countries at 2.8 percent against a target of 3.6 percent.

Growth is expected to pick up to 3.2 percent next fiscal year. The average inflation rate this year is expected to remain at 11.1 percent, with high local electricity and gas prices cited as the major cause of double-digit inflation, while inflation is expected to fall to single digits at 9 percent next fiscal year. The report estimates this year's current account deficit at 0.6 percent and next year's at 0.7 percent, while the fiscal deficit is expected to rise from 6.8 percent to 7.6 percent.
