Astronomers Watch Huge Puffy Star Swallow Smaller Companion in Andromeda

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Astronomers have observed a large dying star, seven times more massive and 18,000 times brighter than the sun, engulfing a smaller companion in a stellar merger unlike any other previously witnessed. The merger involved a pair of stars bound gravitationally in a binary system, in which the larger one greatly expanded in its death throes into a puffy supergiant and enveloped the smaller one, with the two stars then combining into one. This cosmic connection occurred about 2.5 million light-years from Earth on the outskirts of the Andromeda Galaxy, one of our Milky Way's closest galactic neighbors.

The researchers identified the impending engulfment nearly three years before it occurred and then watched as the merger unfolded step by step, providing the first direct observations of a giant star engulfing its companion caught in the act. The larger star puffed up to 350 times the sun's diameter, while the smaller star, whose diameter was about 70 percent that of the sun, orbited at roughly the same distance as between Mars and the sun and was engulfed in the expansion. The researchers detected brightening in 2018 and the smaller star spiralled inward over the next seven years, finally merging with the core toward the end of 2025 and triggering an eruption a million times brighter than the sun.

The researchers first spotted the stars using the WINTER telescope at Palomar Observatory and NASA's NEOWISE space telescope, then watched with SPHEREx and James Webb space telescopes, and nicknamed the merged star Bao from its auto-assigned name AT2025abao.
