Oil prices set for second weekly decline as US sticks with Iran blockade

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Oil prices were mixed on Friday but were set for their second weekly decline, as the United States plans to continue its blockade of Iran, reducing the chance of renewed large-scale fighting that could disrupt supplies from the Middle East, though attacks on shipping remain a danger. Brent futures were little changed, down one cent at $95.41 a barrel, while West Texas Intermediate fell 20 cents, or 0.25 percent, to $88.52 a barrel. Friday's declines reversed two days of gains in a volatile trading week, with earlier hopes for a rapprochement in the US-Israel war against Iran, now in its ninth month, offset by strikes on Saudi Arabian infrastructure by Yemen's Houthis and attacks on tankers carrying crude out of the Gulf.

Prices slipped after US President Donald Trump said on social media that a deal with Iran was not necessary to end the war, indicating he will stick with the blockade on oil shipments and trade and economic sanctions. Despite the swings, Brent is set for a 2.4 percent drop this week, after plunging 16 percent the previous week, while WTI is set for a 2.5 percent decline, after dropping 13 percent previously. Risks remain for oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, the key waterway for crude exports from the main Middle East producers, after Iran's military said on Thursday that four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the strait, though the accounts could not immediately be verified.

Also pressuring prices, US crude and fuel inventories rose last week, with crude stocks up 4.7 million barrels to 414.4 million barrels in the week ended November 6, against expectations for a 1.1 million-barrel draw.
