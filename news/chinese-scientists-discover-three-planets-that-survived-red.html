Chinese scientists discover three planets that survived red giant destruction

Chinese scientists have discovered three exoplanets that survived destruction despite their stars becoming red giants, Express News reported. According to scientists, the current theories suggested these planets should have merged into their host stars. The surprising discovery was made by a research team led by Professor Qian Shengbang of Yunnan University in China.

The research examined three planets named 24Boo, HD12648 and TYC4282-605-1. Experts say that when the hydrogen in a star's core runs out, it expands into a red giant and its size can grow millions of times. During this process, nearby planets are swallowed by the star or destroyed by its gravitational pull.

However, all three discovered planets are still in their orbits despite theoretically being in the danger zone. The researchers confirmed the evolutionary state of the host stars by analysing slight changes in their light. According to Professor Qian Shengbang, the simultaneous discovery of three such planets points to a common physical process rather than a coincidence.

The discovery opens new paths of research and possibilities in current scientific theories about the evolution of planets and stars.
