Pakistan Faces Doctor Shortage Crisis, Needs 179,000 More Doctors

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan needs approximately 179,000 additional doctors to meet the growing demand for healthcare, as public hospitals across the country continue to struggle with overcrowding and an acute shortage of medical facilities. According to official figures, the country has 340,780 registered medical practitioners, including 164,554 male and 176,223 female doctors. The figures indicate a significant shortfall in medical staff against the population's healthcare needs.

The World Health Organization recommends one doctor for every 1,000 people, but teaching hospitals across Pakistan have on average one doctor available for every 1,300 people, reflecting the widening gap between healthcare demand and available medical personnel. The situation is considerably worse at tehsil headquarters and district headquarters hospitals, as well as basic health units, where only 0.23 doctors are available for every 1,000 patients. The shortage has added to the pressure on government hospitals, where patients face overcrowding and limited access to medical facilities.

The issue is further compounded by the lack of a comprehensive national policy to ensure the equitable distribution of medical facilities among government hospitals across provinces and cities. Pakistan Medical Association Secretary General Abdul Ghafoor Shoro also said there are more than 600,000 fake doctors operating across Pakistan.
