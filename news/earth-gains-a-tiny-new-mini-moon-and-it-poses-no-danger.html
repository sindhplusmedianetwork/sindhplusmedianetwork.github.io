Earth gains a tiny new 'mini-moon' — and it poses no danger

A tiny asteroid, 2026 QQ8, was captured by Earth's gravity in April 2026 and will stay until the end of December 2026, completing about one and a half orbits before returning to orbit the Sun. The asteroid, just one to two metres across, is more than 500,000 kilometres away, which is further than the Moon, and it poses no danger. It is too small to see with the naked eye, and it would burn up in the atmosphere if it ever came closer.

Hungarian astronomer Krisztian Sarneczky spotted it in August 2026. The IAU Minor Planet Center designated it on 29 October 2026, confirming Earth's newest temporary companion.
