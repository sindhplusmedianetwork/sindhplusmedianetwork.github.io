Sugar mills demand immediate export of surplus sugar and deregulation of the industry

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 11, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Pakistan Sugar Mills Association has demanded the immediate export of surplus sugar and the deregulation of the sugar industry. The general body meeting of the association was held simultaneously in Lahore, Karachi and Islamabad, and was attended by a large number of mill owners from across the country. According to the spokesperson, the meeting discussed the current situation of the industry in detail, particularly the large surplus stocks of sugar in the country.

Mill owners from Punjab, Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa expressed deep regret and concern, saying that despite promises, the government has not yet allowed the export of the fixed quota, and that even after permission for 200,000 tonnes, around 1.1 million metric tonnes of surplus sugar remain in the country, leaving mill owners unable to run their mills in the present circumstances. The members unanimously agreed that, given the current stock position, the crushing season 2026-27 cannot begin before December. The meeting gave the association's executive committee full powers and directed it to approach the government at the appropriate level with two demands: permission for the immediate export of 400,000 metric tonnes of surplus sugar, and the immediate launch of the long-pending policy to deregulate the sugar sector.

The meeting stressed that launching the policy has now become unavoidable, as another large surplus of sugar is expected by the end of the crushing season 2026-27, and without timely policy measures the industry will face continuously rising stocks and severe financial pressure. The meeting also decided that mill owners will hold a press conference in Lahore on 16 November 2026 to decide the future course of action, and it was also proposed that the association write a formal letter to the Deputy Prime Minister and seek a meeting to inform him directly about the industry's alarming situation.
