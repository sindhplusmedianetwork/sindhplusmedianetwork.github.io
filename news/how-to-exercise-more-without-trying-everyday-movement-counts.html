How to Exercise More Without Trying: Everyday Movement Counts, Research Shows

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Everyday movement that is not formal exercise can help protect health, according to research highlighted in a new report. Activities such as fidgeting, housework, gardening, playing with children and walking faster count as incidental activity, rather than gym workouts. The report cites research suggesting that such movement can help offset the health risks of a sedentary lifestyle, including heart disease and early death.

In one study of more than 22,000 adults in the United Kingdom followed for eight years, even short bursts of vigorous incidental activity were associated with benefit. The report suggests pairing movement with an existing habit, such as walking while on the phone, and breaking up long periods of sitting. The message is that small amounts of movement spread through the day can add up to a meaningful difference for health.
