US could strike Iran this weekend, American envoy suggests; Trump says he knows nothing of it

By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 24, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The United States' ambassador to Turkey, Tom Barrack, has said in an interview that Washington could launch military action against Iran over the coming weekend, though he stressed that regime change is not the objective. According to the French news agency AFP, Barrack — who is also the US special envoy for Syria and Iraq — was asked by online host Mario Nawfal whether the US might attack Iran this weekend, and he replied that it was entirely possible. He added that President Donald Trump remains ready for a deal with Iran and that the US has been engaged in negotiations for months, keeping diplomatic options open while warning of consequences if threats to American allies and Gulf states continue.

When journalists asked Trump about the ambassador's remarks, the president expressed surprise, asking whether his own ambassador had really said that, and added in a sarcastic tone that he seemed to be a powerful man, but that he himself knew nothing of it. The conflicting statements have raised fresh questions about the timing of any possible operation and whether any final decision has been taken. Barrack is not directly responsible for the US negotiations with Iran, and his remarks cannot be treated as official authorization for military action.

The envoy also clarified that Washington does not seek the fall of the Iranian government but wants to improve the regional balance of power so that threats to US allies and Gulf countries are reduced. His position differs from that of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who has recently advocated the end of Iran's current government and pressed the case with Trump in their latest conversation. Trump has previously kept the option of further military action against Iran open, though he has also indicated no attack would come before the US midterm elections on November 3.

Observers say a new US strike on Iran could affect Middle East security, Gulf states, and international energy markets.
