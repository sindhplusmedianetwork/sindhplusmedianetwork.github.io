UN Panel Welcomes Pakistan's Marriage-Age Law, Raises Bonded Labour Concerns

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The United Nations Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights has concluded its review of Pakistan's second periodic report, welcoming the country's 2025 amendment raising the minimum age of marriage to 18. Committee expert Michael Windfuhr welcomed the amendment to the Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929, which set the minimum marriage age at 18 for both males and females. However, he said the committee remained concerned about the high number of child brides and asked about the impact of measures to prevent child marriage.

Another committee expert, Lazhari Bouzid, said bonded labour continued in agriculture and in the informal sector, particularly brick making, and asked how Pakistan had responded to reports that construction companies and government agencies bought bricks produced through debt bondage. The Pakistani delegation, led by Minister of State for Law and Justice Barrister Aqeel Malik, said child marriage restraint laws had set the minimum age at 18 in Islamabad, Balochistan, Punjab and Sindh, and criminalised child, early and forced marriages. On flood recovery, the delegation said 600,000 of the 2.1 million homes destroyed in the 2022 floods had been rebuilt, with reconstruction of the rest under way.

The committee's concluding observations will reflect the information shared during the dialogue, which the panel hopes Pakistan will implement in good faith.
