Global tech stocks plunge as US midterms fuel fears over AI

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 06, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Technology shares plunged on Friday after a rout on Wall Street, as fears grew that Democrats would accelerate a backlash against artificial intelligence after their defeat of President Donald Trump's Republicans in the US midterm elections. The Tokyo and Seoul stock markets closed out their weeks with losses of six per cent following the Nasdaq's 5.5 per cent collapse on Thursday, its worst showing since April last year when Trump unveiled his wide-ranging global tariffs. Data centres have faced broad opposition from voters across the political spectrum in the United States, and many midterm winners have pledged to put the brakes on such projects, while progressive US Senator Bernie Sanders called for a national moratorium on their building.

The results compounded already heightened fears about the hundreds of billions firms had pumped into the sector and when they would see a return, while the recent spike in borrowing costs also fuelled concerns about their financial position. Bloomberg said the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had collapsed around 16 per cent this week, while on Seoul's Kospi index chip giant SK hynix slumped 10 per cent and its rival Samsung lost 6.4 per cent. Tokyo dived 6 per cent on Friday as Japanese tech investor SoftBank gave back more than 10 per cent, while chipmaker Kioxia collapsed 8.7 per cent and semiconductor tester Advantest retreated 5 per cent, and a 4.7 per cent drop for Taiwanese chip giant TSMC dragged down Taipei's Taiex.

The London, Paris and Frankfurt stock markets shed around 2 per cent, while the dollar firmed before key US jobs data on Friday and oil prices steadied following steep declines this week. Some analysts said the sell-off looked overdone and the bottom was not far off, with futures contracts pointing to a rebound on the Nasdaq later in the afternoon.
