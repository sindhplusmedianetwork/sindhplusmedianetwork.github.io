Israeli Ad Firm Hacked Al Jazeera Journalists' Phones With 'Zero-Click' Spyware, Citizen Lab Finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

An Israeli advertising firm ran a sweeping 'zero-click' espionage campaign through ordinary online advertisements that infected the phones and computers of Al Jazeera journalists in 2024 and 2025, according to the University of Toronto's Citizen Lab. Al Jazeera's technical team noticed unusual activity in its systems in 2024 and brought in Citizen Lab to investigate; the network called it an 'attack on press freedom' and demanded a full independent investigation. The most alarming finding is that the journalists never had to click anything — the moment an ordinary-looking ad, such as one for a mobile phone or earbuds, appeared on screen, the Israeli-made spyware slipped into the device.

Experts have dubbed the technique 'Ad Fiction', describing it as the first time online advertisements have been weaponised to hack journalists. The report says at least 45 Al Jazeera journalists and staff members were infected, after which hackers could steal files, documents, emails and passwords, and gain access to every keystroke, screenshots, microphones, cameras, WhatsApp and other messages. The malicious ads ran on 120,000 websites and 75,000 mobile apps, and the net was cast far wider than Arab media — NATO, the United Nations, the Pentagon, the European Commission and the International Court of Justice, which issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu, were also caught in it, while nearly every website in the Palestinian territories carried the spy ads.

Google has blacklisted the firm, Discover-Tech, for violating its policies, and a European advertising body has suspended it. The revelations echo a 2020 incident in which the phones of 36 Al Jazeera journalists were hacked with the Israeli NSO Group's Pegasus software.
