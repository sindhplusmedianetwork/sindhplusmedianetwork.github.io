Peaceful Resolution of Disputes Central to Pakistan's Foreign Policy: FO

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan remained committed to the peaceful resolution of disputes as a central pillar of its foreign policy, alongside national security, economic prosperity and regional cooperation, Foreign Office spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan said on Friday at a weekly press briefing. He recalled Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif's November 10-12 visit to Antalya for the COP31 climate summit, where he met Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and highlighted Pakistan's efforts towards rapid solarisation and renewable energy. Khan said Pakistan hosted the 30th ECO Council of Ministers meeting in Islamabad on November 15 and 16 as the organisation's chair for 2026-27, and that FM Dar held bilateral meetings with counterparts from Iran, Tajikistan, Turkey and Kyrgyzstan.

He noted the seventh round of political consultations with Italy and the 20th round with Sweden on November 19, Dar's November 17 meeting with Turkmenistan's Foreign Minister Rashid Meredov, and talks with ASEAN Secretary-General Dr Kao Kim Hourn on November 19. He announced Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik had been nominated to represent Pakistan at the SCO Civilisation Dialogue Forum in New Delhi from November 20 to 22. Khan reiterated Pakistan's condemnation of the Houthi militia's attack on a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District as a grave violation of Saudi Arabia's sovereignty, and on Palestine said Pakistan had joined other foreign ministers in condemning an Israeli airstrike on the Nasr Medical Complex in Khan Younis, reaffirming support for a two-state solution based on the June 4, 1967 lines with East Jerusalem as Palestine's capital.
