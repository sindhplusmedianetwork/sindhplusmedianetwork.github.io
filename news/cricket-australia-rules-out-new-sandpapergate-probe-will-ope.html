Cricket Australia rules out new Sandpapergate probe, will open forum for players

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Cricket Australia on Wednesday ruled out reopening an investigation into the 2018 ball-tampering scandal but will launch an independent forum for players to share their version of events as a circuit breaker. Festering wounds over what happened in the third Test at Cape Town eight years ago reopened last week when former Australian cricketer Usman Khawaja released his autobiography, in which he alleged that the whole team knew about the plot to rough up the ball with sandpaper to make it swing, an allegation strenuously denied by current captain Pat Cummins. A Cricket Australia probe at the time concluded only skipper Steve Smith, his deputy David Warner and Cameron Bancroft were in the know, with all three sent home in disgrace and banned.

Warner, who has backed Khawaja's new version of events, accused Cricket Australia of conducting a shoddy investigation, claiming not all players were interviewed. Chief executive Todd Greenberg, speaking in Hobart ahead of Australia's one-day clash with England, said the investigation has been completed and ruled out making it public, citing confidentiality. Interim chairman David Boon said instead that a forum, likely led by a former judge, would be established, but there would be no new sanctions coming out of it, and Cricket Australia hopes it can be wrapped up within a month.

Warner said he agreed there should be no new sanctions but would prefer an open inquiry, while Khawaja welcomed a safe space to chat but voiced concern that it would be behind closed doors and the board would decide what comes out at the end.
