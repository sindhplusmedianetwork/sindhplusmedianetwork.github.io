Sons of the mountains in danger: climate change threatens life in Gilgit-Baltistan

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The people of Gilgit-Baltistan are called the sons of the mountains, but living in the shadow of the mountains is not easy for everyone. Life there is beautiful, yet behind that beauty lies a long story of hardship, deprivation and danger. The high mountains, snow-covered peaks and great glaciers are a priceless natural treasure for the world, but the people living among them are today fighting for their survival.

Climate change has affected natural systems around the world, but its effects are felt most in areas where glaciers, glacial lakes and mountain rivers lie close to human settlements, and Gilgit-Baltistan is one of those regions. Its glaciers are vital not only for local people but for the water system of Pakistan and the whole region, as the water flowing from them is tied to farming, settlements and the life of rivers. When a glacial lake bursts, when hill torrents turn towards settlements and when floods wash away homes, fields, link roads and people's hopes, the first to suffer are those who have lived in the shadow of these mountains for generations.

The tragedy, the piece argues, is that the state machinery moves only after a disaster has passed, with visits to affected areas, expressions of regret, sympathy for victims and promises of rehabilitation, but with time the victims' problems disappear from government priorities until the next flood. It asks whether merely consoling a citizen whose home has been destroyed fulfils the state's responsibility, and whether shifting affected families permanently to safe places, building protective embankments, identifying dangerous locations and setting up an effective early warning system are not the government's duty. The Karakoram Highway, the piece adds, is another great test for the people of Gilgit-Baltistan.
