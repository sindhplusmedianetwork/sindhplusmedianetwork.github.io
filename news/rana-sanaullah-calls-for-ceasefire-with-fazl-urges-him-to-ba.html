Rana Sanaullah calls for 'ceasefire' with Fazl, urges him to back armed forces

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Adviser to the Prime Minister on Political Affairs Rana Sanaullah on Wednesday called for a 'ceasefire' between the government and Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman, saying that he should not, under any circumstances, stand on the other side in the war against terrorism. Speaking on the private television programme Capital Talk, Sanaullah acknowledged Maulana Fazl's efforts and sacrifices in the fight against terrorism and said that if, for some reason, Maulana stood on the other side, it would be a loss for the country. He said he would discuss the matter with the prime minister and would also speak to Maulana Fazl, stressing that both sides have to make an effort and there should be a ceasefire in this matter.

Sanaullah expressed concern over the JUI-F chief's recent speeches, saying he appeared to be attributing the mistakes and undemocratic actions of successive establishments over several decades to the current military leadership. He said there was a need to engage Maulana Fazl in dialogue and explain why such criticism was inappropriate, adding that the incumbent army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, had brought honour to the armed forces, the nation and Pakistan through Marka-e-Haq. He stressed that all political differences should be set aside in the fight against terrorism, which he described as a threat to Pakistan's existence, and said that given Maulana Fazl's standing and influence in Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa, he should support the armed forces in their fight against terrorism.

Tensions between the JUI-F chief and the federal government have escalated sharply over constitutional disputes, security policies in K-P and new accountability inquiries targeting his inner circle. Following the imposition of the emergency and governor's rule in K-P, Fazl accused the government of backing out of negotiations with the PTI and the opposition despite initially agreeing to their demands, and he categorically rejected the governor's rule, saying that if an emergency had to be imposed, it should be imposed on the federal government.
