'Lawfare' now the biggest threat to journalists in Pakistan, says Freedom Network

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan remained deeply hostile to journalism and its practitioners in 2025-26 despite a decline in documented violations against media workers, according to a new report by Islamabad-based media watchdog Freedom Network. The annual Impunity Report 2026, titled 'From Violence to Lawfare: Process as Punishment for Journalists', says about 10 cases a month against media personnel showed that fewer violations did not mean greater safety. Cases documented between October 2025 and August 2026 included one work-related killing, three attempted assassinations, 18 threats of murder or harm, 11 assaults, 13 legal actions and 32 threats of legal action.

Punjab recorded 49 cases, just over half the total, followed by Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad and Balochistan, with digital and independent journalists targeted most often. The report identifies the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (Peca) and its increasing use against journalists by the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency to punish dissent as 'central to this legal siege' of online journalism. It says that even without a confirmed FIR or conviction, the process itself becomes 'punitive' through summonses, device scrutiny, travel, legal costs, detention, repeated hearings and reputational harm.

The report adds that relief is commonly limited to bail or procedural protection rather than final vindication, and expresses concern over the lack of institutional safeguards despite two high-powered safety commissions. The federal journalist protection mechanism is 'effectively dysfunctional', it says, while the Sindh Commission, although more visible, has limited reach and protection impact outside Karachi.
