Astronomers spot possible new dwarf planet beyond Pluto, named ED 138

By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read

Astronomers have identified a possible new dwarf planet beyond the orbit of Pluto, provisionally named ED 138. The discovery emerged from observations by the Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile, which began operating in 2025 and carries the world's largest digital camera, capable of photographing the entire sky every three days.

Astronomer Matthew Holman spotted the object while analyzing data collected in early 2026, reviewing observations of billions of moving bodies. He later worked with University of Washington astronomer Mario Jurić to verify the find. Jurić located 20 additional sightings of ED 138 in the observatory's records dating back to March 2026.

Using data from other sources, the researchers were also able to trace the object's orbit back ten years. If future observations confirm the discovery, ED 138 could join the five officially recognized dwarf planets: Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake and Eris. Further observations and verification are still needed before it can be formally classified as a dwarf planet.
