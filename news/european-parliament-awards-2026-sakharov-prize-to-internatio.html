European Parliament Awards 2026 Sakharov Prize to International Criminal Court

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The European Parliament has awarded its 2026 Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought to the International Criminal Court (ICC) and its officials, sending a strong message of support for international justice. Announcing the decision on Thursday, October 22, at the plenary in Strasbourg, Parliament President Roberta Metsola said the European Union is built on respect for multilateralism and international institutions, and that the prize recognises the court's important work in upholding international law and justice. The recognition is the second for the war crimes tribunal this month and comes less than two weeks after US President Donald Trump announced sanctions against the court on October 9, hours after former ICC judge Navi Pillay was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

The Hague-based ICC has long been targeted by Trump, whose campaign against it escalated after the court announced arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his former defence minister. ICC President Tomoko Akane said the unprecedented challenges made the prize all the more significant, adding that there is no peace without justice and no security without law. The prize, named after the late Soviet dissident Andrei Sakharov, has been awarded annually since 1988, and the court is due to collect the 50,000-euro award in Strasbourg on December 16.
