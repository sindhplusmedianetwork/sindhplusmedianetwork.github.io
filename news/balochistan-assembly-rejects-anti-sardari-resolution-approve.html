Balochistan Assembly rejects anti-sardari resolution, approves Arabic in primary schools

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Balochistan Assembly on Friday failed to approve a resolution seeking effective and non-discriminatory implementation of the 1976 Act aimed at ending the traditional Sardari system across the province, while it unanimously passed another resolution calling for the introduction of basic Arabic as a compulsory subject in all government primary schools. The session, which began 40 minutes late under the chairmanship of Deputy Speaker Ghazala Gola, saw Jamaat-e-Islami member Maulana Hidayatur Rehman Baloch present the resolution on the Sardari system, arguing that the 1976 Act was enacted nationwide to abolish illegal powers, coercion and outdated tribal practices under the system. He claimed that even with 140 sardars, none could protect Asma Jattak's father, and demanded a ban on the use of the titles Sardar and Nawab.

Provincial Minister Mir Zahoor Buledi said there was no opposition to the persons of nawabs and sardars, but certain incidents of human rights violations were unacceptable, while Provincial Minister Ali Madad Jattak supported the resolution, stating that the 1976 Act had abolished the system. After a vote, the resolution failed for lack of majority. Earlier, JUI's Shahida Rauf moved the resolution for basic Arabic in primary schools, saying education must include moral and religious development and that Arabic, being the language of the Holy Quran, would help children understand its vocabulary and meaning.

A separate resolution seeking direct Quetta-Sukkur flights could not be put to a vote after the House lost quorum, and the session was adjourned until Monday.
