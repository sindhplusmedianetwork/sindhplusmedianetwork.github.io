SBP Annual Report Sees Stronger Economic Resilience Despite Shocks

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's economic resilience has strengthened despite a series of shocks, the State Bank of Pakistan governor's Annual Report for fiscal year 2025-26 says. The report cites monsoon floods, uncertainty over United States trade tariffs, the Middle East conflict and a sharp rise in global commodity prices as the main shocks. It says average national consumer price inflation was 5.5 percent for July to February 2026, close to the upper bound of the medium-term target range.

However, a rise in global oil prices after the Middle East conflict pushed headline inflation into double digits from April 2026. The report notes that foreign exchange reserves exceeded the 18 billion dollar target for end-June 2026, with the central bank making net purchases of 8.1 billion dollars and cutting its forward and swap liabilities by 1.6 billion dollars. The Monetary Policy Committee cut the policy rate by 50 basis points in December 2025 and raised it by 100 basis points in April 2026.

The report adds that fiscal consolidation, successful IMF reviews and stronger reserves helped improve sovereign ratings, restore investor confidence and support exchange-rate stability.
