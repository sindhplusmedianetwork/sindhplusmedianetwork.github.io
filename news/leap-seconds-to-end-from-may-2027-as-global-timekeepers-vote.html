Leap Seconds to End From May 2027 as Global Timekeepers Vote for Change

The world's official timekeepers have decided to end the practice of adding leap seconds to global time, with the change taking effect on May 20, 2027. The resolution was adopted on Thursday, October 15, 2026, at the 28th meeting of the General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) in Versailles, France, where the future of the global time system was under discussion. Until now, an extra second has occasionally been inserted to keep atomic time synchronised with the Earth's rotation, which speeds up and slows down by tiny amounts.

From May 20, 2027, Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) will run continuously with no pauses for extra seconds. The old system existed to reconcile two different ways of measuring time: UTC, based on ultra-precise atomic clocks, and astronomical time, determined by the Earth's rotation. Experts backed the change because of the risks leap seconds pose to computer systems, and because of the growing prospect of having to introduce a negative leap second — removing a second — for the first time in history.
