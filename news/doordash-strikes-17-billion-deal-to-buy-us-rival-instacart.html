DoorDash strikes $17 billion deal to buy US rival Instacart

By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read

DoorDash said on Thursday it would buy Instacart in a deal valued at about $17 billion, stepping up its push into the fast-growing US grocery delivery market as competition intensifies among app-based platforms. The deal, DoorDash's biggest yet, will give the company greater scale as it expands beyond restaurant delivery into groceries and other retail categories, helping it take on deep-pocketed retailers such as Walmart and Amazon. 'Grocery is one of the hardest things to get right online,' said Tony Xu, co-founder and CEO of DoorDash, adding that Instacart has spent years learning to do that well for grocers.

Instacart founder Apoorva Mehta said the two companies had explored a combination on and off for years. The companies began talks about a potential deal over the summer, and in early September, Instacart's CEO signalled that the company was exploring strategic alternatives, according to a source familiar with the matter. Instacart also held preliminary deal talks with Uber, but the ride-hailing company ultimately did not pursue a transaction, in part because it had announced a $14.8 billion acquisition of German food delivery platform Delivery Hero just a few months earlier.

DoorDash has offered Instacart shareholders $65 per share, a premium of about 26% to the stock's closing price on October 22 before reports of deal talks first emerged. The consideration will be at least 75% cash. The transaction is expected to close at the end of 2027 or the first half of 2028.

'The main risk is regulatory approval,' analysts at CFRA Research said in a note, adding that the long timeline suggests both sides expect an extended antitrust review. DoorDash plans to retain Instacart's brand and app after the deal closes and combine the companies' membership programs.
