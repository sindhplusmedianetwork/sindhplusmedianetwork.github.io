Energy Drinks Pose Serious Risks to Children, Experts Warn

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 23, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Health experts are warning parents about the serious risks energy drinks pose to children, saying high caffeine levels can cause abnormal heartbeats, increased heart rate, high blood pressure, sleep disturbances, anxiety and seizures. A newly formed coalition of US doctors has highlighted the risks in the wake of a sponsorship deal between Monster Energy and the Big 12 college athletic conference, asking conference leaders to pause the sponsorship and the NCAA to review energy drink marketing to youths. The doctors cited the deaths of three children, including Davis, a 16-year-old from South Carolina whose heart fell out of rhythm after chugging an energy drink.

Idaho cardiologist Dr. Matthew Nelson said children would see the drinks promoted by admired universities and athletes and think of them as sports drinks, which they are not. The American Academy of Pediatrics has long advised that children and teenagers avoid energy drinks entirely, recommending that those aged 12 to 18 limit caffeine to 100 milligrams a day, while a single can may contain 80 to 300 milligrams.

Separately, a French parliamentary report submitted on October 7, 2026, proposed banning the sale of energy drinks to under-16s, citing rising palpitations and sleep disorders among teenagers. Experts advise parents to read labels carefully, keep such products out of reach, and offer water or milk instead.
