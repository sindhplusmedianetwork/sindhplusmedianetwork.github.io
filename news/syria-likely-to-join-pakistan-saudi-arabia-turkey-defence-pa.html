Syria likely to join Pakistan-Saudi Arabia-Turkey defence pact; decision reported but no formal announcement yet

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 09, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Syria's chances of joining the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have increased, with reports saying Damascus has decided to join but has not announced it publicly. The Associated Press reported on November 8 that two Syrian officials said the decision had been taken, while a regional official said talks are still under way, so Syria cannot yet be called a formal member. The three officials spoke on condition of anonymity because of the sensitivity of the matter.

Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani had said on October 31 at the Manama Dialogue conference in Bahrain that his country was reviewing the possibility of joining the alliance. Defence cooperation between Syria and Saudi Arabia has accelerated, with a defence cooperation agreement signed on November 4 and Syrian military advisers arriving in Saudi Arabia. The report said an initial group of around 600 Syrian special forces personnel is expected to be sent to Saudi Arabia, though this has not been officially confirmed, and Damascus has denied reports of sending combat troops to Yemen.

The Mecca agreement, reached in August 2026, treats an armed attack on one member as an attack on all three, and Pakistan's military spokesperson has already clarified that the alliance is defensive, not aggressive.
