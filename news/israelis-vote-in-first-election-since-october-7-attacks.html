Israelis Vote in First Election Since October 7 Attacks

By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Israelis began voting on Tuesday in the country's first general election since the Hamas-led attack of October 7, 2023, in a vote widely seen as a test of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's leadership. Polls opened at 7am local time, with around 7.3 million people eligible to elect the 120 members of the Knesset. The Central Elections Committee said turnout in the first three hours reached 16.68 per cent, the highest at that stage since 1981, with more than 1.2 million Israelis having cast ballots by 10am.

Netanyahu, 77, Israel's longest-serving prime minister, faces his main challenger Gadi Eisenkot, 66, a former military chief whose son and two nephews were killed fighting in Gaza. Final polls showed the two blocs running neck and neck, with neither projected to win the 61 seats needed for a majority in the 120-seat parliament. The election is the first since the October 7 attack, in which more than 1,200 people were killed and 251 taken hostage, and the wars that followed.

Polls close at 10pm, when broadcasters will release exit polls, and coalition talks after the vote are likely to decide who becomes the next prime minister.
