UN report urges Sindh to take stock of land and water resources

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 29, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A United Nations report has advised the Sindh government to take careful stock of its land and water resources, saying water and land management in the province has become complicated. In its study titled 'Water and Land Accounting in Sindh: Quantifying Water Availability and Uses', the Food and Agriculture Organisation said the system now involves multi-layered regulations and varied approaches to water supply and access for different uses. Traditional seasonal irrigation allocations and canal operations, once regulated by intermittent flow based on capacity limits, have been replaced by a new regime focused on availability and access.

Limited domestic supplies that once depended on irrigation canals have been supplemented by dedicated urban supply structures, while lakes, local dams and drainage networks have become a significant addition to the traditional irrigation system. The report warned that gaps and inaccuracies in public-sector data can distort water accounting, influence decision-making and lead to misguided development priorities. The Crop Reporting Service was identified as the most problematic source, with its land boundaries, crop survey methods, sampling and district coverage all needing revision, and its irrigated-area figures do not align with canal command areas reported by the Sindh Irrigation Department.

The gap between formally allocated water and the water people and sectors can actually access is growing, and investments in drinking-water and industrial infrastructure and local storage are not reflected in operations and monitoring. The report recommended that Sindh establish regular annual and seasonal sector-based provincial water accounts to address water availability, water stress, inter-sectoral transfers, unaccounted uses and climate-induced changes.
