Pakistan wastes Rs3.5 trillion a year on energy imports, experts warn

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 06, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan is wasting 3.5 trillion rupees annually, more than three times the federal development budget, on unnecessary and rising energy-sector imports, experts warned at an event on Pakistan's energy security organised by the LUMS Energy Institute. The experts blamed mismanagement, delays in adopting building codes and the failure to implement an indigenisation plan for energy materials and equipment that used to be produced locally. Research presented at the event showed that despite generation capacity exceeding 36,000 megawatts, Pakistan's base electricity load of about 8,000 megawatts did not grow between 2015 and 2023, even as the population grew.

Conversely, the country imports 2.7 billion dollars worth of liquefied natural gas for four winter months for space and water heating, while most of the electricity capacity remains unused. The federal government had approved an energy conservation building code in 2023 which could have reduced cooling load by 51 per cent, but the provincial governments of Punjab, Sindh and Balochistan have not yet adopted these codes. Research indicated that improving energy efficiency in just 25 per cent of residential buildings could reduce peak electricity demand by approximately 3,000 megawatts and yield potential annual energy-cost savings of 442.6 billion rupees.

Experts identified approximately 1.1 trillion rupees in potential indigenisation opportunities against 1.9 trillion rupees in projected equipment demand across the transmission and distribution sectors. They urged the government to expedite the long-delayed Power Sector Indigenisation Plan to strengthen domestic manufacturing and reduce import dependence.
