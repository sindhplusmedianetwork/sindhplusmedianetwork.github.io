World Needs $35 Billion a Year More for Pre-Primary Education by 2030, Report Says

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 21, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Ensuring that every child receives at least one year of pre-primary education by 2030 would require more than $35 billion in additional annual investment globally, according to a new report released on World Children's Day. The 2026 Global Report on Early Childhood Care and Education, published jointly by Unesco, Unicef and the OECD, says pre-primary enrolment has stalled over the past decade, with only about six in ten children entering primary school worldwide having attended at least one year of preschool. The report estimates the cost of inaction at an average of 3 per cent of GDP globally, rising to 7 per cent in low-income countries, while every $1 invested in closing childcare policy gaps could generate an estimated $3.76 in additional global GDP by 2035.

It says only 64 per cent of children aged 24 to 59 months are developing as expected for their age, falling to 53 per cent in sub-Saharan Africa. Enrolment in early childhood education rose from 64 per cent in 2003 to 75 per cent in 2024, but growth has stalled over the past 10 years, and participation one year before primary entry exceeds 90 per cent in Europe, Northern America and Latin America and the Caribbean, against close to 50 per cent in Northern Africa, Western Asia and sub-Saharan Africa. Government spending on pre-primary education averages only 3 to 4 per cent of education budgets in low- and middle-income countries, which would need to invest an average of $69 billion a year between 2027 and 2030, leaving an average annual financing gap of $35 billion.
