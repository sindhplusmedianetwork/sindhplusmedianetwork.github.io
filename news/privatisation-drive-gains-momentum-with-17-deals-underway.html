Privatisation Drive Gains Momentum With 17 Deals Underway

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 28, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Work is underway on 17 major privatisation transactions, including nine power distribution companies, three major airports, financial institutions and the insurance sector, said Prime Minister's Adviser on Privatisation and Chairman of the Privatisation Commission Muhammad Ali. He said the privatisation of PIA and First Women Bank showed that major economic goals can be achieved through cooperation among all relevant stakeholders, speaking during a visit to the Korangi Association of Trade and Industry on Friday. He said the due diligence process for the privatisation of Fesco, Gepco and Iesco is underway, with bidding expected in the first quarter of 2027, adding that the privatisation of Hesco and Sepsco would also be taken up in subsequent phases.

The government is targeting the issuance of a Request for Proposals during the same period to outsource operations at Islamabad International Airport, Mr Ali said. According to the association's press release, the adviser also said that the Hyderabad-Tando Adam and Tando Adam-Nawabshah sections of the Hyderabad-Sukkur M-6 Motorway are expected to attract a combined investment of about 700 million dollars, structured under a 25-year concession model, with 156 million dollars in viability gap funding proposed for the first section. He said the primary objective of privatisation is to improve the efficiency of public sector entities, reduce financial losses, ensure the effective use of national resources, and ease the burden on the national exchequer through a transparent, merit-based process.

He observed that uncertainty surrounding the business environment has remained the biggest obstacle to attracting investment, and emphasised that bidders for the power distribution companies must be provided with clear, comprehensive information in advance on future investment plans and multi-year tariff arrangements. He added that fundamental reforms in the energy and taxation sectors are essential to making Pakistan's industries and businesses competitive in international markets, and that a fair and stable taxation system could give businesses the confidence needed for long-term planning and investment.
