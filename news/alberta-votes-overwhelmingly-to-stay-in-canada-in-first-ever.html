Alberta Votes Overwhelmingly to Stay in Canada in First-Ever Separation Referendum

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Alberta residents voted to remain part of Canada in the province's first-ever referendum on separation, delivering a much-needed win for Prime Minister Mark Carney as he works to unify the country amid a trade war with the United States. CBC News, Canada's national broadcaster, called the result late Monday after 33 per cent of the vote had been counted, with 76 per cent of tallied votes supporting the 'remain' side versus 23 per cent favouring a binding vote on separation. The referendum was nonbinding.

'Albertans made a clear choice,' Carney said in a statement. 'They chose unity, to build together, and to take care of each other.' Alberta Premier Danielle Smith, who announced the referendum in May, said late Monday she was 'very pleased' that early results showed widespread support for remaining in Canada, and that she would address Albertans on Tuesday. Pollsters had repeatedly dismissed the possibility of a separatist victory, with data showing about one-third of residents supported a future referendum on leaving.

The vote followed a months-long campaign by a small but vocal separatist group arguing the province's energy wealth subsidises poorer parts of Canada. First Nations groups objected to the idea, as their treaties with Ottawa predate the province, and CBC reported strong First Nations turnout, with one polling station staying open past 10pm MT after temporarily running out of ballots.
