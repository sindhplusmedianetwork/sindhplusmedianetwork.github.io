War, poverty, repression stalk Yemen's ancient capital

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Beset by poverty, war and repression, the residents of Yemen's ancient capital Sanaa, under Houthi control for over a decade, are struggling to survive. The Iran-backed Houthi movement seized the city in 2014, forcing the government to flee south and sparking a brutal civil war in one of the world's poorest countries. Residents say they long for the pre-war days, when salaries were paid in full on a regular basis, but today the Houthis have divided salaries into categories, with one civil servant receiving half a monthly salary once every three months and a teacher receiving half a salary each month.

The Old City, with some buildings dating back nearly a millennium, still bustles with vendors selling grains and spices and restaurants grilling meat, and UNESCO added it to its World Heritage in Danger list in 2015 after it was damaged in fighting. Alongside the struggle for basic necessities, residents complain of constant power cuts, paying two electricity bills, one to the authorities and another to private generators, while water is also cut off and has to be bought from trucks at high cost. Living conditions have worsened in recent weeks with Saudi Arabia intensifying its airstrikes on Sanaa, and 44 people were killed in the outlying village of Al-Sharia on Saturday, according to the Houthi-run Saba news agency.

Under the weight of poverty, the education system has largely collapsed and many young people have dropped out of school, while a former Sanaa University lecturer said a new uneducated generation was growing up and that everyone was afraid to speak, with silence becoming the norm.
