China unveils detailed plan for crewed moon landing

China has unveiled a detailed plan for its first crewed moon landing, adopting a two-launch, lunar-orbit-rendezvous architecture, a senior engineer said on Saturday. Wang Xiang, deputy chief designer of China's manned space program and chief designer of the lunar exploration program, outlined the scheme at the 7th Manned Space Symposium in Beijing. Under the plan, two rockets will separately send the Lanyue lunar lander and the Mengzhou crewed spaceship into the Earth-moon transfer orbit, where the two spacecraft will perform a rendezvous and docking.

Astronauts will then transfer from the Mengzhou spaceship into the Lanyue lander for the descent to the lunar surface and subsequent scientific work. Wang said the approach matches China's existing technical capabilities and that developing the spaceship and lander separately will help standardise production for a long-term family of vehicles. Development of the Long March-10 carrier rocket, the Mengzhou spaceship and the Lanyue lander is progressing smoothly, with major tests already completed.

Wang added that future lunar exploration should build knowledge and capability before moving to resource utilisation, and that achievements from the Chang'e program will support the crewed landing effort.
