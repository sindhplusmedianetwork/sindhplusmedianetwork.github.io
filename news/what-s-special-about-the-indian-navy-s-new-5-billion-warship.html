What's Special About the Indian Navy's New $5 Billion Warships?

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read

India's defence ministry has signed a contract worth more than $5 billion with two domestic shipbuilding companies to add eight modern 'next-generation corvette' warships to its naval fleet. The deal was struck with Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers (GRSE) Kolkata and Goa Shipyard, both prominent state-owned defence shipbuilders in India. Officials say the decision was taken in view of growing strategic threats in the Indian Ocean and to further strengthen the Indian Navy, though some experts also see it as a response to rising threats from China and Pakistan.

The project was approved at a meeting of the Cabinet Committee on Security chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on October 28. These medium-range warships of two to four thousand tons will be built by the state-owned companies and are seen as a step in the navy's modernisation and expansion plan. The first ship is expected to join the Indian Navy fleet by 2030.

Defence analyst Rahul Bedi told BBC Urdu that corvettes are small, fast vessels armed to handle different situations, effective for surveillance up to 200-300 miles in coastal areas. Commodore (retd) Uday Bhaskar said the new ships will have stealth technology, making it hard for an enemy to identify them, and noted the design was prepared by the navy itself, which is important for India's self-reliance goal. Navy chief Admiral Dinesh K Tripathi recently claimed the navy is inducting a domestically built ship or submarine every 40 days and is working to grow the fleet to 200 ships by 2035, from 145 at present.
