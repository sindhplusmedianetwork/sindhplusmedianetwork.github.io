Wildfire smoke blamed for 3,560 deaths in Indonesia in September, report finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Air pollution from severe forest and land fires in Indonesia caused an estimated 3,560 deaths in September 2026, according to a new scientific study. The figure comes from the Helsinki-based Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air, known as CREA, and is roughly double its estimate for August. The report says about 57.8 million people were exposed to air quality ranging from unhealthy to hazardous during September.

Forest and land fires, locally called karhutla, were a major driver of the crisis, especially in Kalimantan and Sumatra. Short-term exposure to fine PM2.5 particles from the fires sharply raised deaths, with about 1,470 deaths linked to fire smoke in four Kalimantan provinces and about 1,420 in five Sumatra provinces. The population-weighted average PM2.5 level in September was 36 micrograms per cubic metre, more than seven times the World Health Organization's annual guideline of 5 micrograms.

Researchers also estimated that non-fire pollution from vehicles, industry and other urban sources caused a further 1,750 deaths, bringing the total to about 5,310 for the month.
