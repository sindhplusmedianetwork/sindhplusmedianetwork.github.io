EU Proposes 3 Percent Tax on Big Tech Companies to Fund Next Budget

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 21, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The European Union has proposed a new tax on the world's large technology companies to raise revenue for its next budget, under which a 3 percent annual tax could be levied on companies earning from online advertisements, digital services and user data, according to AFP. European Council President Antonio Costa presented a new plan in Brussels on Saturday with a 204 billion euro reduction compared to the European Commission's initial proposal of a 2,000 billion euro budget for 2028 to 2034. The proposed digital tax would cover companies earning at least 50 million euros annually from digital activities in the EU with global turnover above 750 million euros.

Documents say 3 percent of annual net revenue from online advertisements, digital intermediation services and user-generated data would be collected from these companies. Costa clarified that the proposed tax is not against any specific company or country. The European Council estimates the new sources could yield about 60 billion euros a year, while German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the proposed budget's overall size remains too high and France's President Emmanuel Macron's office called the new proposal disappointing.
