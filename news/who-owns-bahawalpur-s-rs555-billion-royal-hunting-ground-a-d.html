Who owns Bahawalpur's Rs555 billion royal hunting ground? A decades-old land dispute

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 30, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A Jang investigative report by Fakhar Durrani has reopened questions over the legal ownership of the former Bahawalpur state's royal hunting ground, spread over 312,440 acres. The Cholistan Development Authority (CDA) values the land at Rs555 billion, but Punjab's stance is that it was resumed under land reforms, while the former Nawab's 23 legal heirs and later buyers base their claims on a government-supervised distribution process. The turning point came in 2003, when the handwritten word 'Entire' was added to an official order issued for a committee handling the Nawab family's properties, implying the hunting ground too should be divided among the heirs.

Following that, the CDA recorded 95 transfers in favour of heirs and later buyers, which the Senior Member of Punjab's Board of Revenue later directed to be cancelled. Nawab of Bahawalpur's grandson Sahibzada Gazeen Abbasi disputes the government's position, arguing the land was declared unsettled and unsurveyed with 'zero units' assigned under land reforms, and has questioned NAB's probe for hearing the case without the family's stance. Independent valuers put the land's worth above Rs600 billion, believing its value could double once the legal title is settled.

The roots of the dispute go back to Bahawalpur's accession to Pakistan on 3 October 1947 and its merger into West Pakistan on 17 December 1954. The Nawab died on 24 May 1966, and the deputy land commissioner issued the resumption order for the remaining land on 6 May 1968. NAB approved an inquiry in July 2025 and converted it into a formal investigation in September 2025.
