Riyadh Airport Partly Reopens Despite Renewed Houthi Threats

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Saudi Arabia has partly reopened Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, two weeks after a deadly Houthi attack forced it to close, even as Yemen's Houthis vowed to keep striking the facility. The airport resumed operations at two terminals on Sunday, with Terminal 1 designated for domestic flights and Terminal 2 for international flights operated by national carriers. The airport had halted operations on October 10 following an attack claimed by the Houthis that killed 13 people, the deadliest in the kingdom in more than a decade.

Saudi Arabia said it intercepted another wave of missiles and drones on Sunday, while the Houthis said they had launched attacks on the airport and other sites including Khamis Mushait and Jazan. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree warned that the airport would never be safe and urged airlines, airport staff and travellers not to use it. Despite the announcement, flight tracking data showed little traffic at the airport on Sunday, with most flights cancelled.

Saudi civil defence said shrapnel from intercepted missiles over Khamis Mushait and Ahad Rafidah injured two people and damaged homes and vehicles, while the airport closure had already forced the postponement of the kingdom's flagship investment conference to 2027.
