Pakistan's poverty rate rises to 29 percent, jobs challenge grows, World Bank data shows

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's poverty rate has risen from 22 percent in 2019 to 29 percent in 2025, according to figures cited from the World Bank's report on Pakistan's economy. The bank has pointed to the need to create employment for 3.6 million people every year, and to the risk that this target may not be met. Experts say limited economic growth, a modest rise in per-capita income, inflation and insufficient job opportunities are leaving people facing economic hardship, and that prompt and effective reforms are now essential for sustainable growth.

Figures from the report show economic growth of about 2 percent in the last quarter of the last financial year, with annual growth limited to 3.65 percent, while the rise in the average citizen's per-capita income was only 1.6 percent. Inflation was recorded at 11.1 percent in August 2026, and the real effective exchange rate rose by 10 percent. Experts warn that rising inflation can make essentials, energy and other basic costs harder for ordinary people, especially when income growth does not keep pace with prices.

They add that reducing poverty requires more than higher overall output: the benefits of growth must reach ordinary citizens, real incomes must improve, and stable jobs must be created for lower-income groups. The report projects growth of 3.8 percent in the current financial year, with a possibility of reaching 4.4 percent in an election year, and says wide-ranging reforms could lift growth to between 6.2 and 6.9 percent.
