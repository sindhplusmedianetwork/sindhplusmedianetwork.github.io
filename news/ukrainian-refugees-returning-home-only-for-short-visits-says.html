Ukrainian Refugees Returning Home Only for Short Visits, Says IOM

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Ukrainians who fled abroad because of the war are reluctant to return home permanently in view of security fears, damage to their homes, limited jobs and income opportunities, and a lack of medical facilities. According to the latest figures issued by the United Nations, about 5.9 million Ukrainian citizens are still living abroad as refugees. Instead of returning permanently, many of them are travelling home for temporary visits.

A survey by the International Organization for Migration found that about 90 percent of those returning from Poland intended to stay in Ukraine for less than 30 days. Most of the people surveyed had spent an average of three years abroad, yet their connection with their homeland remains strong, and more than three-quarters had visited Ukraine at least three times since being displaced. The survey found that the main purpose of most return visits was to meet family members, while 85 percent wanted to see their home areas again.

However, only eight percent wanted to stay in Ukraine for more than a month. Arthur Erken, the IOM Regional Director for Europe and Central Asia, said that for many Ukrainians, returning home means meeting family, obtaining medical care or spending a few days in their own homes. He said visiting one's country and being able to live there permanently are two different things, and many Ukrainians are still living between their homeland and the country where they now reside, travelling repeatedly to maintain ties with their loved ones and the places their memories are attached to.
