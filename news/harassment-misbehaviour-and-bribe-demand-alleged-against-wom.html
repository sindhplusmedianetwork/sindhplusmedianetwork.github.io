Harassment, misbehaviour and bribe demand alleged against women members at LDA-run sports complex

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A case of alleged harassment, misbehaviour and demand for a bribe against women members has surfaced at the Minar-e-Pakistan Sports Complex in Lahore, which is run by the Lahore Development Authority, raising questions about the administrative system. A woman member of the complex has submitted a written complaint to senior officials, containing allegations of harassment, financial corruption and inappropriate behaviour against Assistant Manager Muhammad Arslan and the ladies gym coach. The complainant alleged that she was threatened with cancellation of her membership for not paying an alleged bribe of Rs 20,000, and that she has faced continuous misbehaviour and harassment for the past two weeks.

According to the details, the complaint sent to the Chairman of the Management Committee Sports, the Director General of the Lahore Development Authority and the Secretary Chief Engineer says the behaviour of the assistant manager and the ladies gym coach towards her has been extremely inappropriate. The complainant says the two officials have forced her to face mental distress and harassment for the past two weeks, and the complaint points not only to misbehaviour but also to alleged financial irregularities and misuse of authority. The woman member has demanded an immediate, transparent and impartial inquiry into the matter, that the stance of the named officials be heard, that the membership record and related evidence be checked, and that strict departmental action be taken against those responsible if the allegations are proven.

It is worth noting that complaints about the administrative matters of the sports complex and alleged inappropriate behaviour towards women employees had already surfaced in October, when complaints were reported against several officers, and the matter had also reached the Punjab Assembly, and the new complaint from a woman member has now raised fresh questions about administrative oversight, redressal of complaints and the provision of a safe environment for women at the sports complex. On the other hand, the Director of the Sports Complex says a legal inquiry into the matter is under way and action will be taken in accordance with the law once the facts emerge, though the detailed stance of the named assistant manager and ladies gym coach is still awaited.
