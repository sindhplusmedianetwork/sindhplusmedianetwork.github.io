Cummins denies role in sandpaper plot, calls Khawaja claims 'utter nonsense'

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Australia captain Pat Cummins has again denied any involvement in the plot to use sandpaper on the ball during a 2018 Test against South Africa, calling the allegations in Usman Khawaja's autobiography 'utter nonsense'. Khawaja, whose autobiography was published this week, said last weekend that all the players at Newlands knew about the plot, a claim that has triggered calls for a new investigation. Speaking at a press conference on Friday, Cummins said: 'When he says we all knew, if by that he means that we knew sandpaper was going out in the field, he's 100% wrong.

I've said that consistently for the last eight years.' He added that Khawaja's claim that he and others were 'architects of a scheme to ball tamper with sandpaper' was 'complete and utter nonsense'. Steve Smith and David Warner, the then Australian captain and vice-captain, were banned by Cricket Australia from elite cricket for a year over the incident, while opening batter Cameron Bancroft, who carried the sandpaper onto the field, received a nine-month suspension. Cummins said he was genuinely shocked to learn that Khawaja had admitted telling Bancroft to be careful when using sandpaper, saying that was not the team-mate he knew.

He said he respected Khawaja's right to tell his story but felt some of the allegations were very serious and had to be addressed, adding that friends were not casual with each other's lives and reputations. Cummins also confirmed he would not take legal action against Khawaja, saying he had been advised some of the claims were probably defamatory but that it had been eight years and it was time to move on.
