Marital rape leaves millions of wives unprotected across South Asia, research finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Millions of women and girls across eight South Asian countries remain without legal protection from rape by their own husbands, according to new research by SAMAJ, an alliance of groups working for women's rights in the region. The study found that the rate of violence by a partner in South Asia is 35 percent higher than the global average. More than 100 countries have made marital rape illegal, but India, Afghanistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh are still among the roughly three dozen countries where it is not a crime.

Only the Maldives treats a husband who rapes his wife exactly the same as any other man who commits rape, while Nepal and Bhutan have criminalised it but with lighter punishments. The laws in much of the region trace back to British colonial rule, with exemptions written into the 1860 laws of India and Bangladesh and the 1885 law of Sri Lanka. Pakistan removed the marital exemption in 2006, yet no high court has ruled that rape laws apply inside marriage, and a recent high court decision gave husbands wide immunity for non-consensual sex.

In Afghanistan, a new penal code introduced in January allows a husband to be prosecuted for violence against his wife only if he broke a bone, caused an open wound or inflicted severe injuries, and even then the maximum sentence is 15 days in prison. In India, ten petitions challenging the law are pending before the Supreme Court, where final hearings are due to begin on December 9.
