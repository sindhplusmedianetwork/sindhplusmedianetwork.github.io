Merz Urges Russia to End War, Proposes Oleshky Ceasefire as Model for Broader Truce

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 04, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Tuesday hosted Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Berlin and urged Russia to end its war against Ukraine, proposing the regional ceasefire in Oleshky as a model for broader truces. 'Our message to Russia is therefore: it is time for an end to this war,' Merz declared at a joint press conference, adding that the Oleshky arrangement could serve as a model 'for a ceasefire for energy and in the Black Sea.' He said Russia had so far refused such steps, warning that its continued attacks in the Black Sea risked further escalation and a worldwide food crisis at the expense of the poorest countries. Merz noted that NATO allies had agreed on 70 billion euros ($78.3 billion) in support for Ukraine for 2026 and stressed that all partners must fulfil the commitment.

He announced that Germany, France and the United Kingdom would jointly host a Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin on November 19 to coordinate long-term support, security guarantees and diplomacy, and said a German-Ukrainian economic forum would follow the next day. Stressing Europe's central role, Merz said key elements of any peace settlement — sanctions and the security architecture — could only be resolved with European involvement, and welcomed technical-level talks between Russian and Ukrainian representatives scheduled for November 10-11 in Abu Dhabi under US mediation, the first direct talks since February. He also pointed to the Koalicja2026 military exercise beginning this week, centred in Poland with activities in Romanian airspace and the western Black Sea and involving German, British, French and Polish troops, as linked to preparations for future security guarantees.

Zelenskyy said Russia had launched 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles overnight against Ukraine's power grid, and insisted that decisions on Ukraine's future EU membership must involve European countries.
