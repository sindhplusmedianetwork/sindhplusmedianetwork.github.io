Lahore High Court moved against dropping 'university' from names of six institutions

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A petition has been filed in the Lahore High Court challenging the removal of the word 'university' from the names of six public sector varsities and demanding the restoration of their former title of 'college'. The petitioner, Raheela Murtaza, an MS student at the Government Sadiq College for Women (University), filed the petition through Advocate Aftab Mubarik. The petition mainly challenges the Public Sector Universities (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act XXIII of 2026), published in the Punjab Gazette on May 6, 2026, and the subsequent notification issued by the Higher Education Department on October 23, 2026, through which the word 'university' was removed from the names of six higher education institutions.

It contends that the amendment arbitrarily downgraded the nomenclature of the institutions without consulting stakeholders, including vice chancellors, faculty members, academic bodies and students. The petition argues that the decision could adversely affect the academic standing, international recognition, research records and global rankings of the institutions, as well as the degree verification of thousands of current and former students. It claims the change could also create complications for graduates seeking employment, higher education and immigration-related credential verification abroad, as their degrees and academic records carry the previous university names.

The petition asks the court to declare the amendment unconstitutional and illegal to the extent that it changes the status and nomenclature of the affected universities.
