Israel's President Urges Compromise as Deadlocked Election Heads to Coalition Talks

Israel's President Isaac Herzog has opened formal consultations on forming a new government with an appeal for compromise, declaring that neither side won the country's divisive October 27 elections. Herzog condemned 'racist' rhetoric directed at Arab parties, which made significant gains in the vote, and urged political leaders to rise above the campaign's bitterness. With no bloc securing a majority in the 120-seat Knesset, the president must decide whether to task incumbent Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu or his rival Gadi Eisenkot with forming a coalition first.

Netanyahu has attacked Arab-majority electoral lists during the campaign, while Eisenkot has accused him of trying to 'undermine Israeli democracy' by questioning the results. Herzog's remarks set the tone for what could be weeks of coalition bargaining in a deeply fractured parliament. Analysts say the Arab parties' strengthened position could make them kingmakers in the coming negotiations.
