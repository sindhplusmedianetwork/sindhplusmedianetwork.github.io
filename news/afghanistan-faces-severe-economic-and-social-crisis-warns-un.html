Afghanistan Faces Severe Economic and Social Crisis, Warns UN Envoy

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Afghanistan is facing a humanitarian crisis of a serious nature, and despite apparent law and order and relative stability, a weak economy, strict restrictions on civil rights and severe humanitarian needs have become grave threats to the country's future. The United Nations Deputy Special Representative and humanitarian coordinator for Afghanistan, Bruno Lemarquis, has said the problems facing the country continue to grow more serious. Women and girls face strict and wide-ranging restrictions on their freedom and are being deprived of opportunities to get an education, find employment and take part in public life.

He said restrictions on girls' access to education are the most sensitive issue for the country's future: since 2021 more than 2.6 million girls have been deprived of secondary education, while the enrolment rate of women in universities is zero. The return of 6.7 million refugees since 2020 has added further pressure on already strained public services and basic facilities, with a large number deported from Iran and Pakistan, where they had taken refuge. Humanitarian needs in Afghanistan are expected to rise significantly in 2027 because of refugee returns, regional instability and severe El Niño weather, putting food security and access to basic facilities at serious risk.

Lemarquis stressed that returning Afghans need legal documents, protection and, above all, jobs. He also cited a recent United Nations report showing that opium cultivation in the country rose by 330 percent last year, after the Taliban's 2022 ban had sharply reduced it, and said eradication efforts can only work if farmers are given alternative jobs and sources of income. Aid to Afghanistan was two billion dollars in 2023 but fell to one billion dollars in 2025, with only 570 million dollars received so far this year, and there are fears that 40 percent of the country's basic health centres could close during 2025 and 2026.

Lemarquis urged the international community not to leave Afghanistan alone, because the effects of this crisis could spread beyond the country's borders.
