Cummins defiant as 'Sandpapergate' roils Australian cricket

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pat Cummins remained defiant on Thursday as his position as Australia Test captain was called into question after he was dragged back into the Sandpapergate scandal by Cameron Bancroft's explosive revelations. Bancroft, a central figure in the 2018 ball-tampering plot in South Africa that rocked world cricket, claimed in a lengthy newspaper column that the whole team knew what was going on. It came just days after Cummins held a 70-minute press conference to rebut the same allegations by Usman Khawaja in his autobiography, calling them 100 percent wrong.

Bancroft was the player who used sandpaper during a Test in Cape Town, with a Cricket Australia probe concluding only he, then-skipper Steve Smith and his deputy David Warner were responsible. They were all sent home in disgrace and banned. In a statement, Cummins' manager Neil Maxwell said Pat's position had not changed in eight years and that he had been consistent and adamant from day one.

Despite the defiance, calls are mounting for his position to be reviewed, with The Australian newspaper saying his future as Test captain should be placed under immediate review. Hours before Bancroft's revelations, Cricket Australia said it would not reopen its investigation but instead set up a forum for players to share their experiences about 2018. Chief executive Todd Greenberg also declared Cummins' version of events was not under scrutiny.
