Pakistan Reaffirms Solidarity With Kashmiris on Black Day

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 27, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The country's top leadership on Tuesday reaffirmed Pakistan's support for the people of Indian Illegally Occupied Jammu and Kashmir as the country observed Kashmir Black Day to mark the anniversary of the arrival of Indian forces in Srinagar on October 27, 1947. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said the arrival of Indian forces in Srinagar 79 years ago marked the beginning of a tragic chapter in the history of Jammu and Kashmir, and that lasting peace in South Asia could not be achieved without a just resolution in line with United Nations Security Council resolutions and the aspirations of the Kashmiri people. President Asif Ali Zardari said the people of the occupied region had endured decades of conflict, restrictions and denial of their fundamental rights since October 27, 1947.

Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said the Kashmiri people's struggle for self-determination, recognised by the United Nations, remained unfinished, and called on the international community to urge India to end rights violations. PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari said Kashmiris had been waiting for decades for their right to self-determination as recognised in United Nations resolutions. The National Assembly also unanimously passed a resolution reaffirming Pakistan's moral, political and diplomatic support for the Kashmiri people's right to self-determination, strongly condemning extrajudicial killings, arbitrary arrests and detentions, enforced disappearances, custodial torture and restrictions on fundamental freedoms.
