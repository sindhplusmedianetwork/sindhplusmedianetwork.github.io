Politicians blame incitement by Netanyahu for attack on mosque in Israel

Police in Israel said they were investigating a suspected arson attack on a mosque in the Arab village of Dahi in northern Israel on Thursday, which a slate of politicians blamed on incitement by the camp of caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Police said an investigation had been opened into the suspected arson of a room in the lower part of the mosque and the spraying of graffiti on a nearby street, adding that any attempt to harm religious symbols would be taken very seriously. The mosque's imam, Khaled al Zoubi, said he had seen smoke and a fire when he arrived for prayers on Thursday and that security cameras recorded three masked individuals who tried to enter the mosque but failed, then broke the glass of a second-floor window and threw incendiary bottles inside.

Zoubi described the incident as a racist attack but said the damage was not extensive, while photos showed graffiti reading revenge scrawled on the walls of buildings near the mosque. Yair Lapid, a leading member of the anti-Netanyahu bloc, called setting a mosque on fire a heinous hate crime and said the rampant incitement against Arab Israelis must stop. Yair Golan, who heads the left-leaning Democrats party, described the incident as Jewish terrorism and the consequence of the campaign of incitement Netanyahu and his government are waging against Arab citizens of Israel.

Youssef Jabareen, head of the Arab-majority Joint List, visited Dahi and held Netanyahu, his entire cabinet and extremists with him responsible for translating dangerous incitement into reality through attacks.
