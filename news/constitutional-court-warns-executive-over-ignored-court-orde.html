Constitutional Court Warns Executive Over Ignored Court Orders

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Federal Constitutional Court has reminded the executive that compliance with a judicial decision is a constitutional requirement, not a matter of courtesy, amid an ongoing dispute over the non-implementation of superior courts' orders. In a 10-page judgment authored by Justice Ali Baqar Najafi, the court said disregarding or delaying a verdict amounted to challenging the very framework of the legal system. Justice Najafi was part of a three-member bench led by Chief Justice Amin-Ud-Din Khan that heard a case on the implementation of a Supreme Court judgment in the sacked employees' case.

The order said non-compliance not only disturbed the equilibrium between the court's prestige and the enforcement of rights but also set a dangerous precedent that could lead to executive overreach. The court directed government authorities to comply with the detailed judgment in letter and spirit and to consider the petitioners as unconditionally and regularly appointed beneficiary employees. In December 2021, the Supreme Court, exercising suo motu jurisdiction, had restored thousands of government workers sacked after its earlier judgment struck down the Sacked Employees (Reinstatement) Ordinance Act 2010.

The court also noted that after the 27th amendment, the Federal Constitutional Court was the country's apex court entrusted with interpreting the law and upholding the Constitution.
