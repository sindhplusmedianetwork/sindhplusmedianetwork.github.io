Tiny creatures found living inside million-year-old rocks deep in the Pacific

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Scientists have discovered a surprising world deep in the Pacific Ocean, where extremely tiny sea creatures live inside ancient metal-rich rocks. The creatures are less than a millimetre in size and shelter in the fine cracks and empty spaces of rocks that take millions of years to form. According to the research, these rocks are called polymetallic nodules: deep-sea mineral formations that look like ordinary stones but provide an extraordinary habitat for marine life.

The nodules are made mostly of iron and manganese oxides, are usually black, and are about the size of a human fist, yet they grow extremely slowly, adding only a few millimetres in a million years in some cases. The mineral rocks are found about 4,000 to 6,000 metres below the surface of the sea and can remain on the seabed for millions of years. Scientists say most of the deep seabed consists of soft mud, but these mineral rocks provide hard surfaces there, while the fine cracks and empty spaces inside them serve as extra shelter for small sea creatures, and the researchers examined these ancient rocks to learn how important they are in maintaining biodiversity in the depths of the sea.

Dr Tania Stratmann, a deep-sea ecologist and one of the three lead authors of the study, said the research proved that the cracks inside these rocks are large enough to provide shelter to creatures smaller than a millimetre. The results revealed an extraordinary and far more diverse world of marine life than expected: the researchers examined 244 mineral rocks and identified a total of 4,139 individual creatures, an average of 17 per rock, although one rock astonishingly sheltered 245 creatures.
