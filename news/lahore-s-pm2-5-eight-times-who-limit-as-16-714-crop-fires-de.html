Lahore's PM2.5 eight times WHO limit as 16,714 crop fires detected, report says

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 10, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Satellite data detected 16,714 likely crop-residue fires in Punjab between October 1 and November 9, while Lahore's average PM2.5 concentration was eight times the World Health Organisation's guideline value, according to a report by the Pakistan Air Quality Initiative. Lahore's average PM2.5 stood at 126 micrograms per cubic metre during the period, exceeding the 24-hour national environmental quality standards on all 40 days for which valid data were available, though the average was 11 per cent lower than in the corresponding period in 2025. Satellite instruments detected 18,825 fires across Punjab, 89 per cent of which were classified as likely crop-residue burning, with the highest daily count of 1,102 recorded on November 1.

Sheikhupura recorded the highest number of fires at 2,559, followed by Jhang, Okara, Hafizabad and Gujranwala, with the five districts accounting for 54 per cent of detections. The report found PM2.5 levels rose on days when air passed over burning areas, reaching 175 micrograms per cubic metre in Lahore compared with 93 on other days, though it cautioned that the link did not establish the precise contribution of crop burning. The assessment also examined Diwali fireworks in India and found the evidence did not support attributing Lahore's poor air quality after Diwali to fireworks across the border.

Meanwhile, the Punjab Environment Protection and Climate Change Department reported an improvement in Lahore's air quality on Monday, with the city's average air quality index falling from 489 at 9am to 232 by 3pm, and the Provincial Disaster Management Authority forecast rain in most Punjab districts over the next two days.
