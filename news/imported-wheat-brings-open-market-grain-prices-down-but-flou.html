Imported wheat brings open-market grain prices down, but flour and roti stay dear

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 03, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

KARACHI: Imported wheat has begun cooling the open market, but the relief has not reached consumers' kitchens. When the first ship carrying 51,000 tonnes berthed on October 28, wheat prices plunged to Rs130 from Rs140 per kg, and a second ship with 58,132 tonnes was due at Karachi on Tuesday. Millers cut mill prices of flour No 2.5, maida and fine flour to Rs156, Rs168 and Rs171 per kg ahead of the arrivals, down from Rs161, Rs173 and Rs176 in early October.

Yet retail rates stayed put: flour No 2.5 sells at Rs170–180 and maida at Rs185–190 per kg, while chapati costs Rs20 and naan Rs30–35. Tandoor owners blame costly LPG at Rs500–550 per kg for keeping roti prices high. Karachi and Hyderabad pay the highest flour prices in the country, with a 20kg bag reaching Rs3,540 in Karachi against a national average of Rs3,200.

Wholesaler Rauf Ibrahim urged the government to let the private sector and millers import grain, warning that 750,000 tonnes of planned imports will fall short of the over two-million-tonne shortfall until Sindh and Punjab harvests arrive between March and May.
