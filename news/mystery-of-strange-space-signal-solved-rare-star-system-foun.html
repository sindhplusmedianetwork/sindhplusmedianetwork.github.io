Mystery of strange space signal solved: rare star system found pulsing every eight minutes

Astronomers have traced a mysterious radio signal that appears like a heartbeat every eight minutes to a rare pair of stars thousands of light-years from Earth. Experts at Australia's Curtin University and the International Centre for Radio Astronomy Research announced on Friday that the short bursts of radio waves were coming from an unusual pair of stars. The pair includes a white dwarf star, the dead core of a once-active star, which completes one rotation on its axis every eight minutes, alongside a G-type star that resembles our Sun and is generally called a yellow dwarf.

Scientists say it is the first time such radio signals have been found coming from this kind of star pair, as previously such signals were generally seen in systems of small and relatively cool red dwarf stars, among the smallest and coolest stars in the universe. Researcher Dr Sam McSweeney said it was the first time the signals had been seen so clearly that the white dwarf could also be observed orbiting its companion star, and that the observation showed the radio signals were coming from a place close to the white dwarf or possibly from the white dwarf itself. Astronomers say the discovery could prove an important step forward in understanding unusual star systems in space and the mysterious radio signals coming from them.
