Hours of TV watching may raise kidney disease risk, study finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A study suggests that people who spend hours sitting in front of the television may face a higher risk of kidney disease, and researchers say it also increases the risk of type 2 diabetes. Researchers found that spending more time watching television each day was linked to a faster decline in kidney function over more than a decade. Uncontrolled diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes of declining kidney function, although the risk also rises significantly with age.

While kidney disease can be treated, it becomes incurable once it reaches its final stages. Researchers from Swinburne University of Technology in Australia and the University of Leicester and Cambridge University in Britain say that staying away from physical activity and sitting continuously is a new risk factor for kidney disease. They studied more than 6,200 Australians aged over 25, tracking their kidney function for 12 years and asking about their television, video and DVD viewing on weekdays and weekends.

Over the 12 years, average daily television viewing rose from one hour 46 minutes to one hour 55 minutes, and those who watched the most television each day saw their kidney function decline fastest on average. People who watched television for four or more hours a day faced the highest risk, and those with both heavy television viewing and type 2 diabetes saw the steepest decline. The researchers said they hope the findings will strengthen the evidence on the harmful effects of sitting for too long on kidney health, especially for people with type 2 diabetes or those who are physically inactive, and that the study highlights the importance of following physical activity guidelines.
