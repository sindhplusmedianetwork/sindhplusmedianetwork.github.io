No Clear Winner After Tight New Zealand General Election

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

New Zealand's general election on Saturday produced no clear winner, with the country's two major parties preparing for negotiations to form a coalition government. Preliminary results showed Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's centre-right National Party as the largest party with 42 seats, but 19 short of the simple majority needed to govern alone. Together with its coalition partners ACT, on 13 seats, and New Zealand First, on 12, the conservative bloc would hold 66 seats in the 129-seat parliament, just one more than the 65 needed for a majority.

Opposition leader Chris Hipkins, whose Labour Party won 36 seats, showed no signs of conceding, saying the shape of the next government would remain uncertain for a while. The Greens won 16 seats, while the centrist Opportunity Party crossed the five percent threshold to enter parliament for the first time with seven seats, and its leader Qiulae Wong said she was open to working with either side. National and Labour were neck and neck in the party vote, separated by only 0.02 percentage points, and the Electoral Commission is not due to announce the official result until November 27.

Luxon said he would run a caretaker government until the final results are confirmed.
