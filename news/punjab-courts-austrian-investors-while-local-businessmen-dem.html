Punjab Courts Austrian Investors While Local Businessmen Demand Equal Treatment

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

As Punjab rolls out the red carpet for Chinese, Austrian and other foreign investors with long-term land leases and tax breaks, local businessmen are asking a pointed question: will they get the same facilitation? The issue came into focus after Austrian Ambassador Wolfgang Oliver Kutschera met Punjab Minister for Industries and Commerce Chaudhry Shafay Hussain in Lahore on Friday, calling for "more effective steps to increase bilateral economic cooperation" and signalling Austrian interest in setting up small hydroelectric power projects in the province. The minister told the ambassador that Austria's hydropower expertise was welcome, that small dams could be completed in two to three years, and that infrastructure in Punjab's Special Economic Zones had been built to international standards — inviting Austrian firms to explore agro-based value addition, seed development, dairy joint ventures and wastewater treatment.

Local industrialists welcomed the foreign interest but said it should not stop at the border: "We are happy to see Austrian, Chinese and other investors coming, but a local manufacturer who has paid taxes for 20 years should not be forced to run between five offices while the foreign company gets the red carpet treatment," said a Lahore-based industrialist. He added that facilitation should depend on the project and the jobs it created, not on the passport of the investor — the same incentives, the same speed in approvals and the same certainty that policy will not change. The complaint is not new: in earlier consultative sessions at the Lahore Chamber of Commerce and Industry, office-bearers said foreign investment could not be encouraged without promoting local investment first, while the Board of Investment maintains that the government treats both equally.

The government says investors in Punjab's SEZs — domestic and international alike — are offered a 10-year income tax holiday and one-time duty-free import of machinery, but businessmen say the real test is whether a small or medium-sized local firm can access those benefits as easily as a large foreign company. The stakes are visible in the trade numbers: UN Comtrade data shows Austria bought about $428.52 million of Pakistani goods in 2025, mostly clothing and textiles — up 20 per cent — while Pakistan imported $115.73 million of Austrian goods, with Austrian investment in energy, dairy and agriculture still small compared with the textile trade.
