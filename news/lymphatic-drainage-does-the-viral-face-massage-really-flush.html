Lymphatic Drainage: Does the Viral Face Massage Really Flush Toxins From the Body?

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Social media is full of videos promoting facial care with tools made of semi-precious stones, including rollers and flat gua sha stones, massaged slowly over the cheeks and jawline, with creators claiming the practice stimulates lymphatic drainage, reduces facial swelling, removes toxins from the skin, softens fine wrinkles and makes the face look slimmer. The lymphatic system itself is a real and important drainage network made up of lymph fluid, lymph vessels, bone marrow, the thymus gland, lymph nodes, the spleen, tonsils and lymphatic tissue in the small intestine. Professor Neil Piller of Flinders University in Australia, who has researched the system for almost five decades, describes it as a sewage or cleaning system that removes waste, large particles and bacteria from the body and carries them to the lymph nodes, where the immune system is activated.

Associate Professor Hiroo Suami says the system has three main jobs: trapping germs and foreign matter and carrying them to the lymph nodes, absorbing fats and some nutrients from the small intestine, and collecting excess fluid that leaks from tiny blood vessels into the tissues, filtering it and returning it safely to the blood. When lymph fluid collects and cannot drain properly, it causes a chronic condition called lymphoedema, marked by swelling especially in the arms and legs, which can follow cancer surgery or radiotherapy, infection or inflammation, and Britain's National Health Service treats it with compression bandages, a special massage called manual lymphatic drainage, exercise and skin care. Experts warn that most commercial detox claims lack strong scientific evidence, since the body already has organs and systems, including the kidneys, liver, lungs and immune system, that remove waste.

Professor Piller says manual lymphatic drainage may have some benefits but few high-quality studies prove the benefits come directly from the technique, while Professor Suami says healthy people with swelling can try self-drainage only after learning the correct structure and pathways of the lymphatic system. The experts' conclusion is that facial massage with gua sha tools may bring temporary improvement or less swelling, but the most effective way to keep the lymphatic system healthy is a healthy lifestyle: good sleep, a balanced diet, regular exercise and proper breathing.
