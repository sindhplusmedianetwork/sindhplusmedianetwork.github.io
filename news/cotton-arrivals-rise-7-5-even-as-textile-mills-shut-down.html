Cotton Arrivals Rise 7.5% Even as Textile Mills Shut Down

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 19, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Cotton arrivals at ginning factories rose 7.53 per cent year-on-year to 4,081,265 bales by October 15, up 285,628 bales from a year earlier, according to Pakistan Cotton Ginners Association data. Punjab recorded 1.64 million bales, up 7.79 per cent, while Sindh rose 7.35 per cent to 2.44 million bales; Bahawalnagar led Punjab with 418,750 bales and Sanghar topped Sindh with 1.14 million bales. The industry blames rising production costs and alleged misuse of the Export Facilitation Scheme, under which imported yarn and fabric is reportedly sold in the domestic market instead of being re-exported.

Cotton Ginners Forum Chairman Ihsanul Haq estimated that 40 to 50 million metres of fabric were imported from China alone under the scheme. Aptma figures show man-made yarn imports rising from 3,056 tonnes to 12,773 tonnes and man-made fabric imports from 660 tonnes to 8,628 tonnes in the first quarter. About 471 ginning factories were operational; textile mills purchased around 3.45 million bales and exporters 97,000 bales, leaving 531,530 bales unsold.

CCRI Multan's Sajid Mahmood warned that if cotton keeps offering lower returns than competing crops, farmers could reduce acreage next season.
