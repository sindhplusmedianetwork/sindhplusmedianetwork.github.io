Nine Rare Vultures Spotted in Rajanpur, Raising Hopes of Their Return

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 11, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

At least nine rare vultures belonging to two species have been spotted in the tribal area of Rojhan in Rajanpur district. The sighting has raised hopes of their return to parts of South Punjab. According to wildlife officials, the birds belong to the Himalayan griffon and cinereous vulture species.

Wildlife rangers have begun monitoring their movement and stay in the area to ensure their safety and assess the conditions of their natural habitat. The Punjab Wildlife and Parks Department said sightings of different vulture species had been reported intermittently from various parts of the region over the past year. Experts say one of the major reasons behind the sharp decline in the vulture population in Punjab is the use of a drug administered to livestock, as vultures feeding on carcasses of treated animals suffered kidney failure.

The government had banned the veterinary use of diclofenac in 2006, although other toxic drugs, contaminated food sources and habitat loss continue to pose threats. A vulture breeding centre is operating in the Changa Manga forest to support conservation, focusing on the captive breeding of white-rumped vultures with the aim of increasing their population and reintroducing them into their habitats.
