Lahore High Court Rules Divorce-Linked Quran Oath Offer Unlawful

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Lahore High Court has held that an offer to settle a civil dispute through a special oath on the Holy Quran, coupled with a condition of divorce if the oath is false, is not a lawful offer. Justice Anwaar Hussain issued the ruling while allowing a civil revision filed by Muhammad Azam Khan against Akhtar Khan and others. During a pending appeal, the petitioner had offered in August 2023 to have the matter decided by an oath on the Holy Quran taken by the opposing side, with a condition that the oath-taker's wife would stand divorced if the oath proved false.

On August 22, the respondents appeared before the appellate court and expressed willingness to take the oath, while the petitioner said he did not wish to proceed and formally applied to withdraw the offer on August 24. The appellate court dismissed his withdrawal application in September 2023, ruling that the offer had been accepted, and directed that the oath be taken on the Holy Quran alone. Justice Hussain held that the appellate court had erred, noting that a special oath is governed by the Oaths Act, 1873, under which only oaths that are not repugnant to justice or decency and do not affect any third person are permitted.

He ruled that a condition tying a penalty to the oath-taker's wife's divorce violated both limits, and that a court cannot strike out an objectionable condition and enforce the rest against a party who never assented to it. The judge set aside the appellate court's order and directed it to decide the appeal on its merits without treating the proposed oath as binding on the petitioner.
