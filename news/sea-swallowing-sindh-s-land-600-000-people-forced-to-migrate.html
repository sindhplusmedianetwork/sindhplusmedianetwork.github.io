Sea swallowing Sindh's land: 600,000 people forced to migrate from Indus Delta

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Sea intrusion and a shortage of fresh water in the Indus River have put the survival of the Indus Delta at risk. About 600,000 people have migrated from their ancestral areas as the land around them has become unliveable. Farmland in the region has turned barren, and fishing opportunities, on which many families depend, have become limited.

Figures from the Fishermen's Cooperative Society show that a large part of the delta has been lost to the sea since the 1980s and 1990s. The Sindh Forest Department says more than 300,000 acres of mangrove forests have been restored along the coast over the past 15 years. The restoration projects are supervised by Shehzad Sadiq, the District Forest Officer of the Coastal Forest Division in Karachi.

Experts say mangrove restoration alone is not enough: the Indus's fresh water must reach the sea, and its required flow must be ensured, to stop coastal erosion and protect the livelihoods of the people living there.
