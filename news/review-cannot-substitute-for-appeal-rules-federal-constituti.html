Review Cannot Substitute for Appeal, Rules Federal Constitutional Court

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Federal Constitutional Court has dismissed more than 395 review petitions challenging its earlier judgement upholding the constitutional validity of the super tax, ruling that review jurisdiction cannot be used as a substitute for an appeal. A three-judge bench, headed by the chief justice, had reserved its ruling on Nov 2 on review petitions challenging the court's Jan 27, 2026, judgement upholding the super tax under Sections 4B and 4C of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001. The detailed reasons left intact the earlier judgement, including the applicability of Sections 4B and 4C from the dates on which the respective provisions were introduced.

The review petitions were filed by major banks, textile mills, fertiliser companies, pharmaceutical firms, oil and gas companies, cement manufacturers, power producers and individual taxpayers. The judgement explained that mere disagreement with the reasoning of a judgement affords no ground for review, and the jurisdiction cannot be employed as a disguised appeal or an instrument for rehearing a matter on its merits. The court also ruled that following the Constitution 27th Amendment Act, 2025, the FCC exercises independent constitutional jurisdiction, and Supreme Court decisions, while persuasive, were not binding on it.

Dismissing the petitions, the court held that no error apparent on the face of the record or other legally cognisable infirmity had been demonstrated to justify review.
