Brick kilns fail to fully adopt zigzag technology as smog season bites

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Brick kilns across Punjab have failed to fully adopt zigzag technology despite a government mandate, keeping one of the province's major sources of air pollution active as the smog season sets in. The Punjab government made it mandatory in 2017 for brick kilns to shift from traditional firing methods to zigzag technology, and the Environmental Protection Agency later claimed the transition had been completed by 2021. However, ground observations in the agricultural districts of south Punjab, including Khanewal, Vehari and Multan, show that most kilns have not implemented the technology in its complete form and instead run on a hybrid mix of traditional practices and partial zigzag adjustments.

Around 9,041 brick kilns are operational across Punjab, and researchers say kilns in kiln-dense districts contribute between 20 and 30 per cent of black carbon pollution during peak winter months. Scientific studies suggest properly run zigzag kilns can cut overall carbon emissions by up to 30 per cent, black carbon by as much as 80 per cent and fuel use by nearly 30 per cent. With the smog season running from October to December, the provincial government has decided to keep brick kilns across Punjab closed from November 15 to December 15, while enforcement teams continue to fine and seal non-compliant kilns.
