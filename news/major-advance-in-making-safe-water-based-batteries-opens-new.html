Major advance in making safe water-based batteries opens new energy storage possibilities

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 15, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

New research published in the scientific journal Nature has improved aqueous, or water-based, batteries, which are seen as a safer and more environmentally friendly alternative to lithium-ion batteries for large-scale energy storage. A team led by University College London developed a new electrolyte chemistry that lets iodine transfer four electrons instead of two during charging and discharging. This increases the amount of energy the battery can store.

Tests showed the zinc-iodine batteries retained almost all of their energy storage capacity after thousands of charge and discharge cycles. The researchers say the technology could also be adapted for aluminium-iodine batteries. Water-based batteries do not catch fire the way lithium-ion batteries can, making them attractive for storing energy from solar and wind power.

The team believes the advance brings large-scale, low-cost energy storage a step closer.
