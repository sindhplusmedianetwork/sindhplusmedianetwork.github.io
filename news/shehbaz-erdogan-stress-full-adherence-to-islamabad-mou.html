Shehbaz, Erdogan Stress Full Adherence to Islamabad MoU

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif held a bilateral meeting with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan on Thursday on the sidelines of the COP31 Leaders Summit in Antalya. The two leaders reaffirmed the deep-rooted, historic and brotherly ties between Pakistan and Turkiye and expressed satisfaction at the positive direction of bilateral relations, according to a Prime Minister's Office statement. Their talks covered trade and investment, defence cooperation, energy and connectivity, and both sides stressed speeding up work on ongoing initiatives and finding new avenues for economic engagement.

Prime Minister Shehbaz highlighted the efforts of regional countries, particularly members of the Makkah Accord, to promote de-escalation and dialogue, while President Erdogan appreciated Pakistan's constructive role and mediation efforts that led to the signing of the Islamabad MoU. The two leaders underscored that all parties must adhere to their commitments under the agreement to ensure durable peace in the wider region, and they reaffirmed mutual support on issues of core national interest. In his national statement at the summit, the prime minister called for grant-based climate financing, including public-private partnerships, and a significant increase in resilience and adaptation funds for climate-vulnerable countries.

Recalling the devastating floods of 2022, when one-third of Pakistan was submerged, more than 1,700 people lost their lives and losses exceeded 30 billion dollars, he said vulnerable nations needed timely help that did not add to their debt burden. He also noted that Pakistan had added more than 20 gigawatts of distributed solar capacity in two years, that clean sources now made up nearly 55 per cent of the energy mix, and that the country aimed to cut projected emissions by up to 50 per cent by 2035.
