Israel's double-envelope votes could still shift election seats

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 29, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Seats in Israel's parliament could still change after the initial election results, because a large number of votes cast under the double-envelope system have yet to be counted. Under the system, soldiers, reservists, police employees, prisoners, hospital patients and diplomats posted abroad are allowed to vote away from their assigned polling station. Their votes are collected in double envelopes: the voter places a secret ballot in an inner envelope, which is sealed inside a second envelope carrying personal details.

Election officials first use the outer envelope to verify that the voter has not already voted elsewhere, and only then is the inner envelope separated and the vote counted, keeping the voter's identity and choice secret. The number of such votes is expected to be higher this time because of the large numbers of military personnel and displaced citizens. Some reports have put their total at a level equivalent to about 15 parliamentary seats, although that does not mean 15 seats would go to a single party.

The final count could still alter the distribution of seats, particularly for parties close to the electoral threshold, and could therefore affect the arithmetic for forming a majority government.
