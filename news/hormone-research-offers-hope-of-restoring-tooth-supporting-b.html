Hormone Research Offers Hope of Restoring Tooth-Supporting Bone Damaged by Diabetes

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Type 2 diabetes affects not only various organs of the body but also the health of teeth and the mouth, and can make teeth weak and loose so that they may fall out. Research published on 29 October 2026 in the scientific journal International Journal of Oral Science examined the possibility of restoring damaged bone through specific hormones. Researchers at Indiana University and the University of Arkansas studied the effects of parathyroid hormone, known as PTH, and abaloparatide, known as ABL.

According to the research, diabetes can damage the alveolar bone that holds the teeth to the jawbone, and diabetes patients also face a higher risk of periodontitis, the chronic inflammation of the gums. For the study, the researchers conducted experiments on mice made diabetic through a high-fat diet and streptozotocin, examining new bone formation and the breakdown of old bone through advanced scanning and tissue analysis. The study also used genetically modified mice to examine the cells that play an important role in bone restoration.

Earlier research had already shown the possibility of bone growth and gum restoration through these hormones, but information about their effects and working methods in diabetes patients was limited. The research may open new paths for the possible treatment of dental and jawbone problems in diabetes patients, but since these experiments were carried out on mice, further research is needed to prove the hormones' effectiveness and safety in humans.
