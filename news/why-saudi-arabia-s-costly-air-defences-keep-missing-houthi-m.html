Why Saudi Arabia's Costly Air Defences Keep Missing Houthi Missiles

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Saudi Arabia's multi-billion-dollar air defence network is facing hard questions after repeated Houthi missile and drone strikes penetrated its shield, including the deadly October 10 attack on Riyadh airport. The kingdom operates American-made Patriot batteries and the THAAD system, with its first THAAD battery becoming operational only in July 2025. Experts say the Houthis are overwhelming defences by combining ballistic missiles, low-flying cruise missiles and long-range drones in simultaneous salvos.

Cruise missiles skim close to the ground, making them harder to detect, while the sheer volume of incoming weapons forces defenders into difficult choices about which threats to engage. Analysts also point to interceptor depletion as a credible explanation, noting that both Saudi Arabia and the United States have burned through expensive Patriot and THAAD interceptors in recent conflicts. The cost imbalance is stark: a single THAAD interceptor costs around $12 million against Houthi missiles estimated at about $1 million each.

Despite spending roughly $83 billion on defence in 2025, according to Forbes analysis, Riyadh is struggling to protect critical infrastructure from an adversary using Iranian-assisted weapons like the Hatem-2 and Palestine-2 missiles.
