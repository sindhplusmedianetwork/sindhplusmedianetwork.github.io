Part of London's Oxford Street Goes Traffic-Free for Good

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 26, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A stretch of London's iconic Oxford Street was made traffic-free permanently from Monday, as pedestrians took over one of Europe's busiest shopping streets without the capital's red double-decker buses and black cabs. Mayor Sadiq Khan said he wanted about a kilometre of the street, almost half of it, to remain traffic-free forever, arguing that pedestrianisation would bring in more shoppers. Shoppers on the street on a rainy Monday backed the change, saying it gave people more room to explore the shops and walk without dodging traffic.

The street brings in about 3.1 billion pounds in spending each year, but it has faced decline over the past two decades, with shops closing during the Covid-19 pandemic and footfall falling as shopping moved online. The scheme has proved controversial among residents worried about traffic in neighbouring areas and among disability rights activists, while black cab drivers called the move crazy, saying people with disabilities would have to walk around the back of stores to reach a taxi. Khan insisted that cab ranks and bus stops near the street would keep the area accessible, and said the move would revitalise the road and make the capital greener.

Around half the street has now been pedestrianised, with plans to close the rest to vehicles depending on the success of the first phase.
