Seven UAE beaches reopened after oil spill

Seven beaches in the UAE emirate of Ras Al Khaimah have been reopened nearly three weeks after they were closed as a precaution following an oil spill at sea. The Ras Al Khaimah Public Services Department announced on Wednesday that access to public beaches and beaches at prominent tourist resorts had been restored, while restrictions on other beaches in the emirate remain in place. The four public beaches reopened are Flamingo Beach, Dahan Beach, Al Mairid Beach and Al Rams Corniche, and the three resort beaches are at Anantara Mina Al Arab, InterContinental Mina Al Arab Resort and Spa, and The Cove Rotana Resort.

Authorities had announced on 24 October that all beaches in the emirate would be closed indefinitely after reports of an oil spill from a tanker near the UAE's northern coastline, and the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment confirmed on 23 October that oil was leaking into the sea from a tanker near the coast of Umm Al Quwain. Emirati authorities announced on 30 October that further oil leakage from the tanker had been stopped and that marine pollution had decreased significantly, although cleanup operations, environmental assessments and monitoring continued. No date has yet been set for reopening the remaining beaches, and residents and tourists have been advised to visit only locations officially declared open and to follow the safety instructions of the relevant authorities.
