FCC Reasserts Itself as Apex Court, Says Supreme Court Judgments Not Binding

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Federal Constitutional Court has once again asserted its position at the top of Pakistan's judicial hierarchy, ruling that Supreme Court judgments are not binding on it. In an eight-page judgment authored by Justice Aamer Farooq, a three-member bench said the court's review jurisdiction is extremely limited and confined to errors apparent on the face of the record. The bench held that Supreme Court jurisprudence carries only "persuasive value" and may be followed where it appears consistent with the law and the Constitution.

It also ruled that Supreme Court precedents on leave to appeal against high court decisions under Article 199 are not binding on the FCC, though wisdom can be drawn from them. The judgment warned litigants and lawyers against treating review petitions as a second chance to argue a case, noting that frivolous or vexatious applications can attract costs of at least Rs25,000. It also made a Rs50,000 security deposit mandatory for hearing a review petition, which can be forfeited if the application is dismissed.

The ruling elaborated the court's appellate jurisdiction under Article 175F(1)(c) of the Constitution, introduced through the 27th Constitutional Amendment in 2025. The bench clarified that a petition for leave to appeal is not an appeal as of right and only acquires that status once leave is granted.
