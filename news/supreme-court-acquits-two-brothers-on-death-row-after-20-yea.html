Supreme Court Acquits Two Brothers on Death Row After 20 Years, Citing Mental Illness

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Supreme Court has acquitted two brothers who had spent around 20 years on death row in a blasphemy case, on account of their mental illness. Reports of an eight- and nine-member Board of Certification, dated August 31, 2023 and September 18, 2025, established that the appellants suffer from bipolar disorder. The seven-page judgement, authored by Justice Ishtiaq Ibrahim, said the disorder deprives a person of the capacity to understand the nature or lawfulness of their actions during active manic episodes.

Justice Ibrahim was part of a three-member bench led by Chief Justice of Pakistan Yahya Afridi, which heard the brothers' appeals and set aside their death sentences. The verdict said the expert opinion conclusively established that bipolar disorder is episodic in nature, making it highly probable that the appellants were in an active manic episode when the alleged offence was committed. The court noted that both brothers were independently diagnosed with the exact same psychiatric condition, which it said provides compelling scientific corroboration that their mental unsoundness was an inherited, pre-existing illness.

The bench also expected the executive director of the Punjab Institute of Mental Health to ensure that the appellants' medical treatment is not discontinued. The court said that since the appellants have spent around twenty years behind bars and the medical board's opinion raises doubts about their culpability, the benefit of doubt cannot be withheld.
