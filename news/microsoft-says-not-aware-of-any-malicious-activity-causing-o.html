Microsoft says 'not aware of any malicious activity' causing Outlook outage

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read

Microsoft said it was not aware of any malicious activity behind the hours-long disruption of its Outlook services, which hit businesses and government agencies globally. In a statement, the company said it would continue to treat the incident with urgency and priority until full resolution, adding that efforts to improve serviceability were not progressing as expected. Microsoft services such as Outlook and Teams were disrupted for about 10 hours on Friday as the company struggled to fix issues with some servers.

Downdetector, which tracks outages by collating status reports, showed more than 25,000 reports of Outlook problems in the United States at 9:30pm Eastern Time, and at its peak more than 130,000 reports around 1pm Eastern Time on Thursday. In a service health notice, Microsoft said features of Microsoft 365 services that depended on Exchange Online and email were affected, while elements that did not rely on Exchange Online remained functional. Australian Home Affairs and Cyber Security Minister Tony Burke said Microsoft believed it was not dealing with a cyberattack, and that the situation was in some ways similar to the CrowdStrike malfunction of 2024, with an update gone awry.

Australia's spy agency and federal police were among the organisations caught up in the outage, while the US House of Representatives said the outage impacted its users and its engineers were working with Microsoft.
