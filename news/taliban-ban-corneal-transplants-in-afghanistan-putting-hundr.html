Taliban Ban Corneal Transplants in Afghanistan, Putting Hundreds at Risk of Vision Loss

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Afghanistan's Taliban authorities have banned corneal transplant operations, ruling that the procedure is not permitted under Islamic teachings and verbally instructing surgeons to stop performing it. Doctors who spoke to The Telegraph said the ban leaves hundreds of patients at risk of permanent vision loss and has halted a programme that recently trained local doctors to restore sight in Kabul and Jalalabad. A hospital in Kabul had performed 90 corneal transplant operations in recent months before the order brought the programme to a halt, and its waiting list consists mainly of young people who lost their eyesight through trauma, injuries, explosions and welding-related accidents.

Doctors say Afghanistan already faces a shortage of specialist eye doctors and locally available corneas, making treatment difficult to access. The United States-based charity APPNA, which provided free corneas and supported training for doctors in Kabul and Jalalabad, has urged the Taliban authorities to reverse the decision and allow corneal transplantation to resume.
