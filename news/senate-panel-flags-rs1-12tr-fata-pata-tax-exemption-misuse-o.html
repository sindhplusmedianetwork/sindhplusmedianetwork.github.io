Senate panel flags Rs1.12tr FATA/PATA tax exemption misuse, orders recovery drive

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 03, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A Senate Sub-Committee on Interior and Anti-Narcotics has expressed serious concern over the alleged misuse of the FATA/PATA tax exemption scheme, delays in recovering billions of rupees in outstanding taxes and the failure of several companies to provide required records. The panel met under the convenorship of Saifullah Abro at the Parliament House, where officials informed the committee that imported goods worth approximately Rs1,120 billion under the FATA/PATA tax exemption scheme were under scrutiny. Consumption certificates and related records had been identified for goods worth around Rs389 billion, while further details were required regarding the remaining imports, their utilisation and outstanding tax liabilities; 223 consumption certificates had been issued to 108 companies.

Senator Umar Farooq and officials from the Interior Ministry, the Federal Board of Revenue, the Federal Investigation Agency, the police and other departments attended the meeting and briefed the committee. Abro directed the FBR, FIA and other relevant institutions to submit complete documents, determine potential revenue losses and establish responsibility for any violations of the law, questioning why complete information about imports, factory production, utilisation of goods and tax payments was unavailable for all companies. He questioned the FBR's performance, saying Pakistan was seeking loans from international financial institutions while billions of rupees in alleged tax dues remained unrecovered, and declared that public money would be recovered at all costs with no compromise in protecting national resources.

He said that if investigations established tax evasion or financial irregularities, those responsible must be identified, legal action initiated and outstanding amounts deposited into the national exchequer, directing officials to prepare a separate case file with import records, customs documents, consumption certificates, tax payments and bank statements. He also sought clarification about the legal basis concerning approximately Rs85 billion and the requirements fulfilled before the release of imported goods, with officials explaining that companies were required to deposit an amount equivalent to the value of imported goods with customs authorities before release to factories.
