Arshad Nadeem's Early Coach Disappointed by Star's Decline, Still Hopeful

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Fayyaz Ahmed Bukhari, the early coach of Olympic javelin champion Arshad Nadeem, says he is deeply disappointed by his former student's poor performances, but remains hopeful that with determination and modern training, Arshad can win more honours for Pakistan. Bukhari, who serves as a technical coach at Pakistan Wapda, was speaking during the 54th National Athletics Championship at NED University Karachi, where he recalled that Arshad, born on 2 January 1997 in Mian Channu, Khanewal, to a mason's family, first played cricket, football and kabaddi before Bukhari recognised his hidden talent and guided him into javelin throw. His rise began with a bronze medal at the 2016 South Asian Games in Guwahati with a 55.33-metre throw, followed by a bronze at the 2018 Asian Games in Jakarta with a new national record of 80.75 metres, a fifth-place finish at the Tokyo Olympics with 84.62 metres, gold at the 2022 Commonwealth Games in Birmingham with 90.18 metres as the first Asian to do so, and gold at the Paris Olympics 2024 with 92.97 metres, setting a 118-year Olympic record and winning Pakistan's first individual gold medal.

Bukhari said injuries, including elbow pain and knee and shoulder problems, have broken Arshad's consistency, and recent results, including ninth place at the Commonwealth Games in Glasgow, fifth at the World Championships in Budapest with 81.49 metres, fourth at the Asian Games in Japan with 80.29 metres, and withdrawal from the recent national championship in Karachi, show the decline. He warned that if Arshad, at just 29, is forced to quit the sport, it would be a great tragedy, but with the right guidance he has the ability to perform for Pakistan for five more years. He added that Arshad must stay away from politics in sport and that the honours won in sport are a greater wealth than money.
