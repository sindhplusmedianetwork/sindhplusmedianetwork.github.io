Saudi Arabia rejects WSJ report on Houthi demands for $51bn and recognition

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 06, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Saudi Arabia on Thursday categorically denied a Wall Street Journal report claiming that Yemen's Houthi militia had demanded 51 billion dollars and formal recognition as the country's rulers in exchange for ending hostilities with the kingdom. In a post on X, Deputy Minister for Public Diplomacy Rayed Krimly said the report had no basis in truth, describing the claims as fabrications by the newspaper's writers or their alleged sources. Krimly accused the Houthis of rebelling against Yemen's government, contributing to the starvation of its people and dragging the country into conflicts and agendas that did not serve its interests.

He also accused the militia of attacking commercial shipping in the Red Sea, as well as civilian and economic facilities, and rejecting peace efforts. According to the Wall Street Journal, the Iran-backed Houthis had demanded the money to cover the cost of years of Saudi strikes and a prolonged blockade, and had sought Saudi Arabia's recognition as Yemen's legitimate rulers. The newspaper reported that the demands emerged after Saudi Arabia approached the Houthis to explore a negotiated end to the conflict following attacks on Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea and the group's seizure of territory overlooking the Bab Al Mandab Strait.

The development comes amid escalating hostilities between the Houthis and the Saudi-led coalition, which supports Yemen's internationally recognised government.
