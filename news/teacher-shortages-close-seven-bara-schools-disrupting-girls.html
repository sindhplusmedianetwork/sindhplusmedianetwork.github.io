Teacher shortages close seven Bara schools, disrupting girls' education

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A severe shortage of teachers, compounded by security concerns, has forced the closure of seven government girls' primary schools in Bara, Khyber district, according to figures from the Sub-Divisional Education Office (Female). Of Bara's 142 government girls' primary schools, four are closed for lack of teachers and three because of the law-and-order situation, with 101 teaching posts and 34 non-teaching positions lying vacant across the sub-division. Of the 310 sanctioned teaching posts, only 209 are filled: primary school teacher positions stand at 81 filled against 145 sanctioned, and head teacher posts at 106 against 143 sanctioned, while all 22 trained teacher posts are occupied.

Sub-Divisional Education Officer (Female) Shakeela Hussain said rising enrolments had deepened the crisis, with most schools having only one or two permanent female teachers yet some enrolling as many as 500 girls. Through the Parent-Teacher Council, 282 local candidates have been selected against 300 female teacher posts and will serve until the end of the current academic session, and a request has been submitted for 333 additional PTC teachers. Government girls' primary schools in Bara currently have 24,500 students, with admissions continuing until December, and officials say permanent recruitment through the Educational Testing and Evaluation Agency remains essential.

The temporary education office itself, housed in a single room inside the Bara Tehsil Building without a washroom for women, has prompted local elders and parents to urge the governor to shift it to the Municipal Building for the convenience of women teachers and parents.
