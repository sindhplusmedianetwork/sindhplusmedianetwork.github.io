Balance of Payments Swings to $4.39 Billion Deficit in September

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's overall balance of payments swung to a deficit of $4.39 billion in September, compared with just $118 million in August, as the current account deficit widened to $356 million from $73 million, according to State Bank of Pakistan data. For the July-September quarter, the balance of payments recorded a deficit of $3.12 billion, against a surplus of $274 million in the same period last year. The figures highlight the growing gap between the country's foreign exchange earnings and payments, with the merchandise trade deficit remaining the largest component of the imbalance.

Pakistan's goods trade deficit widened 21.1% to $9.33 billion in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026-27, as imports grew nearly twice as fast as exports. Goods imports increased 14.3% to $17.78 billion during July-September, while exports rose 7.7% to $8.45 billion, and the current account recorded a deficit of $874 million for the quarter against $755 million a year earlier. In September alone, exports jumped 15.5% to $3.02 billion, reversing a decline in August, but imports grew faster still, rising 22.7% to $6.17 billion and pushing the monthly goods trade deficit to $3.16 billion.

On a brighter note, foreign direct investment increased to $720 million in the quarter, up 20% from $600 million a year earlier, though monthly inflows slowed to $219 million in September from $322 million in August.
