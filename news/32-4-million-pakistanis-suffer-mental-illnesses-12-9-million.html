32.4 Million Pakistanis Suffer Mental Illnesses, 12.9 Million Have Hepatitis: Health Ministry

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 31, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Islamabad — The Ministry of National Health has revealed that 32.4 million Pakistanis suffer from mental illnesses and 12.9 million from hepatitis B or C, in data presented to the National Assembly. According to the report, the number of mental-illness patients has risen from 30 million in 2019 to 32.4 million in 2023 — an increase of 2.4 million in five years. More than 300,000 residents of Islamabad alone suffer from mental and psychological problems, and the country faces an acute shortage of psychiatrists: only 1,050 are registered at the national level, meaning just one psychiatrist for over 245,000 citizens.

The ministry told the House that Pakistan now ranks first in the world in the number of hepatitis C patients and is among the top 10 countries for deaths caused by hepatitis C. Citing World Health Organization figures, the report said hepatitis C patients in Pakistan have reached 9 million, while more than 3.86 million suffer from hepatitis B — a combined 12.9 million. Around 48,000 people die every year in Pakistan from hepatitis complications, and more than 88,000 new hepatitis C infections were reported in 2024 alone.

The ministry also presented HIV figures: more than 417,000 people are affected, including 3,900 children registered between January 2025 and June 2026. The report was covered by Waqas Ahmed on October 30.
