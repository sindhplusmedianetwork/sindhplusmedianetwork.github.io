Azerbaijan's School Hijab Curbs Spark Protests and Detentions

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Azerbaijan is witnessing growing protests after new school uniform rules effectively barred hijab-wearing students and teachers from state schools. The rules, approved on September 21, prohibit clothing that contradicts the “principle of secularism in education” without explicitly naming the hijab, and were extended to teachers on October 6. Demonstrations have taken place outside schools in Baku and the southern districts of Masally and Jalilabad, with students holding signs reading “The hijab does not prevent anyone from getting an education.” The Interior Ministry said 37 people were detained on Monday for obstructing enforcement of the rules and violating public order.

A teacher, Zahra Hasanova, who was told she could no longer wear the hijab at school, called it a personal choice, saying if she one day wanted to remove it, that should also be her choice. The controversy has even strained ties with close ally Turkiye, after an adviser to President Erdogan called the move “unacceptable” and Azerbaijan's ruling party hit back. Azerbaijan is 96 percent Muslim but its constitution defines it as a secular state guaranteeing freedom of religion.
