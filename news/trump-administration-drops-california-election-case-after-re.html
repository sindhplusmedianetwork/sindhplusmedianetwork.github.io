Trump Administration Drops California Election Case After Repeated Court Defeats

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Trump administration has withdrawn its election case in California after repeated legal defeats and strong opposition from state officials. The US Justice Department had demanded scrutiny and segregation of around 20,000 votes in California and Los Angeles County that the administration described as suspected non-citizen votes. The administration's position was that the votes should not be counted until citizenship was verified, but California officials rejected the federal government's demand, after which the administration approached the court.

US District Judge David Carter expressed reservations about the government's request during the hearing, saying the move could deprive eligible voters of their right to vote and create confusion in the electoral process, and called changing procedures during ongoing elections an extraordinary step. The Justice Department argued that segregating the disputed votes from other votes was necessary because once included in the count, their separation would no longer be possible. The US Supreme Court rejected the Trump administration's emergency appeal a day before the election, and lower courts and a nine-member appellate circuit had already ruled against the administration.

After the counting of votes was completed, the Justice Department took the position that the court relief sought was no longer possible, and on Friday it formally filed a request to withdraw the case. Legal objections by labour organisations and voting-rights groups over threats of criminal proceedings against election staff have also been resolved or withdrawn.
