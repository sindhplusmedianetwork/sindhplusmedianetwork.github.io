Punjab says air quality improved as smog monitoring and enforcement expand

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Senior Provincial Minister Marriyum Aurangzeb said Punjab has established a comprehensive, science-based system of round-the-clock monitoring, data analysis, early warning and enforcement to combat smog, resulting in a 23 to 64 per cent improvement in air quality indicators across various areas of the province. Addressing a press conference at Punjab Climate Watch, she said Lahore's Air Quality Index, which reached 2,400 in November 2024, was currently below 450 and had remained below 340 so far in November. She said ten enforcement squads were operating across Punjab, emission control systems had been installed at more than 9,000 industrial units and were being digitally monitored, and more than 14,000 brick kilns had been shifted to QR coding and zigzag technology.

The government has provided 10,045 Super Seeders to farmers at a 60 per cent subsidy to discourage crop residue burning, and incidents of crop residue burning have fallen by 39 per cent while fire anomalies around Lahore have decreased by 85 per cent. Around 600,000 vehicles have obtained fitness certification for the first time in Punjab, and the province is pursuing a ten-year Punjab Smog-Less Action Plan. Highlighting the impact on public health, she said outpatient visits for five respiratory-related ailments had declined by 69 per cent, while patient numbers at the secondary healthcare level had decreased by 21 per cent.

She cautioned that smog was a scientific, environmental and public health challenge that should not be politicised, and noted that Beijing had worked for 25 years and London for 20 years to control air pollution.
