No Gulf Security System Without Iran Can Be Stable, Says Omani Foreign Minister

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Oman's Foreign Minister Badr al-Busaidi has said Gulf states must take responsibility for their own security and reduce military dependence on outside powers, and that no Gulf security system can be stable or strong without including Iran. Speaking at Girton College at the University of Cambridge, Busaidi said a new regional security system should be formed covering all eight Gulf states, and that its foundation should be a non-aggression pact among them. He said the six Gulf Cooperation Council states, along with Iraq and Iran, should discuss the new system together, warning that ignoring Iran in regional security matters had already produced negative results.

Busaidi said Iran had been excluded from the security system and presented as a threat to the region, and that the idea of a fundamental enmity between Iran and Gulf Arab states was not correct, noting that Arab and Persian peoples had lived peacefully around the Gulf for centuries. He said outside friends and allies had offered material and political support for Gulf security at a heavy price, and that the long political cost of depending on outside powers for security had been very high. Partnerships with countries outside the region should not end, he said, but their centre should shift from military cooperation to economic cooperation and investment.

Busaidi also said there had been important progress in Iran-US nuclear talks and the basic points of a deal still existed, though returning to negotiations had now become extremely difficult, and he said Oman had helped prepare the outline of a temporary arrangement on the Strait of Hormuz through consultations with Iran.
