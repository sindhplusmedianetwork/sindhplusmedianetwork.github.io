PPP has no objection to new provinces except in Sindh, says Murad Ali Shah

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 12, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Chief Minister Syed Murad Ali Shah said on Wednesday that, except for Sindh, the Pakistan Peoples Party had no objection to the creation of new provinces in other parts of the country if their people so desired. He was speaking to the media in Thatta after attending the urs of Pir Syed Ghulam Jilani Shah Jilani in Makli. The chief minister made it clear that the division of Sindh was neither legally nor politically acceptable, as the people of the province had repeatedly rejected the idea through their elected representatives.

He said the Constitution provided that the boundaries of a province could not be altered unless its people wanted such a change, and noted that the Punjab Assembly had already passed several resolutions seeking the creation of new provinces. Mr Shah said the Constitution also provided a mechanism for a referendum, but only on an issue where there was ambiguity, and even after a referendum the final decision would rest with the assemblies, as no referendum could override the Constitution. On the water-sharing issue, he said Sindh's case for distribution of water under the 1991 Water Apportionment Accord was already before the Council of Common Interests, and rejected the notion that Sindh was responsible for water shortages.

He asked how Sindh could be accused of stealing water when water did not reach the province in the first place, and said the provincial government had taken up the matter with the federal government.
