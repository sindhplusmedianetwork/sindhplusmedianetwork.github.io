Houthi fighters advance towards Aden as government forces pull back to defend the port city

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Iran-backed Houthi rebels, who seized much of Yemen in 2014, have forced the army of the internationally recognised government to withdraw from the Red Sea coast in order to defend their interim capital, Aden. Government forces announced on Sunday that they were pulling back from the area around the strategic Bab al-Mandab Strait, including Dhubab airport, to strengthen the defence of Aden and neighbouring areas. The Houthis seized the strait area in a surprise attack in September, and their media say they have taken control of the areas the government forces left.

Saudi Arabia has now removed the commander of the Saudi-led coalition forces, Lieutenant General Fahad Al-Salman, and appointed the chief of general staff, Lieutenant General Fayyad al-Ruwaili, as the new commander. Nicholas Hopton, a former British ambassador to Yemen and Iran, said the government's decision to prioritise the military defence of Aden at the expense of withdrawing from other areas suggests the war is not going well for the Saudi-backed government, and warned that the rest of the world should be worried. He warned that if the port of Aden, a city of roughly 1.25 million people, were to fall to the Houthis, it would undoubtedly lead to a further escalation of the regional crisis and more fragility in the global economy.

The Bab al-Mandab Strait is a key global trade route, with about 12 per cent of global trade normally passing through it, and a former British defence attache to Yemen warned that the Houthi advance could trigger a spike in oil prices, as it did when they seized the strait in September.
