GCC Rejects Threats to Its Security, Sovereignty and Territorial Integrity

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary-General Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi has reaffirmed that the GCC states reject any infringement on their security, sovereignty or territorial integrity. Briefing the United Nations Security Council in New York on Friday, he said attacks against the GCC states violate international law, threaten international peace and security, and require Security Council action to halt them and prevent their recurrence. He told the Council that GCC states had been targeted by more than 7,000 missiles and drones since 28 February 2026, striking vital installations and civilian facilities, causing deaths, leaving people wounded and inflicting extensive damage on infrastructure and the marine environment.

Albudaiwi called for follow-up on the implementation of relevant Security Council resolutions, enforcement of the arms embargo, accountability for those responsible and compensation for the resulting damage. He also warned that the repercussions of the attacks had extended to international maritime routes, condemning attacks on commercial vessels and oil and gas tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, the Red Sea and the Bab Al Mandab Strait, and rejecting any attempt to close these waterways or obstruct navigation. The GCC chief reiterated the Council's long-standing position on the Palestinian cause and its support for an independent Palestinian state along the borders of 4 June 1967, with East Jerusalem as its capital, and reaffirmed support for Lebanon's sovereignty under Security Council Resolution 1701 and for Syria's sovereignty, unity and stability.

He stressed that the GCC states remain committed to resolving crises through dialogue and diplomacy, and concluded by calling on the Security Council and the international community for an immediate and unconditional cessation of attacks against GCC states and commercial vessels, stressing that the security of the Arabian Gulf is an integral part of international security.
