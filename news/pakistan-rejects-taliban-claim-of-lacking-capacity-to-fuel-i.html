Pakistan rejects Taliban claim of lacking capacity to fuel insecurity

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 25, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan's information ministry on Saturday rejected the Afghan Taliban's position that it lacked the capabilities to fuel insecurity in other countries, accusing the regime of resorting to diversionary allegations instead of addressing Pakistan's security concerns. The rebuttal followed a statement by Taliban spokesperson Zabiullah Mujahid on X, in which he said it was not in Afghanistan's interest to create insecurity in Pakistan and claimed the Taliban did not possess the capabilities or resources to foment or finance instability elsewhere. The ministry said Pakistan's concerns were not based on conjecture and pointed to the United Nations Security Council's Analytical Support and Sanctions Monitoring Team, whose 16th report examined the presence and activities of terrorist groups in Afghanistan.

It also cited a report by the US Lead Inspector General for Operation Enduring Sentinel covering January to March 2026, which said the Taliban continued to allow the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan to operate from Afghan territory with greater liberty and support, leading to more attacks against Pakistan. The report estimated TTP strength at 4,000 to 6,000 fighters and described it as the largest among the groups present. The ministry further rejected former US envoy Zalmay Khalilzad's claim that Pakistan was arming Afghans to fight the Taliban, calling it a sweeping and baseless assertion.

It demanded that the Afghan Taliban regime end any tolerance, facilitation or support for terrorist organisations, including the TTP and the Baloch Liberation Army, and ensure Afghan territory is not used to plan or launch attacks against Pakistan.
