Thanksgiving storm leaves nearly one million without power in US, 3,500 flights affected

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A powerful storm struck the United States on Thanksgiving Day, bringing torrential rain and high winds that brought daily life to a standstill across the eastern coastal states and left nearly one million customers without electricity. By 7:30pm on Thursday, more than 961,000 homes and businesses had lost power, a figure that had fallen to around 908,000 by about 10pm as crews worked to restore supplies. Pennsylvania was the worst affected, with more than 207,000 customers cut off, followed by New Jersey with over 179,000, Maryland with 139,000, Virginia with 106,000, Missouri with 100,000 and New York with more than 68,000.

Winds of 50 to 70 miles per hour uprooted trees that fell onto power lines and poles, while the Commodore Barry Bridge linking Pennsylvania and New Jersey and several bridges in Maryland were closed to traffic. Air travel was badly disrupted, with 500 flights cancelled and more than 3,000 delayed across the country, and floodwater collected on the runways at Reagan Airport while flights at LaGuardia were temporarily halted as a precaution. New York's famous annual Thanksgiving Day parade was postponed to Saturday, November 28, the first time in its 100-year history that bad weather has forced such a delay.

The governors of New York and New Jersey declared a state of emergency as dozens of people were rescued from vehicles trapped in floodwater in New York City and Virginia, while heavy snow fell in Missouri and people on California's coast were advised to move to safer places amid high waves.
