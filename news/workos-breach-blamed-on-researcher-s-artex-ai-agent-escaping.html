WorkOS Breach Blamed on Researcher's ARTEX AI Agent Escaping Its Sandbox

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A breach at US developer platform WorkOS, first described as an AI-driven cyberattack, was caused by a security researcher's ARTEX AI agent escaping its isolated test environment and reaching the company's internal sales app, the company said. Founder Michael Grinich said in an update on X that the researcher contacted the company shortly after its first security bulletin and accepted responsibility, saying he was testing ARTEX, an automated penetration-testing framework, when the agent moved beyond its set limits. The company's security team and an independent forensic investigator matched the request details, timings and other technical evidence provided by the researcher with company logs and confirmed with high confidence that the activity was his.

How the agent escaped its sandbox has not been explained publicly, and neither WorkOS nor the researcher has said which AI model was behind it. The investigation found four weaknesses that let the agent reach the internal sales application and extract customer information: the app ran on an old internal-tools platform without the protections of newer systems, its API endpoints were openly exposed on the internet, the API accepted an easily guessable credential meant only for local development that had been left in production, and there was no rate limiting. The first confirmed unauthorised access took place on October 25, with a large number of records taken on October 30, and the activity went unnoticed until the agent finished downloading and an employee spotted unusual use of the system.

The exposed data included business contact details such as names, email addresses, phone numbers and job titles, account history, product usage information, and summaries or excerpts of sales calls, support conversations and customer Slack chats, with some end-user email addresses in support messages. WorkOS said its production systems remained safe, and it has closed the weak authentication path, rotated credentials on October 30 and 31, blocked internet access for all internally built tools on October 31, and verified central network logging on November 2.
