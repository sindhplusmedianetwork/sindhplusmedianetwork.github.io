Over 20 rights groups call for Imaan and Hadi's release after latest sentencing

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 06, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

More than 20 international human rights organisations, including Amnesty International, on Friday called on the government to immediately and unconditionally release human rights lawyer Imaan Zainab Mazari-Hazir and her husband, Hadi Ali Chattha, after the two were sentenced in the security breach case. On October 29, an antiterrorism court sentenced Imaan and Hadi to 10 years in prison each in the security breach case, along with one-year sentences and fines of 50,000 rupees each under Section 186 of the Pakistan Penal Code, and two years each under Section 353. Earlier in the year, the two were also sentenced by a trial court to 17 years on multiple charges under the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act in the controversial social media posts case.

In a joint statement on Friday, 23 organisations, including the Alliance for Lawyers at Risk, the New York City Bar Association, the Council of Bars and Law Societies of Europe and PEN Norway, condemned the recent sentences and sought an end to the extensive misuse of the criminal justice system against the two lawyers. The statement said the convictions raised serious concerns over antiterrorism legislation being used to criminally punish and intimidate lawyers undertaking permissible human rights work, and noted that the verdict was delivered despite a petition pending in the Supreme Court. The organisations said the trial, conducted at Adiala jail in Rawalpindi, did not meet fair trial standards, noting that the couple had been kept in a cage-like structure in the courtroom and that their counsel was not given sufficient access.

They called on the government to take appropriate steps to have the convictions quashed, to drop charges arising solely from the couple's work and defence of human rights, and to review the use of the electronic crimes law and antiterrorism legislation against lawyers, journalists and human rights defenders.
