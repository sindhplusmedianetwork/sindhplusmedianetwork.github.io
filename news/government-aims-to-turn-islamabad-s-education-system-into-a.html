Government aims to turn Islamabad's education system into a national model, says minister

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Federal Minister for Planning, Development and Special Initiatives Ahsan Iqbal has said that the government was working to transform Islamabad's education system into a model that can serve as a benchmark for other parts of the country. While visiting the Islamabad Model College for Girls (IMCG) Golra Girls School under the ICT Education Reforms initiative, the minister said the world was witnessing an unprecedented knowledge revolution, in which national progress would increasingly depend on intellectual capacity, creativity and the ability to generate knowledge rather than physical strength. He said the government's objective was not to remove anyone from the system but to transform it into a high-performance institution capable of delivering quality education and measurable results, adding that the foremost priority was a uniform and equitable education system providing every child with equal opportunities.

The minister stressed that Pakistan must move beyond rote learning and promote critical thinking, creativity, research, problem-solving and the ability to question and generate knowledge, observing that the traditional system had placed excessive emphasis on memorisation. Highlighting early childhood development, he said the first five to seven years of a child's life were crucial for developing intellectual and creative abilities, which required a supportive learning environment at home, in schools and throughout society. He said teachers must move beyond delivering curricula and preparing students for examinations, and instead encourage curiosity, independent thinking, reading habits, observation and experiential learning, with classrooms becoming centres of inquiry and research.

Clarifying the scope of the ICT education reforms, he said the initiative aimed at fundamental improvements in teaching practices and learning outcomes rather than merely administrative changes, and invited teachers and education personnel to share their suggestions and practical recommendations.
