Sindh cane growers in limbo as crushing season start still not notified

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Sugar cane growers in Sindh remain in limbo as the provincial government has yet to issue a notification for the start of the 2026-27 crushing season, even though the cabinet decided on November 3 that mills would begin crushing from November 15, and the crop is ready for harvest across Sindh, with wheat to be sown after it. Under the Sindh Sugar Factories Control (Amendment) Act 2009, the provincial government, through the agriculture department, must fix the cane price, the date for the start of crushing and the quality premium payable over and above the notified price, and the Act also requires mills to begin crushing by November 30 at the latest, yet as of Tuesday no notification announcing the crushing date had been issued. Sindh produced 2.02 million tonnes of sugar in 2025-26 from 19.18 million tonnes of cane crushed, with sugar recovery of 10.38 per cent, while in 2024-25 it produced 1.62 million tonnes from 16.4 million tonnes of cane, with recovery of 9.48 per cent.

The cane price has not been fixed for the past two years, under IMF conditions, and the last price fixed was Rs425 per 40kg in 2023-24, with the government not expected to notify a price for 2026-27 either, the third year in a row. Growers say the delay causes heavy financial loss and mental stress, as cane loses weight in the field if it is not harvested on time. Sindh Abadgar Board president Mahmood Nawaz Shah said a late harvest suited the millers because it raised sugar recovery, while the weight loss meant monetary losses for growers, who are paid by weight.

Millers, through the Pakistan Sugar Mills Association, are demanding permission to export their stocks, and the federal government allowed the export of only 200,000 tonnes in September, which the association rejected. Sindh Chamber of Agriculture senior vice president Nabi Bux Sathio urged Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari and the Sindh government to intervene to rescue the growers, warning that the crushing season would coincide with Ramazan, which would slow harvesting and crushing and could stretch it to April, when the kharif season begins.
