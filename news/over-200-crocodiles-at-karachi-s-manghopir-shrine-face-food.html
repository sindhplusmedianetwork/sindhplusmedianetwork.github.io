Over 200 crocodiles at Karachi's Manghopir shrine face food, space and water crisis

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 15, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

More than 200 crocodiles living in the lake beside the shrine of Hazrat Sakhi Sultan Shah at Manghopir in Karachi are facing serious shortages of food, space and clean water. The crocodiles have grown weak and begun falling ill as the lake's dirty, algae-covered water is not refreshed and low rainfall in Karachi has kept fresh water out. Visitors now bring far less meat than before because goat meat, which once cost 700 to 1,000 rupees per kilogram, now costs 2,500 rupees per kilogram.

The lake covers only about 1,000 square yards, and with more than 200 crocodiles living in it, the animals attack each other for want of space. The protective wall around the lake has crumbled badly and could collapse at any time, causing a serious accident, according to the shrine's administration. The shrine's caretaker and administrator, Khalifa Sajjad, whose family has looked after the crocodiles for three generations, said repeated appeals to the Sindh government for a permanent food arrangement have brought no action.

He said the administration would consider it a great help if the government provided even one kilogram of meat per crocodile each day, while a philanthropist from Karachi donates four cows every month to help keep the feeding going. The oldest crocodile, Mor Sahib, is said to be about 175 years old, while the others are aged between 50 and 70 years.
