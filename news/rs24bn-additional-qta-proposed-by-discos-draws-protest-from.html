Rs24bn additional QTA proposed by Discos draws protest from industrial consumers

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 18, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Industrial consumers on Tuesday protested over a Rs24 billion additional quarterly tariff burden proposed by power companies due to higher capacity charges, variable operation and maintenance costs and the impact of system losses, resulting in an estimated tariff impact of around Rs1.30 per unit for three months. The public hearing was presided over by National Electric Power Regulatory Authority members Amina Ahmed, Maqsood Anwar Khan and Ghulamullah Shaikh, on the request of 11 distribution companies to seek approval for charging the additional quarterly tariff adjustment for the July-September 2026 period. A brief intervention from the power division, represented by the chief financial officer of the Power Planning and Monitoring Company, Naveed Qaisar, was the request that the additional adjustment be applied beginning in the December 1, 2026 billing and ending in February 2027.

Two industrial interveners, Amir Shaikh and Rehan Javed, expressed serious concern over Rs39 billion in additional capacity payment claims for the quarter against pre-approved references built into the base tariff, besides the Rs5.6 billion impact on variable operation and maintenance and Rs7.8 billion impact of system losses in monthly fuel charges. Shaikh said the major reason appeared to be units sold to selective consumers under the incremental consumption package at an unrealistic cost of Rs10 cheaper than normal tariffs, which translated into a higher adjustment for all consumers despite the government's promise that the impact of the package would not be charged to consumers except for its beneficiaries. Javed noted the Discos claimed Rs29.25 billion of negative impact of the incremental consumption package on industrial consumers, saying it was actually an additional burden on all consumers, and he alleged the Discos had counted the wrong units and measured against fiscal year 2023-24 instead of references set for this year.

Cumulatively, all 11 Discos reported that capacity charges during the July-September period had increased by Rs39 billion, with the highest additional claims coming from Islamabad Electric at Rs10.833 billion, followed by Rs10.46 billion from Faisalabad and Rs7 billion from Peshawar Electric, while a significant portion was offset by negative adjustments of Rs13 billion from four other Discos. In line with policy guidelines issued by the federal government for uniform quarterly adjustments, the adjustment determined for Discos for the third quarter of calendar year 2026 will also apply to K-Electric consumers once Nepra issues its judgment.
