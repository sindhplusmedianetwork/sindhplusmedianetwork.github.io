First Pakistani Astronaut to Undertake Space Mission From China on Nov 25: Ahsan Iqbal

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal announced on Sunday that Pakistan's first astronaut will undertake a space mission from a Chinese space centre on November 25. He made the announcement while addressing the concluding ceremony of World Space Week in Narowal, held in collaboration with the Pakistan Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (Suparco), according to a planning ministry statement. The minister said the country was also aiming to achieve a lunar landing by 2035, expressing hope that Pakistan's cooperation with China would help it reach new heights in space exploration.

He highlighted the importance of space technology in agriculture, communications, climate monitoring, education and national security. Iqbal stressed that scientific research and space exploration were essential for technological advancement. The astronaut will be one of two pilots — Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud — whom China selected in April this year as candidates for its manned space programme and who have been undergoing intensive training in Beijing.

China has not said which of the two will fly, but Suparco's Amer Gilani said last month that a final selection decision was expected in mid-to-late October. Once in orbit, the chosen pilot will carry the title of 'payload specialist', running Pakistan's science experiments aboard a Chinese-built spacecraft, trained by Chinese instructors and communicating in Mandarin.
