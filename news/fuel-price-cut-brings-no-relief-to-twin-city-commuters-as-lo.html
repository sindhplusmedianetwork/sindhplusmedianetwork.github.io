Fuel Price Cut Brings No Relief to Twin-City Commuters as Local Fares Stay Put

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Despite a fifth consecutive reduction in petrol and diesel prices, fares of private public transport on local routes in Rawalpindi and Islamabad remain unchanged, leaving daily commuters to bear the same travel costs. Operators of inter-district and inter-provincial services running from Rawalpindi have reduced fares by between Rs50 and Rs1,000, but local passengers travelling between the twin cities for work, education and labour continue to pay the previous rates. Local transporters attribute their reluctance to uncertainty over fuel prices, saying the latest reduction may be temporary and they would have to raise fares again if prices rise within days.

A review of petroleum prices shows that between October 31 and November 7, petrol became cheaper by Rs31.91 a litre, falling from Rs377.91 to Rs346, while high-speed diesel fell by Rs24.96 a litre, from Rs402.96 to Rs378. In the latest reduction, petrol was cut by Rs6.59 a litre and diesel by Rs4.25 a litre, with the revised rates fixed for November 7 to 10. According to fare lists issued in Rawalpindi on November 7, Kohistan Express reduced its Rawalpindi to Peshawar fare from Rs1,200 to Rs1,150 and its executive-class fare to Lahore from Rs2,500 to Rs2,400.

Crystal Lines cut its Rawalpindi to Karachi fare from Rs7,600 to Rs7,300 and its fare to Sukkur from Rs6,050 to Rs5,800, while Safe Way Travel reduced its executive-class fare to Karachi from Rs8,000 to Rs7,000 and its sleeper-class fare from Rs13,000 to Rs12,000.
