Pakistan-Iran Border Partly Reopens, but Trade Remains Limited and Thousands of Trucks Still Stuck

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Trade across the Pakistan-Iran border has resumed only in part after a suspension lasting about ten days, with thousands of trucks still waiting on both sides. The Gabd-Rimdan and Taftan-Mirjaveh crossings were confirmed closed on 17 October, and reports on 25 October said the two main crossings had reopened, allowing trucks carrying liquefied gas and food to move again. Business representatives in the southwest said long queues remained, with three to four thousand trucks on the Iranian side and two to three thousand on the Pakistani side.

Traders and chamber officials said that, at present, only goods booked before 16 October were being allowed to enter Pakistan from Iran, leaving the wider situation uncertain. The two sides gave different explanations for the original suspension, with Balochistan officials pointing to security concerns and the prevention of smuggling, while the Iranian foreign ministry described unclear technical problems requiring immediate precautionary steps. Pakistan's ambassador-level exchanges also pointed to changes in the implementation of Pakistan's e-form procedure for imports from Iran, which Iranian officials said had in practice left traders unable to send goods to Pakistan.

Balochistan's interior minister said the border with Iran was open, but added that trade matters were for the federal government, which issues orders to close or open the border.
