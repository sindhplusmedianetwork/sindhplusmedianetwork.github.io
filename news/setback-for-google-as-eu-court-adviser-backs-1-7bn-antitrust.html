Setback for Google as EU court adviser backs $1.7bn antitrust fine

Google suffered a setback on Thursday as an adviser to the Court of Justice of the European Union said a lower court had been wrong to scrap a €1.49 billion ($1.7 billion) antitrust fine against the company. The European Commission imposed the fine in 2019, finding that Google had used restrictive clauses in contracts with publishers that stopped rivals from placing search advertisements on their websites and strengthened its dominance in online search advertising. The practices ran from 2006 to 2016, and Google removed the disputed clauses from publisher agreements in 2016.

The EU's General Court rejected the Commission's decision in 2024, prompting the Commission to appeal to the Luxembourg-based Court of Justice. Advocate General Kokott said the General Court had imposed excessive requirements on examining the duration of the practices and had not sufficiently linked its conclusion to the criteria for assessing whether a company holds a dominant position. The court, which usually follows most such recommendations, will rule next year.

The AdSense fine was one of six EU antitrust penalties that have cost Google €10.38 billion over nearly two decades.
