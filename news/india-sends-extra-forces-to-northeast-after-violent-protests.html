India Sends Extra Forces to Northeast After Violent Protests Against Mega-Dam

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 28, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

India has deployed hundreds of additional security personnel in its remote northeast after youth protesters clashed with police last week over a plan to build a mega-dam to counter a Chinese hydropower project upstream. Protests in Arunachal Pradesh's Siang Valley, which borders Tibet, turned violent last week, triggering clashes in which police made at least 21 arrests. Police fired tear gas shells, while youth protesters pelted stones and set fire to a police station and an office of the state-owned National Hydroelectric Power Corporation, authorities said.

Arunachal Pradesh police chief S D Singh Jamwal told Reuters that forces were being increased where required and that authorities did not want any law and order problem. Sporadic protests have taken place in the Siang Valley in the last two years over the proposed 11,200-megawatt Siang Upper Multipurpose Project, which will be developed by the state-run corporation. Villagers who oppose the project fear the dam would submerge ancestral villages belonging to the Adi tribe, with one campaigner saying 27 villages would be completely submerged and at least 40 or 50 more affected.

The Siang river flows into Arunachal Pradesh from Tibet, where it is known as the Yarlung Zangbo, and China announced plans in December 2024 to build the world's largest hydropower dam upstream, triggering fears in New Delhi that its rival could weaponise its control of the river.
