Too little sleep may raise type 2 diabetes risk in women, study finds

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Insufficient sleep does not only cause tiredness and sluggishness; it can also affect the body's metabolism and the system that regulates blood sugar levels. A study has pointed to a link between insufficient sleep and insulin resistance, particularly in women, especially those going through menopause. The research, published in the medical journal Diabetes Care, was carried out on 40 women aged 20 to 75 who were at higher risk of cardiometabolic disease because of factors including being overweight, obesity, a family history of type 2 diabetes, high blood lipid levels or heart disease.

During the study, the participants went through different six-week phases: in one phase they followed a healthy and adequate sleep routine, while in another they were given only six hours of sleep a day. The findings showed that when sleep was limited, women's bodies needed more insulin to keep blood glucose levels normal, and insulin resistance was higher in women who slept less than in those who got adequate and complete sleep. According to the study's senior author, Marie-Pierre St-Onge, the findings highlight the importance of sleep quality for women's health, especially in the period after menopause.

The study also noted that women generally sleep less than men, which can increase the risk to their health over the long term.
