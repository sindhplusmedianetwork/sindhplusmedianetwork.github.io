Asia stocks tumble after AI rout rocks US markets

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 06, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A rout in Asia's tech-heavy markets deepened on Friday as fear of increased regulatory scrutiny and a slowdown in artificial intelligence build-out precipitated the steepest decline in US stocks since the tariff-triggered selloff in April 2025. Japan's Nikkei slumped 7 per cent immediately after the opening bell, while benchmark share price indices in South Korea and Taiwan dropped more than 8 per cent. AI chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell nearly 10 per cent and 12 per cent respectively in South Korea, while Kioxia plunged 14 per cent in Japan and TSMC fell as much as 8.5 per cent in Taiwan.

Wall Street's volatility fear gauge soared, the Nasdaq sank 5.5 per cent and 3 trillion dollars was wiped off S and P 500 firms, with investors worried that Democrats who dominated this week's midterm elections will apply brakes to the AI boom. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index slumped 12.2 per cent overnight and the Nasdaq has now shed 12 per cent from its record close a month ago. Strategists said there was clearly an element of panic in the selling, and that simultaneous unwinding of positions was making the adjustment more violent.

Bonds gained in Asia as investors sought refuge, with the yield on the benchmark US 10-year Treasury note at 4.838 per cent, hovering near its lowest level since September 9. Some analysts said the selloff was overdone and the bottom was not far off, noting that even TSMC hit its daily limit down circuit breaker at one point, which typically marks the tail end of a downturn.
