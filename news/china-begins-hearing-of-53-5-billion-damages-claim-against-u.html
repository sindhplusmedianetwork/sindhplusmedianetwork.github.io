China Begins Hearing of $53.5 Billion Damages Claim Against US State of Missouri

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A court in China has begun hearing a $53.5 billion damages case against the US state of Missouri over the alleged spread of false information about the coronavirus and damage to China's reputation. The case has been filed in the Wuhan Intermediate People's Court by the Wuhan municipal government, the Wuhan Institute of Virology and the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Apart from the US state of Missouri, the current and former attorneys general Eric Schmitt and Andrew Bailey have also been named in the case.

The Chinese institutions say Missouri officials carried out malicious legal proceedings and spread false, discriminatory and insulting information about the origin of the coronavirus, damaging the reputation of Chinese institutions and officials. The Chinese side has demanded 356.4 billion yuan, about $53.5 billion, in damages. It has also demanded that the New York Times, CNN, YouTube and the Chinese news agency Xinhua publish a formal public apology in American and international media.

The case has come in response to a lawsuit filed by Missouri in April 2020 against China's Hubei province and other Chinese parties, holding China responsible for alleged economic losses from the pandemic. In March 2025, a US federal court ordered China in a one-sided judgment to pay $24 billion to the state of Missouri, a claim linked to pandemic responsibilities and a dispute over protective medical supplies. Legal experts say the new Chinese case could complicate Missouri's efforts to enforce the US court judgment and seize Chinese assets abroad.
