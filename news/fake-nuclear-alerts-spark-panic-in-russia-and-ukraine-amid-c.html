Fake nuclear alerts spark panic in Russia and Ukraine amid cyberattacks on warning systems

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 02, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Fresh cyberattacks struck the emergency warning systems of Russia and Ukraine, spreading fabricated alerts that claimed nuclear-armed drones were heading toward Russian cities. The fake messages urged residents to gather three weeks of food and supplies and move to secure locations. Russia's Ministry of Emergency Situations confirmed its alert application had suffered a large-scale breach that could have let attackers reach millions of users, though it said the country's main warning infrastructure stayed online and declined to name who was responsible.

Ukraine reported a parallel incident on its air raid app, with false messages appearing just minutes before a six-day ceasefire around the occupied town of Oleshky was due to end; Ukrainian officials blamed Russia and urged people to trust only official channels. The temporary truce had allowed hundreds of residents to flee the conflict zone in a rare moment of cooperation between the two sides. Both governments say they have agreed to continue regular humanitarian missions despite the continued fighting.

The twin incidents have exposed how the digital systems millions of civilians rely on for survival have become targets in the war.
