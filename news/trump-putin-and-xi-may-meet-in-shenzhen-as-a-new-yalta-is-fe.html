Trump, Putin and Xi may meet in Shenzhen as a new 'Yalta' is feared

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The world's three most powerful leaders could sit down together next week to discuss global flashpoints, from the wars in Ukraine and Iran to nuclear weapons and artificial intelligence, though the prospects of a grand bargain emerging from the rare encounter are slim. Down-to-the-wire negotiations over a meeting between US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin have not yet yielded an agenda or format, according to two people familiar with the planning, but a meeting is expected in Shenzhen, where the leaders will attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum. The three men come with very different goals: Putin sees a chance to secure an agreement to end the war in Ukraine on his terms, Trump sees a moment for the history books with possible movement towards a three-way agreement on nuclear arms control, and Xi faces an unpredictable gathering full of risks.

The meeting has drawn comparisons, particularly in Russia, with the 1945 Yalta Conference, which formalised spheres of influence and became shorthand for great-power deals imposed on smaller countries, raising anxiety from Kyiv to Tokyo. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said last month that Ukraine did not want the meeting to turn into "Yalta 2, 3, 4" with a redistribution of influence, and for smaller governments not at the table, including Ukraine and Taiwan, the idea of the big powers cutting a private deal without them is a cause of deep unease. A senior Trump administration official said the United States did not expect to reach any kind of agreement on nuclear issues, while China, which has around 500 nuclear warheads compared with more than 5,000 each for Russia and the United States, has resisted putting the war in Ukraine on the agenda.

Beijing's embassy in Washington said China was in communication with relevant parties about a meeting between the three heads of state, and Putin's spokesman Dmitry Peskov said suggestions were being exchanged about a possible agenda and that the meeting would certainly be interesting.
