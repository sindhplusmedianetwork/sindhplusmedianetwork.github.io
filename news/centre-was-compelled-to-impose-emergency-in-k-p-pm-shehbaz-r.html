Centre Was Compelled to Impose Emergency in K-P, PM Shehbaz Reiterates

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 18, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Saturday reiterated that the Centre was "compelled" to impose emergency and governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa due to the provincial government's lack of cooperation in countering terrorism. The Centre imposed emergency and governor's rule in KP on October 11, citing the provincial government's "deliberate disengagement" from the fight against terrorism, and the decisions were endorsed by Parliament this week amid opposition protests. Speaking in Gujranwala after offering condolences on the death of PML-N lawmaker Mahmood Bashir Virk's father, the premier said the Centre made a "last attempt" through rounds of talks by a joint delegation of the PPP and the government with the opposition, with the hours-long second round on October 3 ending without a breakthrough.

He said he had held multiple meetings with Sohail Afridi, the KP chief minister whose role now stands non-functional, as well as with his predecessor Ali Amin Gandapur, assuring them of the Centre's support in fighting terrorism together. "But, unfortunately, despite all these efforts, thousands of our law enforcement agencies personnel, soldiers, officers and officers of the armed forces were sacrificing their lives every day to defend the homeland," the prime minister said. He added that the federal government was met with "threats and long marches every other day in response", recalling the PTI's 2014 D-Chowk sit-ins and last year's November protest that jammed the country, and alleged that "this time as well, they had an entire plan of bloodshed".
