At UNSC, Pakistan Condemns Houthi Attacks on Saudi Arabia, Backs Kingdom's Security

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 16, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan on Thursday strongly condemned Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia's civilian and economic infrastructure at a United Nations Security Council debate on the Middle East, reaffirming its support for the kingdom's security and right to self-defence. Speaking as Pakistan's acting Permanent Representative, Ambassador Usman Jadoon said the attacks "must cease immediately" and backed the UN special envoy's efforts to resume dialogue toward a comprehensive and lasting peace in Yemen. The condemnation comes as Saudi Arabia faces sustained attacks in recent days, including a strike on Riyadh's international airport described as the deadliest in years, amid a dramatic escalation of the war with the Houthis.

Yemen's civil war reignited in July after a UN-brokered truce collapsed, and the Saudi-led coalition has fought the Houthis since 2015, when they seized the capital Sanaa and forced out the internationally recognised government, until a 2022 ceasefire. In the same session, Pakistan joined calls to halt settler violence in the West Bank and abandon attempts to annex occupied Palestinian territory, with Jadoon warning that settlement expansion — particularly the E1 project — threatens to sever Palestinian territorial contiguity. He condemned Israeli incursions into Al-Aqsa Mosque and demanded respect for the historical and legal status quo of the holy sites, while expressing concern over continued Gaza ceasefire violations, Palestinian deaths and restrictions on humanitarian access.

Jadoon called for a "credible, time-bound and irreversible" political process to end the Israeli occupation and establish an independent, sovereign, contiguous and viable Palestinian state on pre-1967 borders, with Al-Quds Al-Sharif as its capital. The Palestinian representative, Deputy Permanent Observer Majed Bamya, said Israel continued to kill Palestinians despite the ceasefire and noted that more than 1,000 Palestinians had been killed over three years in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. France's Ambassador Jerome Bonnafont and Britain's Ambassador Sarah MacIntosh also criticised Israeli settlement policy, while the United States defended President Donald Trump's comprehensive Gaza peace plan, endorsed by Security Council Resolution 2803, and the UN's acting Special Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov warned the ceasefire remained extremely fragile, citing about 1,500 Palestinian fatalities since the ceasefire, including more than 320 children and 140 women.
