Recycling projects power UAE's shift to a circular economy

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Recycling and resource recovery projects are gaining momentum across the United Arab Emirates, supporting the country's transition towards a circular economy by converting waste into valuable resources, reducing landfill disposal and improving resource efficiency. Initiatives implemented or expanded in 2026 span biofuel production from used cooking oil, organic waste processing, materials recovery, food waste reduction and product reuse, reflecting efforts to maximise the economic and environmental value of waste. An energy group and a city municipality have launched a partnership to explore collecting used cooking oil from restaurants, businesses and households for conversion into biodiesel.

One municipality is developing a system to collect approximately 195,000 litres of used cooking oil annually and convert it into an estimated 176,000 litres of biofuel, and plans to expand grease trap installations to 503 food establishments in 2027. In organic waste management, a university and a biotechnology firm have launched a two-year research project to process about 40 kilogrammes of waste daily through anaerobic digestion and composting, while exploring the potential to produce hydrogen from biogas. Meanwhile, development work on a materials recovery facility in Al Ain had reached 50 per cent completion in October, and once operational it is expected to process 400,000 tonnes of municipal solid waste annually and divert approximately 60 per cent of such waste from landfill.

Textile recycling is another priority, with one initiative addressing an estimated 220,000 tonnes of textile waste generated annually through collection, sorting, reuse and recycling, while a national food initiative has helped rescue more than 300,000 tonnes of food and reached over 10,000 families.
