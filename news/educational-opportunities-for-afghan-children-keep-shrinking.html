Educational Opportunities for Afghan Children Keep Shrinking, UN Agencies Warn

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 05, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Educational opportunities for Afghan children are continuously shrinking, United Nations agencies have warned. More than half of Afghan children of primary-school age are outside the formal education system, while millions of girls cannot access secondary or university-level education. The number of children aged 7 to 18 rose from about 10.4 million in 2019 to 11.5 million in 2024, the report notes.

Pointing to incomplete data on private, religious and alternative institutions, the report warns that access to secondary and higher education, technical training and employment is becoming ever more limited. With university doors closed to women, Afghanistan's higher education sector has shrunk markedly since 2021: about 208,000 students are enrolled this year, compared with 189,000 in 2024 but far below the roughly 430,000 in 2020. The agencies called for the complete restoration of Afghan girls' and women's fundamental right to education and the removal of all barriers harming educational quality.
