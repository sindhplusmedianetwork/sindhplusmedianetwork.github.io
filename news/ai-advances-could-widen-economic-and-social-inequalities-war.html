AI Advances Could Widen Economic and Social Inequalities, Warns Shaza Fatima

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 04, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Federal Minister for Information Technology and Telecommunication Shaza Fatima Khawaja has warned that rapid advances in artificial intelligence could widen economic and social inequalities, stressing that Pakistan must move beyond being a consumer of technology to becoming a producer of AI-driven products and solutions. Addressing the concluding ceremony of the 29th Sustainable Development Conference and 4th Sustainable Investment Expo organised by the Sustainable Development Policy Institute, she said the pace of technological advancement could create disparities increasingly difficult for developing countries to overcome. She said the shift from the digital age to the intelligent age, driven by generative and agentic AI, was fundamentally transforming economies, labour markets, education systems and societies, and that Pakistan needed mathematicians, statisticians and AI specialists capable of designing algorithms rather than merely using products built elsewhere.

The minister voiced concern that AI was influencing cultural identities, languages and ethical values, noting children's growing reliance on platforms such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, and emphasised building linguistic datasets to preserve Urdu and Pakistan's regional languages in AI systems. Referring to the government's digital agenda, she said the Digital Nation Pakistan Act had laid the foundation for a national digital master plan, with work underway toward a cashless economy, paperless government and contactless society, adding that the federal government had already gone paperless and the prime minister was personally leading the cashless push. She identified digital connectivity, cybersecurity and trust, and AI as the three pillars of Pakistan's digital transformation.

On jobs, she said: 'It's not going to take our jobs away, but it will definitely create polarity between people who know AI and people who don't know AI,' stressing continuous upskilling and reskilling as four-year degree programmes struggled to keep pace. She called for coordinated efforts by academia, researchers, investors, policymakers and international partners so Pakistan and other emerging economies are not left behind, adding: 'Technology is only a means to an end. And the end is every single citizen's livelihood in the country.'
