Pakistan Woos Global Investors for Its Untapped Mineral Wealth

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan on Thursday sought to attract global investment in its vast untapped mineral resources, hosting a two-day international forum in Islamabad attended by more than 5,000 delegates from over 50 countries. Senior officials, including the chief ministers of Punjab, Sindh and Balochistan, the Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa governor and several federal ministers, addressed the Pakistan Minerals Investment Forum 2026. Meanwhile, Canadian company Ivanhoe Mines signed a memorandum of understanding with Pakistan's Mari Energies, and its chairman Robert Friedland said the firm was in the early stages of setting up a local office in Pakistan and would sign another agreement on Friday with Mari Minerals, which holds copper and gold exploration projects.

Federal Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal called for speeding up development of the Nokundi-Gwadar road corridor to connect Balochistan's mineral-rich areas with seaports and unlock export potential. Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb told the forum that the minerals and mining sector would play a crucial role in driving sustainable economic growth, boosting exports and attracting foreign investment. He said Pakistan had reduced its twin deficit from around 12.5 per cent of GDP a few years ago to 2.6 per cent by the end of June 2026, recorded a fiscal deficit at a 22-year low, and posted primary surpluses for three consecutive years.

He added that foreign exchange reserves had risen to 21.5 billion dollars, the highest in the country's history, and that the government aimed to raise exports to 100 billion dollars under the National Economic Transformation Plan 2035. The finance minister also noted that the country had raised 750 million dollars through a private placement in April 2026, accessed the Chinese capital market through its first Panda bond in May, and issued 3 billion dollars in Eurobonds in September after a gap of around four years.
