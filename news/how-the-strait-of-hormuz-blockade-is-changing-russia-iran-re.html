How the Strait of Hormuz Blockade Is Changing Russia-Iran Relations

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 13, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The war launched against Iran in February by the United States and Israel, and the continuing American blockade of Iranian ports, have brought indirect benefits to Russia, Iran's ally. With the blockade in place, Russia is earning windfall gains from higher oil prices, benefiting from its opponents being distracted, and strengthening its presence in the Iranian economy. Russia is also working to expand its presence in Iran's war-affected energy markets and has offered to supply gas to the country's northern areas, where shortages sometimes occur in winter.

Shipping links across the Caspian Sea have grown markedly since the middle of 2026, when the United States blockaded Iran's southern ports and Ukrainian attacks stopped Russian ports in the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea from working. The Caspian provides Russia with a route to send military parts to Iran, while Iran is also buying more Russian grain, easing some of the pressure of logistical problems on Russian grain producers. Iran expert Nikita Smagin wrote in October that Tehran is forced to seek more help and expand its ties with Russia, and that Iran remains one of the few foreign places where Russian companies work on major energy projects such as the Bushehr nuclear power plant.

President Vladimir Putin said in October that bilateral trade rose by 10.4 percent between January and August, and Russian officials have said trade grew by 20 percent in 2025 and that Moscow has no intention of abandoning its economic and investment agreements with Iran.
