Why Pakistan's Fatah-3 missile is being compared with India's BrahMos

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 24, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Pakistan says it has successfully tested Fatah-3, its first supersonic cruise missile, a claim that has drawn comparisons with India's BrahMos missile. The military's public relations wing, ISPR, says the missile is fitted with advanced navigation and propulsion systems and can strike distant targets with precision and lethal capability at supersonic speed. ISPR says the missile's induction will increase the operational flexibility and targeting reach of the Pakistan Army's Rocket Force Command and further strengthen the country's conventional defence capability.

Defence experts estimate Fatah-3's speed at between Mach 2.5 and 3.5, although Pakistan has not officially released its final speed, range or other technical details. Douglas Barrie, a defence expert at the London-based International Institute for Strategic Studies, says the missile's design closely resembles China's HD-1 missile, and that it would not be surprising if it were based on Chinese technology or a Chinese design given the close defence ties between Pakistan and China. He explains that the missile uses a solid rocket booster to gain initial speed, after which a ramjet engine takes over and helps it sustain supersonic speed, and estimates it could travel at about Mach 3 during flight.

Weapons researcher Hammad Waleed says supersonic cruise missiles keep the accuracy, low-altitude flight and course-changing ability of cruise missiles while reaching several times the speed of sound, making them harder to stop. Experts caution, however, that without verified information about the missile's range, accuracy, payload and deployment, a definitive performance comparison with BrahMos remains impossible.
