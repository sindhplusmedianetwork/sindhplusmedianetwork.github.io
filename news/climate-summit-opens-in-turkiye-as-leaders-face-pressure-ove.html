Climate Summit Opens in Turkiye as Leaders Face Pressure Over Funding

The COP31 climate talks opened on Monday in Antalya, Turkiye, with an early dispute over money as UN chief Antonio Guterres urged world leaders to get serious about tackling the crisis after a year of devastating weather extremes. Guterres said the world had entered a new age of climate extremes and called on leaders to cut emissions faster, speed up a just transition away from fossil fuels, and deliver the finance and protection that vulnerable countries need. Negotiators had been locked in discussions late on Sunday over demands by developing nations to include climate finance items on the official agenda, and African countries are pushing to triple adaptation finance.

The summit follows a year of heatwaves in Europe, wildfires in Indonesia and catastrophic floods in Nepal, with an exceptionally strong El Nino adding to the disruption. The World Meteorological Organization said 2026 has continued a streak of exceptional global temperatures, and the EU's climate monitor warned that 2026 would most likely be the second-hottest year ever recorded.
