Militant influx into South Khyber Pakhtunkhwa raises questions over governor's rule, says Marwat

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 09, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

National Assembly member Sher Afzal Khan Marwat has said the arrival of armed militants in southern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa raises serious questions about the performance of governor's rule. In a statement posted on social media, he said large numbers of armed militants have entered several areas over the past 20 days, and that alarming reports are coming from Kalachi, Tank, South and North Waziristan, Bannu, Karak and Kohat. He said his home district of Lakki Marwat had been badly affected by terrorism, but joint efforts by peace committees, the police and the army had reduced it by about 90 percent.

Marwat said some local sources also claimed the presence of Afghan Taliban among the incoming groups, and urged the institutions to investigate immediately and share the facts with the public. He called on the government to provide resources to the police, win the trust of the local population and produce measurable results in stopping infiltration. He added that preventing terrorism had been made the main justification for governor's rule, so the growing arrival of armed groups would become the biggest question over its performance and justification.
