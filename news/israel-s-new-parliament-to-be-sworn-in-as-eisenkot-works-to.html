Israel's new parliament to be sworn in as Eisenkot works to form government

By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 10, 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

Israel's new parliament will be sworn in on Tuesday, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu set to lose his majority even as rival Gadi Eisenkot still works to form a new government. President Isaac Herzog will deliver a speech at 4pm for the swearing-in of the 120-seat parliament, in which neither Netanyahu's right-wing bloc nor Eisenkot's opposition will hold a majority. The opposition hopes to use its new legislative power to move quickly on key priorities, including a commission to investigate security failures under Netanyahu that allowed the October 7, 2023 attack by Hamas.

Herzog on Sunday gave Eisenkot the initial mandate to form a government, starting the clock for him to put together a coalition within 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension. Ahead of the parliament opening, Eisenkot's party announced progress in naming people to seek key positions. The opposition will put forward Matan Kahana, like Eisenkot a former senior military figure, as speaker of the parliament, while Avigdor Lieberman, a right-wing opposition leader, will be nominated as chair of the foreign affairs and defence committee.

The newly formed government would need 61 votes to be approved to take office, and Eisenkot could secure the threshold with support from Arab-Israeli lawmakers, who secured a record number of seats. Eisenkot's core opposition bloc has fewer seats than Netanyahu at 48, but would achieve a majority if the 19 Arab-Israeli lawmakers either enter the government or vote for it without joining, while the two ultra-Orthodox parties, with 13 seats, could also crown Eisenkot if they end their long alliance with Netanyahu. An alliance with either group would likely be fragile, as Arab-Israelis are likely to object if Eisenkot carries out another major offensive in Gaza, although Eisenkot has largely backed Netanyahu's war decisions and has vowed to crack down on extremist settlers in the West Bank.
