British Pakistani Woman Alleges Harassment by Ex-Husband on Lahore Court Premises

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 08, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

A British Pakistani woman pursuing maintenance litigation for her three daughters has alleged harassment and intimidation by her former husband on the premises of the sessions court in Lahore. In an application submitted to the Islampura police, the complainant, Laleen, said she had visited the court for a hearing against her ex-husband, a businessman, and was confronted by him along with unidentified individuals as she left the courtroom. She alleged that the men harassed her, hurled verbal abuse and continued issuing threats even inside the judge's chamber.

According to the complaint filed on October 23, her former husband repeatedly demanded custody of their daughters, issued death threats and physically shoved her, and the suspects stopped her from dialling the police helpline or reaching the police station. She said she had obtained a khula, or judicial divorce, seven years earlier and a guardian court had decided the custody of the daughters in her favour, a decision that had attained finality. Despite providing video evidence, she alleged, the police were reluctant to register a case, while the inquiry officer told Dawn the matter had been investigated but no cognisable offence was made out.

The woman has appealed to Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz to intervene and ensure justice, and she also seeks the removal of her youngest daughter's name from the Exit Control List. A family court had awarded each daughter Rs100,000 a month in maintenance from January 2017, along with educational expenses and a 10 per cent annual increase, and the Lahore High Court later dismissed the father's appeal and upheld the award, but the children have still not received the maintenance because execution proceedings are pending, and the high court has observed that prolonged delays in execution reduce court judgments to mere paper declarations.
