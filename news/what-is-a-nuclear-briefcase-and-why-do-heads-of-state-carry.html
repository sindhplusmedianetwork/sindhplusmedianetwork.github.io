What is a nuclear briefcase and why do heads of state carry it?

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 20, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

When the presidents of the US, China and Russia arrived in the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen for trilateral talks on the occasion of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, a Russian military officer was seen carrying a mysterious black briefcase. In response to a journalist's question, the Russian officer smiled and confirmed that it is indeed a nuclear briefcase, which contains a system providing the Russian president direct access to the country's nuclear weapons, called Cheget. The US president also has a similar briefcase, called the Nuclear Football, which routinely travels with him, however it has not yet been seen in Shenzhen.

It is unclear whether Chinese President Xi Jinping also carries such a briefcase with him or not. A nuclear briefcase is equipped with special devices, connected to the military command system, through which a head of state can order a nuclear attack. In the US and Russia this system began during the Cold War, when leaders faced the possibility that they might have to make decisions regarding a nuclear attack in an extremely short time.
