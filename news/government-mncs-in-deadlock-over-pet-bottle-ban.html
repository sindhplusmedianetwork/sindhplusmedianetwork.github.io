Government, MNCs in Deadlock Over PET Bottle Ban

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
October 17, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The government and multinational companies have failed to break a gridlock over a sudden ban on the use of recyclable bottles by the food and beverages industry. The action was driven by the authorities' desire to present Islamabad as a plastic-free city at the upcoming Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit. A meeting this week between Minister of State for Interior Talal Chaudhry, whose subordinate department initiated the action, and representatives of Nestlé, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo and Metro remained inconclusive, according to people familiar with the discussions.

The companies maintained that there is no legal ban on the use of recyclable plastic, and their stance was supported by the Pakistan Environmental Protection Agency, whose director has publicly stated that existing regulations do not ban PET bottles. Dr Zaigham Abbas, an official of the federal agency, reportedly said there was no legal basis for removing PET bottles from sale, and that the clarification has been conveyed to the Islamabad Capital Territory administration. Despite these explanations, the government continued to press for alternative packaging, particularly glass bottles, while industry representatives warned that no commercially viable substitute is available at the required scale.

They said the sudden ban would dent federal tax revenues by at least Rs10 billion and send negative signals to international investors about the government itself disregarding environmental regulations.
