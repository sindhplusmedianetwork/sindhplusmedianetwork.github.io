Former Army Chief Gadi Eisenkot Asked to Form Israel's New Government

Former Israeli army chief and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot will form a new government in Israel, after President Isaac Herzog entrusted him with the task. President Herzog announced on Saturday that he had given the responsibility of forming a new government to Gadi Eisenkot, the strongest rival of outgoing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, saying the decision was taken after consultations with the leaders of political parties. A statement from the president's office said the president had informed Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot of his intention to entrust him with forming a new government, and that he had informed both Eisenkot and Benjamin Netanyahu of the decision.

It is worth noting that the Israeli president has the authority to choose a person he believes has the best chances of forming a coalition government, and it is not necessary for the president to nominate the leader of the party with the most seats. According to the statement, the formal mandate to form the government will be given to Eisenkot at an official ceremony at the presidential residence in Jerusalem on Sunday afternoon. After the announcement, Eisenkot said he would take every possible step to form a new government in Israel.

He wrote on the social media platform X that now is the time to form a national emergency government, and that he would do everything possible to ensure they fulfil this responsibility. Eisenkot has received the support of parties representing 67 of the 120 members of the Israeli parliament, while only parties representing 46 members supported Netanyahu's alliance. It is worth remembering that in the recent elections in Israel, no alliance won the 61-seat majority needed to form a government.

Netanyahu's alliance won 53 of the 120 seats in the Knesset, while opposition parties won 48 seats and Arab parties won 19 seats. Netanyahu will remain prime minister in a caretaker government until a new alliance is formed, or new elections will be held if that proves impossible. After receiving the mandate from Herzog, Eisenkot will have 28 days to form a government, with a possible extension of 14 days.

If Eisenkot fails during this time, another candidate can try to form a government, and if that candidate also fails, parliament can nominate another candidate. This process can take about 100 days, and if no one succeeds, new elections will be held. The political landscape changed after Eisenkot received the support of the Arab parties.

During consultations with political parties at President Herzog's office on Thursday, the Arab parties announced their support for Eisenkot. Forming a government in Israel's 120-member parliament requires the support of 61 members. Netanyahu also suffered a major blow because a faction of his old allied party, United Torah Judaism (UTJ), refused to support him.

Mansour Abbas, leader of the Arab party Ra'am, announced his support for Eisenkot on Thursday. His party holds eight seats, and on this occasion Abbas also reiterated his support for the two-state solution and US President Donald Trump's Gaza plan, and demanded the disarmament of all armed and military organisations, including Hamas. The second largest Arab political party, the Joint List, with 11 seats, has also decided to support Eisenkot, describing its decision as a choice between 'bad and worse' in order to remove the Netanyahu government from power.

Joint List head Yousef Jabareen said the decision reflects Arab voters' desire for change, and alleged that under the current government Arab citizens faced racism, discrimination, crime and political and social deprivation. Senior Arab parliamentarian Ahmad Tibi said the Joint List could support a minority government from outside parliament and play the role of a 'blocking bloc' in the chamber, but the party would not join a government that might fall soon. Eisenkot was born in the Israeli city of Tiberias (Tabariya).

If he becomes prime minister, he would be Israel's first Mizrahi Jewish prime minister. Mizrahi Jews trace their origins to the Middle East and North Africa, and about half of Israel's population belongs to this community. Eisenkot began his military career in 1978 when he joined the Golani Brigade of the Israel Defense Forces during compulsory military service, became commander of the same brigade in 1997, served in several senior positions, and was appointed IDF Chief of General Staff in 2015.

After retiring from the army, he entered politics and was elected a member of the Israeli parliament. After Hamas's attack on 7 October 2023, he also joined Netanyahu's war cabinet. His son Gal Eisenkot was killed during fighting in Gaza in December 2023, and he later lost two nephews in the same conflict.

In September 2025, Eisenkot founded his moderate political party Yashar, which means 'straight, true or honest' in Hebrew. He has promised that if he becomes prime minister, he will act on his goal of eliminating the military wing of Hamas and its leadership within 18 months.
