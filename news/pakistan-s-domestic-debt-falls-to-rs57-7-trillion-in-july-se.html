Pakistan's domestic debt falls to Rs57.7 trillion in July-September

SP
By Sindh Plus Media Network
November 14, 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp Facebook X

The federal government's domestic debt significantly declined in the first quarter of 2026-27, reversing the trend in the preceding year. The latest data posted by the State Bank showed that total domestic debt stood at Rs57.757 trillion at the end of the first quarter on September 30, down from Rs59.393 trillion on June 30, marking a decline of Rs1.636 trillion in a single quarter. Planning, Development and Special Initiative Minister Ahsan Iqbal recently said the fiscal deficit narrowed to 2.6 per cent of GDP in FY26, compared with 5.4 per cent in FY25, marking the lowest fiscal deficit recorded in two decades.

State Bank data showed that the government's net retirement of scheduled-bank debt rose sharply to Rs1.847 trillion in July-October FY27, up from Rs45 billion in the same period last fiscal year, indicating that debt repayments exceeded new borrowing. Borrowing through long-term Pakistan Investment Bonds declined by Rs1.756 trillion to Rs33.543 trillion, while borrowing through short-term Treasury bills fell by Rs1.163 trillion to Rs9.640 trillion. External debt increased in rupee terms from Rs24.2 trillion on June 30 to Rs24.9 trillion on September 30, a rise of Rs700 billion, while over 12 months total domestic debt rose by Rs4.331 trillion, with the increase concentrated in FY26 rather than FY27.
