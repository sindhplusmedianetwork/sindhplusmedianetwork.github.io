Reporting Truth in Pakistan: A Young Journalist's Field Notes

By Tahir Adnan
October 10, 2026 · 6 min read

When I told my family I wanted to study journalism, the response was polite concern. "Beta, is mein paisa nahin hai" — there is no money in it. They were half right. What they did not say, because it goes without saying in Pakistan, is that there is also danger in it, pressure in it, and a thousand quiet ways of being told what not to write.

I completed my MA in Journalism anyway. Not because I am naive about the profession, but because I believe a country cannot fix what it refuses to look at — and looking is the journalist's job.

Let me tell you what young reporters in Pakistan actually face. First, the economics: district correspondents — the people who cover everything outside the big cities — are often paid per story, late, or not at all. Many work second jobs. When your salary depends on not angering the powerful, "objectivity" becomes a luxury.

"A press that cannot afford to displease power is not a free press — it is a press release with better lighting."

Second, the pressure is rarely a dramatic phone call. It is softer than that: an advertiser who withdraws, an official who stops returning calls, an editor who spikes your story "for now" until "for now" becomes forever. You learn to read the silence.

Third, the digital space that was supposed to free us has its own chains. Independent YouTubers and Facebook reporters reach millions, but they work without editors, without legal cover, and often without training. Some do brave work. Others chase outrage for views. The audience, drowning in content, struggles to tell the difference.

So why stay? Because I have also seen what honest reporting does. A single story about a broken water scheme can embarrass an administration into fixing it. A camera at a protest keeps the batons lower. Journalism in Pakistan does not always win — but when it works, it works for people who have no other voice.

My own answer to all of this was to build my own platform: Sindh Plus Media Network. No advertiser to please, no editor to spike the story. Just the work — reported carefully, written honestly, in the languages my people actually speak.

The field notes continue. The work is hard, the pay is uncertain, and my family was half right. But the other half — the half about why it matters — belongs to us.

Tahir Adnan

MA Journalism. Founder of Sindh Plus Media Network. He writes about media, power, and accountability in Pakistan.
