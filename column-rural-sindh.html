The Sindh You Don't See on TV

By Tahir Adnan
October 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Turn on any Pakistani news channel and Sindh appears in two forms: Karachi's politics, or a crime headline from somewhere upcountry. There is a third Sindh — the one I come from — and it almost never makes the broadcast.

It is the Sindh of the ajrak weaver in Hala who has dyed cloth the same way his grandfather did, and who can tell by smell when the indigo is ready. It is the primary school teacher in a village near Ghotki who teaches forty children under a tree because the school building's roof collapsed three monsoons ago and nobody came to fix it — and who shows up every single morning anyway.

It is the farmer who reads the sky better than any weather app, the midwife who has delivered three generations, the truck driver who knows every dhaba between Sukkur and Hyderabad and can tell you which one waters down the chai.

"Rural Sindh is not a tragedy waiting for a camera. It is a civilization going about its day — and the cameras keep missing it."

Why does this matter? Because what the news ignores, policy ignores. When rural Sindh appears only as a crime statistic, it gets policed. When it appears as a drought headline, it gets a relief package and a photo opportunity. It rarely gets what it actually needs: schools that stay open, clinics that stay stocked, water that stays clean — the unglamorous machinery of a dignified life.

I started reporting these stories because I am tired of my home being someone else's footage. The weaver, the teacher, the farmer — they are not background characters in Karachi's story. They are the story.

So here is my promise as a journalist from this soil: I will keep pointing the camera where the news channels won't. The Sindh you don't see on TV is the Sindh most worth seeing.

Tahir Adnan

MA Journalism. Founder of Sindh Plus Media Network. He documents the people and places of rural Sindh.
